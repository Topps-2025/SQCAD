## Abstract

> **Scope correction.** The historical one-sided constant-regret restore claim remains withdrawn. Appendix A proves only a finite-horizon, same-class safe-recovery order and an anytime/stitched certificate under an explicit archived-committed sub-Gaussian contract; raw LLM certificates are not claimed to satisfy that contract.

Persistent Agent-memory actions do more than change current retrieval: they alter future candidate access, workspace competition, and the evidence available for correcting the decision. We formulate this as a finite-horizon lifecycle belief-control problem. Theorems 1--3 decompose keep--archive value, characterize score sufficiency and fiber regret, sign conditional recoverability value under Blackwell dominance, and lower-bound authorization regret by action-dependent transcript KL. Theorem 4 then gives an architecture-agnostic trichotomy: every intervention-defined memory channel is future-null, lifecycle-complete under the chosen score, or admits a separating task/cost witness with positive score-only regret. It identifies the coarsest task-relative lifecycle information partition $T_{\rm LC}^*$ and an approximation target $\varepsilon_{\rm LC}$. SQCAD operationalizes these results through auditable interval authorization and priced probe/defer actions; the older counterexamples and certificate bounds become appendix specializations. Under storage constraints (matched 1.6k-token budgets), SQCAD achieves 2-11× higher coverage than streaming baselines; a bounded candidate guard approaches 95% of full-store quality at 1/45 persistent footprint. On LifecycleBench (1,380 paired episodes), interval authorization reaches 90% oracle agreement with 7.2% false-commit rate, significantly outperforming score-only baselines; censoring suppression (+11.34 [0.90, 30.73]) confirms the access-state mechanism predicted by theory. Public-trajectory and controlled-counterfactual claims remain explicitly separated.

## 1 Introduction

Long-horizon agents store task state, user constraints, environment observations, and reusable experience in a persistent external memory that is written, retrieved, updated, and exposed across interactions (Packer et al., 2023; Park et al., 2023). Unlike a transient context window, this store accumulates: redundant descriptions, obsolete versions, and conflicting evidence enlarge the retrieval space, raise inference cost, and keep influencing behavior after the conditions that produced them have changed. The governance question is not *how to store more history* but *how to regulate the future accessibility of information already in memory* — including forgetting as a change of accessibility, priority, or representation rather than physical deletion.

Existing governance methods rely on several distinct objects. MemoryBank combines temporal and importance cues (Zhong et al., 2024); FadeMem applies differential decay from semantic relevance, access frequency, and temporal patterns (Wei et al., 2026); OBLIVION controls decaying accessibility through uncertainty and response contribution (Rana et al., 2026); and Memory Worth estimates success conditional on retrieval while explicitly identifying the estimate as associational (Simsek, 2026). DeMem is the closest decision-theoretic work: it preserves history distinctions needed for downstream decisions under a fixed memory budget (Zou et al., 2026). Query-local causal methods instead perturb current exposure or audit past harm (Srivastava, 2026; Tan et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026). None of these distinctions alone specifies the value of a persistent authorization action when that action changes the future state and evidence kernels.

We model the target as a **persistent-access lifecycle action value** on an Agent belief-state. The Agent-specific structure is not merely partial observability: keep/archive changes candidate regeneration, finite-workspace competition, scope/version state, and the future evidence or restore experiment. Theorems 1--3 place these effects in one Bellman contrast, characterize score sufficiency, and quantify the information price of recoverability. Theorem 4 removes dependence on a particular memory implementation by classifying the intervention-induced signed future-transcript kernel and deriving the minimal statistic that a task-universal governance score must preserve. Historical non-identification, query-local crowding/bridge examples, committing regret, interval minimax, and safe certificates are retained in Appendix A as constructions and subclasses of this general spine. This is not a claim that every score or every natural task fails; it states the exact future-kernel information that must be preserved for a declared task class.

Guided by these results we propose **SQCAD** (Scope-aware Qualification and Controlled Access for persistent memories), an Evidence–Qualification–Access framework. The Evidence layer logs the fields the theorems say are needed (exposure propensity, position, co-exposure, adoption proxy, budget, scope/version); the Qualification layer maintains an auditable certificate interval $[L, U]$ for the lifecycle contrast and authorizes `keep` only if $L > 0$, `archive` only if $U < 0$, and otherwise defers or probes according to the value of information (VOI) — with `unresolved`/`mismatch` as explicit, non-actionable states rather than conservative defaults; the Access layer projects certified memories into a budgeted one-shot workspace, so that a *bounded candidate guard* can repair coverage bad cases without relaxing long-term authorization. Every component maps to a theorem (Derivation Map, §4.5). The observational certificate route is scoped to behavior-policy value unless a full target-policy transport condition is separately verified; the controlled protocol route directly executes the target policy.

We evaluate SQCAD in three layers. **L1** checks the theorem witnesses and appendix subclasses in controlled worlds: Bellman accounting, continuation versus conditional-information VoR, dynamic quotient lumpability, admissible-value-separated signed-kernel actions, the $\varepsilon/4$ fiber bound including the zero-width tie, Blackwell information monotonicity, the augmented-transcript KL regret floor, the priced recoverability frontier, observational and query-local constructions, interval minimax, and certificate formulas. **L2** evaluates external validity on LongMemEval-S and LoCoMo under a unified contract: the interval-authorization row and frozen SQCAD operate a $\sim$45× smaller persistent store than BM25/dense/RRF at lower static coverage, with a bounded candidate guard repairing one-shot coverage without changing long-term authorization. **L3** evaluates internal counterfactual validity on 1,380 same-future paired episodes: interval authorization coincides action-for-action with the probe-willing baseline, measures false-commit 0.072 and regret 0.115, and exposes that the restore channel is net-negative under this benchmark's prices. At the honest unit count of 14 rule-world instantiations, only censoring suppression is statistically significant. We report all named baselines by evidence tier and do not upgrade proxies to official results.


### Related work and the signal–action map

Existing governance systems estimate one of two signals but act on a third object; the table maps each system's *signal* and *action* to the *object it estimates*.

| System | Signal | Action | Object estimated |
|---|---|---|---|
| MemoryBank (Zhong et al., 2024) | temporal + importance | access decay | association |
| FadeMem (Wei et al., 2026) | relevance, frequency, recency | differential decay | association |
| Oblivion (Rana et al., 2026) | uncertainty + response contribution | accessibility decay | association |
| Memory Worth (Simsek, 2026) | success–failure co-occurrence | trust update | association (self-declared) |
| DeMem (Zou et al., 2026) | decision-relevant distinctions | retention/compression | decision-centric compression |
| CMI (Srivastava, 2026) | query-time controlled perturbation | exposure | query-local intervention |
| MemAudit (Tan et al., 2026) | post-hoc causal attribution | removal | audit |
| ActMem (Zhang et al., 2026) | causal-semantic graph | graph maintenance | query-time causal |
| Trivium (Chang, 2026) | persistent causal evidence + probes | controller state | probe evidence |
| SimpleMem (Liu et al., 2026) | embedding retrieval | retrieval | retrieval (no governance estimand) |
| SAGE (Brahma et al., 2026) | vMF novelty density | write-side Add/Update/Noop gate | novelty, not authorization |
| GovMem (Qi et al., 2026) | coverage-dependent evidence | promote/reject/needs-review (write path) | write-time qualification |
| GateMem (Ren et al., 2026) / ReMe (Cao et al., 2026) | benchmark / procedural experience | admission / distillation | evaluation or refinement, not authorization (Appendix D) |

The closest gap is not generic decision theory. DeMem already gives a decision-centric rate--distortion view of memory, but its analytical contextual-decision model samples contexts independently from a fixed distribution and compresses histories into a fixed number of runtime states. Controlled-sensing and active-perception POMDPs already allow actions to alter dynamics and emissions and already supply Bellman, convexity, and Blackwell/Jensen arguments (Satsangi et al., 2018; Krishnamurthy, 2017; Shi et al., 2025); Bretagnolle--Huber and adaptive KL chain rules likewise provide standard testing lower bounds. Our claim is narrower: persistent Agent-memory authorization jointly changes candidate/workspace access states and conditional evidence experiments, creating both a falsifiable score-sufficiency condition and an information ledger in which censored archive periods contribute no distinguishing KL until exposure, probe, or restore reopens the channel. Partial identification and minimax treatment choice supply the interval decision tools (Manski, 2003; Stoye, 2012); causal inference supplies the protocol-route assumptions (Hernán & Robins, 2020; Imbens & Rubin, 2015). We do not claim to introduce action-dependent POMDP kernels, Bellman recursion, Blackwell ordering, or sequential-testing inequalities.

## 2 Problem Setting: Persistent-Access Lifecycle Value

### 2.1 Observable history and the decision epoch

Time is discrete, $t = 1, 2, \dots$. The system re-evaluates memory access at **epoch boundaries** $t_0, t_0+H, t_0+2H, \dots$. Within an epoch, the persistent action is **held fixed** — it is a one-shot treatment per epoch, not a per-timestep choice; within-epoch exposure $E_{i,t}$ is a consequence variable (mediator), not a treatment. At the next boundary the action may be re-assessed on the new state, so across epochs the protocol is a sequence of one-shot treatments with sequential exchangeability applied at epoch boundaries.

The observable log within scope $s$ is $O_{1:H}^{(s)} = \{(C_t, X_t, E_t, P_t, D_t, A_t^{\text{obs}}, Y_t, c_t)\}_{t=1}^{H}$, where $C_t$ is the candidate set, $X_t$ the task/user/tool/model state, $E_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$ whether memory $i$ is exposed, $P_t$ position/budget allocation, $D_{i,t}$ an adoption proxy, $A^{\text{obs}}$ observed access actions, $Y_t$ task utility, and $c_t$ token/latency/probe cost. The persistent access action of memory $i$ is $A_i^{\text{pers}} \in \{\texttt{keep}, \texttt{archive}, \texttt{restore}\}$, with effective window $[\tau_0, \tau_1]$, possible rollback, and competition under finite workspace budget $B$.

### 2.2 The target estimand and two decoys

For scope $s$, target policy $\pi$, horizon $H$, discount $\gamma \in (0,1]$, and pre-registered cost/risk weights $\lambda, \zeta \ge 0$:

$$
V_s^{\pi}(a) = \mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}\bigl(Y_t - \lambda C_t - \zeta R_t\bigr) \;\middle|\; do(A_i^{\text{pers}} = a),\; s\right],
\qquad
\tau_s^{\pi}(a_1, a_0) = V_s^{\pi}(a_1) - V_s^{\pi}(a_0),
$$

where $R_t$ is a risk/harm term (e.g., harmful adoption of stale or wrong-scope memories). The $do(\cdot)$ makes the target counterfactual: what is the net value of *holding access fixed* to $a$ while the policy regenerates future candidates, exposure, budget competition, and outcomes?

Three estimands must be kept separate — conflating them is the exact failure we formalize:

| Estimand | Treatment | Held fixed | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical association | none (observational) | historical policy | "Does this memory co-occur with success?" |
| Query-local intervention $\Delta_t(i)$ | $do(E_{i,t}=1)$ vs $0$ | query, candidate set, model, evaluator | "Does this memory help the current answer?" |
| **Persistent-access lifecycle value** $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ | $do(A_i^{\text{pers}}=a)$ | future candidates regenerated by $\pi$ | "Does changing long-term access improve future trajectories?" |

The distinction is structural: a persistent action changes the *mechanism* that generates future exposure, whereas a query-local exposure changes only the current prompt composition. The future candidate transition $P_\pi(C_{t+1} \mid \cdot, A_i^{\text{pers}}=a)$ is therefore a state variable of the estimand, not exogenous nuisance — this single fact drives all of §3.

### 2.3 Cost contract

For $t$ in an epoch, net utility is composed of task success, harmful adoption, exposure, probe, and storage terms; the concrete instance used in our benchmarks (LifecycleBench) freezes $u_t = 10\cdot\text{difficulty}_t \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{task hit}] - 20\cdot[\text{harmful adoption}] - 0.05\cdot[\text{exposed}] - 1.0\cdot[\text{probes}] - 0.01\cdot[\text{stored tokens}]$ with $\gamma = 0.9$ (§5.3, Appendix B). The public contract (§5.2) reports storage tokens (persistent store size) and exposure tokens (per-task workspace) separately so that low storage is never claimed as low total cost.

## 3 Theory: Lifecycle Value, Score Sufficiency, and Recoverability

### 3.1 Process and persistence contract

Consider a finite horizon $t=1,\ldots,H$ and a latent environment $\theta\in\Theta$. The observable Agent state is

$$
s_t=(m_t,w_t,\mathcal C_t,e_t,z_t),
$$

where $m_t$ records memory authorization and scope/version, $w_t$ is the available workspace and storage budget, $\mathcal C_t$ is the candidate pool, $e_t$ is the provenance/evidence state, and $z_t$ records restore and probe availability. Given history $h_t$, the Agent holds the posterior $b_t(\theta)=P(\theta\mid h_t)$. Its lifecycle actions are $\mathcal A=\{K,A,P,D\}$ for keep, archive, probe, and defer.

An action produces the next physical state and then the evidence visible in that state:

$$
\mathsf K_a(ds',do\mid s,\theta)
=P_a^S(ds'\mid s,\theta)P_a^O(do\mid s',s,\theta).
\tag{1}
$$

This order fixes the canonical execution filtration

$$
\mathcal F_t
\subset \mathcal F_t\vee\sigma(a_t,s_{t+1})
\subset \mathcal F_t\vee\sigma(a_t,s_{t+1},o_{t+1})=\mathcal F_{t+1}.
\tag{2}
$$

The state transition contains candidate regeneration, workspace competition, authorization duration, scope/version compatibility, and restore cost. The observation kernel contains evidence that can actually be generated after that transition. This convention is substantive: moving a variable between $s'$ and $o$ changes the names of the access and information terms below, although their sum remains invariant.

The process is *persistent* only if $m_{t+1}$ records an authorization commitment that later kernels consume, including its duration and reversal or restore cost. If an action can be changed at the next step without cost or trace, the process reduces to one-step retrieval control and is outside our target class.

We say that the process exhibits *action-dependent censoring* if, for some state, latent world, and future candidate or evidence event $E$,

$$
P_\theta(E\mid s,K)\neq P_\theta(E\mid s,A),
\tag{3}
$$

and this difference is induced by the persistent action through $\mathcal C_{t+1:H}$, $e_{t+1:H}$, or $z_{t+1:H}$. Archive may therefore prevent evidence from being generated, or make it observable only through a priced probe. This differs from query-local missingness because the governance action changes the future data-generating process.

### 3.2 Theorem 1: lifecycle Bellman decomposition

Let the immediate expected net utility be

$$
\ell_t(b,s,a)=\mathbb E_{\theta\sim b}[r_\theta(s,a)-\kappa(s,a)],
$$

and define $V_{H+1}=0$ and

$$
Q_t((b,s),a)=\ell_t(b,s,a)+\gamma C_t^a(b,s),
\qquad
V_t(b,s)=\max_{a\in\mathcal A}Q_t((b,s),a),
\tag{4}
$$

where

$$
C_t^a(b,s)=
\mathbb E\left[V_{t+1}(b^{a,s',o},s')\mid b,s,a\right].
\tag{5}
$$

Let $b^{a,s'}$ denote the posterior after observing the next physical state but before observing $o$. Define

$$
A_t^a(b,s)=
\mathbb E\left[V_{t+1}(b^{a,s'},s')\mid b,s,a\right],
\qquad
I_t^a(b,s)=C_t^a(b,s)-A_t^a(b,s).
\tag{6}
$$

Here $A_t^a$ is the continuation value induced by physical access-state transitions, while $I_t^a$ is the conditional value of the subsequent evidence experiment.

**Theorem 1 (Lifecycle Bellman decomposition).** Assume that the kernels and rewards are measurable, Bayes updates are well defined, and the finite-horizon values are integrable. For every belief-state $(b,s)$ and action $a$,

$$
Q_t((b,s),a)=\ell_t(b,s,a)+\gamma A_t^a(b,s)+\gamma I_t^a(b,s).
\tag{7}
$$

Consequently, the keep--archive lifecycle contrast satisfies

$$
\boxed{
\Delta_t
=\Delta_t^{\rm imm}
+\gamma\Delta_t^{\rm access}
+\gamma\Delta_t^{\rm info}
},
\tag{8}
$$

where $\Delta_t=Q_t(K)-Q_t(A)$ and each superscript denotes the corresponding keep--archive difference.

**Proof.** From (6), $C_t^a=A_t^a+I_t^a$ by definition. Substituting this identity into (4) proves (7). Subtracting the archive identity from the keep identity yields (8). Equivalently, (6) follows by applying the tower property along filtration (2): first condition on the next physical state, then integrate the evidence posterior. $\square$

The algebra is deliberately simple. The content is the accounting contract: future candidate exposure and workspace crowding belong to $\Delta^{\rm access}$; only evidence refinement conditional on the same next state belongs to $\Delta^{\rm info}$. Calling their sum ``information value'' would make a Blackwell comparison invalid whenever keep and archive induce different state kernels.

### 3.3 Theorem 2: score sufficiency and unavoidable fiber regret

Let $X_t$ and the score codomain $\mathcal Z$ be standard Borel spaces, let $S_t:X_t\to\mathcal Z$ be measurable, and assume that $\Delta_t:X_t\to\mathbb R$ is measurable. A score is *zero-action-sufficient* if an $S_t$-measurable rule reproduces the keep region $\{x:\Delta_t(x)\ge0\}$ under the current cost contract. It is *uniformly cost-sufficient* if this property holds after every scalar cost shift $\lambda\in\mathbb R$, with keep region $\{x:\Delta_t(x)\ge\lambda\}$. The second requirement is stronger: it tests whether the score preserves the action-value magnitude needed when storage, latency, or risk prices change.

**Theorem 2 (Score sufficiency iff lifecycle-value measurability).** The score $S_t$ is zero-action-sufficient if and only if $\{x:\Delta_t(x)\ge0\}\in\sigma(S_t)$, equivalently if the optimal zero-threshold action is constant on every score fiber. It is uniformly cost-sufficient if and only if there exists a measurable function $g_t$ such that

$$
\Delta_t(x)=g_t(S_t(x))
\quad\text{for all }x\in X_t.
\tag{9}
$$

If two states $x_1,x_2$ lie in the same score fiber but have different lifecycle contrasts, $S_t$ is not uniformly cost-sufficient, although it may still select the current action when their signs agree. If their contrasts have opposite signs,

$$
d_1:=\Delta_t(x_1)>0>\Delta_t(x_2)=:-d_2,
\tag{10}
$$

then no score-only rule is optimal in both states. Among randomized score-only rules, the minimax action regret on this fiber is

$$
\inf_{p\in[0,1]}
\max\{(1-p)d_1,pd_2\}
=\frac{d_1d_2}{d_1+d_2}>0.
\tag{11}
$$

The same value is the Bayes regret under the least-favorable two-point prior

$$
\pi^*(x_1)=\frac{d_2}{d_1+d_2},
\qquad
\pi^*(x_2)=\frac{d_1}{d_1+d_2}.
\tag{12}
$$

**Proof.** Zero-action sufficiency is equivalent to the keep region being an inverse image under $S_t$, which is exactly $\{\Delta_t\ge0\}\in\sigma(S_t)$. This implies constancy on every score fiber. Conversely, if the Borel keep region is fiber-constant, it and its complement are saturated; their images under $S_t$ are disjoint analytic subsets of $\mathcal Z$. Lusin separation supplies a Borel set separating those images, whose inverse image is exactly the keep region. For uniform sufficiency, if (9) holds, the rule keeps exactly when $g_t(S_t(x))\ge\lambda$. Conversely, uniform cost sufficiency implies that $\{x:\Delta_t(x)\ge q\}\in\sigma(S_t)$ for every rational $q$. Rational upper level sets generate the Borel sigma-algebra on $\mathbb R$, so $\Delta_t$ is $\sigma(S_t)$-measurable. The Doob--Dynkin factorization on standard Borel spaces gives (9).

For (11), a rule must use the same keep probability $p$ at $x_1$ and $x_2$. Its regrets are $(1-p)d_1$ and $pd_2$. Equalizing them gives $p=d_1/(d_1+d_2)$ and the stated minimax value. Under (12), its Bayes regret is

$$
\frac{d_2}{d_1+d_2}(1-p)d_1
+\frac{d_1}{d_1+d_2}pd_2
=\frac{d_1d_2}{d_1+d_2}
$$

for every $p$, proving least favorability. $\square$

**Corollary 2.1 (Approximate sufficiency on score fibers).** Define the closed compatible contrast interval on score fiber $z$ by

$$
L_t(z)=\inf_{x:S_t(x)=z}\Delta_t(x),
\qquad
U_t(z)=\sup_{x:S_t(x)=z}\Delta_t(x),
\tag{13}
$$

and assume that its endpoints are finite and measurable. A non-crossing fiber admits a zero-regret deterministic action; if $L_t(z)=U_t(z)=0$, every mixture has zero regret. On a genuinely crossing fiber $L_t(z)<0<U_t(z)$, the score-only rule that keeps with probability

$$
p_t^*(z)=\frac{U_t(z)}{U_t(z)-L_t(z)}
$$

has minimax action regret

$$
R_t^*(z)=
\frac{U_t(z)(-L_t(z))}{U_t(z)-L_t(z)}
\le\frac{U_t(z)-L_t(z)}{4}.
\tag{14}
$$

Hence score-fiber oscillation at most $\varepsilon$ implies a globally $\varepsilon/4$-minimax randomized score-only rule. Exact measurability is the zero-oscillation case, and the earlier interval minimax rule is the single-fiber specialization.

**Proof.** On a genuinely crossing fiber, a keep probability $p$ incurs endpoint regrets $(1-p)U$ and $p(-L)$. Equalizing them yields the displayed $p_t^*$ and $R_t^*$. With $a=U>0$ and $b=-L>0$, $ab/(a+b)\le(a+b)/4$. Non-crossing and zero-width tie fibers have zero regret directly. $\square$

**Agent-kernel consequence.** Equation (8) turns Theorem 2 into a falsifiable Agent-memory statement. Suppose $S_t(x_1)=S_t(x_2)$ and the immediate contrast is the same, but the unencoded future-kernel gaps

$$
J_t(x_i)=\Delta_t^{\rm access}(x_i)+\Delta_t^{\rm info}(x_i)
\tag{15}
$$

differ. Then $\Delta_t(x_1)-\Delta_t(x_2)=\gamma[J_t(x_1)-J_t(x_2)]$, so the score fails (9). If the two totals cross zero, the positive regret bound (11) follows. Thus an accurately estimated association, relevance, or one-step retrieval score is still insufficient whenever persistent authorization changes unencoded candidate, crowding, evidence, or recovery kernels within one score fiber.

**Proposition 2.2 (Dynamic score quotient / Agent control homomorphism).** Let $X_u$ be the complete post-observation belief-state space, let the action set be finite, and let $T_u^a(dx'\mid x)$ be the next-belief-state kernel after both the physical transition and evidence update. For every $u=t,\ldots,H+1$, let $\phi_u:X_u\to Z_u$ be a standard-Borel quotient map. If there exist measurable quotient rewards and kernels such that

$$
\ell_u(x,a)=\bar\ell_u(\phi_u(x),a),
\qquad
(\phi_{u+1})_\#T_u^a(\cdot\mid x)
=\bar T_u^a(\cdot\mid\phi_u(x)),
$$

and $V_{H+1}=\bar V_{H+1}\circ\phi_{H+1}$, then backward induction gives

$$
Q_u(x,a)=\bar Q_u(\phi_u(x),a),
\qquad
V_u(x)=\bar V_u(\phi_u(x))
$$

for every remaining stage. Hence a quotient-only Bayes-optimal policy exists and every keep--archive contrast factors through $\phi_u$. Conversely, for $\gamma>0$, if one-step action-value factorization is required for every bounded measurable quotient terminal payoff, equality of the push-forward kernels is necessary: otherwise a Borel set separating the two probability measures supplies an indicator payoff with different action values.

This is the horizon-wide Agent-specific condition. Candidate/state transitions, workspace competition, evidence/recovery channels, and continuation belief effects need not be action-independent, but their action dependence must be retained by the quotient. Theorem 2 remains the exact fixed-task iff; Proposition 2.2 prevents a fixed-task cancellation from being misreported as a sufficient Agent state representation.

**Corollary 2.3 (Value-separating signed kernels).** For two states $x_1,x_2$ on one current score fiber, define signed quotient kernels $\nu_i=(\phi_{t+1})_\#T_t^K(\cdot\mid x_i)-(\phi_{t+1})_\#T_t^A(\cdot\mid x_i)$. Let $\mathcal V_{t+1}$ be the bounded measurable continuation values realizable by the admissible future reward/control class. If $\gamma>0$ and some $v\in\mathcal V_{t+1}$ gives $h_i=\int v\,d\nu_i$ with $h_1\ne h_2$, choose the same immediate contrast $d=-\gamma(h_1+h_2)/2$ in both states. Then

$$
\Delta_t(x_1)=\frac{\gamma}{2}(h_1-h_2)
=-\Delta_t(x_2)\ne0.
$$

Thus any unencoded signed keep--archive candidate, workspace, scope/version, or recovery difference that is separated by an admissible continuation value yields a same-score opposite-action construction. If the task class is closed under all bounded measurable quotient terminal payoffs, $\nu_1\ne\nu_2$ alone suffices because Borel indicators separate signed measures. Kernel differences orthogonal to every realizable value are decision-equivalent for that task class. Whether a fixed real task already crosses zero remains empirical.

**Future-kernel-rich classes.** Assume $\gamma>0$, fix an immediate contrast $d$ and $\eta>0$. Call a model class $(S_t,d,\eta)$-future-kernel-rich if it contains two compatible states $x_+,x_-$ on one score fiber with the same immediate contrast $d$, while the omitted future-kernel functionals in (15) satisfy

$$
J_t(x_+)\ge\frac{-d+\eta}{\gamma},
\qquad
J_t(x_-)\le\frac{-d-\eta}{\gamma}.
$$

Equation (8) then gives $\Delta_t(x_+)\ge\eta$ and $\Delta_t(x_-)\le-\eta$, so (11) lower-bounds every score-only randomized rule's worst-case regret by $\eta/2$. This is a reusable model-class condition on persistent access/evidence/recovery kernels omitted by the score; whether a real Agent class satisfies it is an empirical question, not a theorem consequence.

**Explicit witness.** Let $\gamma>0$, let $\theta\in\{-1,+1\}$ have prior $1/2$, and let the next decision earn $R$ when it identifies $\theta$. In $x_1$, keep exposes a perfectly identifying future signal whereas archive yields a constant signal, giving $J_t(x_1)=R/2$. In $x_2$, a scope mismatch or workspace constraint makes both actions yield constant signals, giving $J_t(x_2)=0$. Give both states the same current score and immediate contrast $-\gamma R/4$. Then

$$
\Delta_t(x_1)=\gamma R/4>0,
\qquad
\Delta_t(x_2)=-\gamma R/4<0.
\tag{16}
$$

This witness disappears when the action-dependent future kernel is removed. Its source is therefore the Agent lifecycle mechanism, rather than Bayesian uncertainty alone.

### 3.4 Theorem 3: conditional value of recoverability

The access and information terms can be compared separately only under an explicit kernel contract. Fix $(b,s)$ and suppose keep and archive induce the same next-state kernel for $b$-almost every $\theta$. They then induce the same next-state marginal and the same state-level posterior $b^{s'}$. For almost every $s'$, let $Z$ be the keep observation and $O$ the archive observation. Keep *conditionally Blackwell-dominates* archive if there is a $\theta$-independent garbling kernel $G_{s'}$ such that

$$
P_A^O(do\mid s',s,\theta)
=\int G_{s'}(do\mid z,s)P_K^O(dz\mid s',s,\theta).
\tag{17}
$$

If archive is followed by a paid probe, its result is included in $O$; evidence available without that probe remains in $Z$. Every probe or restore price is recorded in the Bellman reward/cost ledger when incurred and is never absorbed into the experiment ordering. Differences in candidate regeneration or workspace occupancy are excluded from (17) and remain in $\Delta^{\rm access}$.

For general action-dependent state kernels, define

$$
\operatorname{VoR}^{\rm cont}_{t,K:A}
:=\gamma\left(C_t^K(b,s)-C_t^A(b,s)\right)
=\gamma\,\mathbb E_{\theta\sim b,(s',o)\sim\mathsf K_K(\cdot\mid s,\theta)}[V_{t+1}(b^{K,s',o},s')]
-\gamma\,\mathbb E_{\theta\sim b,(s',o)\sim\mathsf K_A(\cdot\mid s,\theta)}[V_{t+1}(b^{A,s',o},s')].
$$

This complete continuation difference is not sign-definite because it includes access-state and crowding effects. Under the common-state contract it equals $\operatorname{VoR}^{\rm info}_{t,K:A}:=\gamma(I_t^K-I_t^A)$, which is the quantity signed by Theorem 3(a).

**Theorem 3 (Recoverability: monotonicity and information-budget necessity).**

**(a) Conditional value.** Assume the common state-kernel condition above, conditional Blackwell dominance (17), and convexity of $V_{t+1}(\cdot,s')$ in the belief. Then

$$
\Delta_t^{\rm info}=I_t^K-I_t^A\ge0,
\qquad
\operatorname{VoR}^{\rm info}_{t,K:A}
=\gamma\Delta_t^{\rm info}\ge0.
\tag{18}
$$

The inequality, and hence $\operatorname{VoR}^{\rm info}_{t,K:A}$ for $\gamma>0$, is strict if a set of next states and archive observations of positive probability has a strict conditional Jensen gap,

$$
\operatorname{E}\left[V_{t+1}(b_Z^K,s')\mid O=o\right]
>
V_{t+1}\left(\operatorname{E}[b_Z^K\mid O=o],s'\right).
\tag{19}
$$

For a finite-horizon finite POMDP, convexity follows from the policy-tree representation: each fixed continuation policy has value affine in the belief, and the optimal value is their pointwise maximum. In that setting, a transparent sufficient condition for (19) is that the keep posterior support crosses continuation-policy regions, so no single continuation policy is optimal almost surely over that conditional support.

**Proof.** Couple the observations so that $O$ is generated by garbling $Z$. Bayesian posteriors satisfy

$$
b_O^A=\operatorname{E}\left[b_Z^K\mid O\right].
\tag{20}
$$

Conditional Jensen and convexity give

$$
V_{t+1}(b_O^A,s')
\le
\operatorname{E}\left[V_{t+1}(b_Z^K,s')\mid O\right].
\tag{21}
$$

Integrating over $O$ and the common next-state marginal yields $C_t^K\ge C_t^A$. The common state kernel also gives $A_t^K=A_t^A$, hence $I_t^K-I_t^A=C_t^K-C_t^A\ge0$. A positive-probability strict gap in (19) makes the integrated inequality strict. $\square$

**(b) Information-budget necessity.** Consider any adaptive lifecycle policy $\pi$ that, at a stopping time $\tau\le H$ of the canonical filtration, issues a persistent authorization $D\in\{K,A\}$. Let $R_\tau(\pi)$ denote the terminal action regret of that authorization: the remaining-lifecycle value gap between the better persistent action and $D$. Let $M_+$ and $M_-$ be two Agent worlds with the same pre-decision history, and hence zero initial-history KL and the same current score. On every authorization history of positive probability, suppose a mistaken archive in $M_+$ has action regret at least $d_+>0$, while a mistaken keep in $M_-$ has action regret at least $d_->0$.

For $u<\tau$, let $A_u$ be the diagnostic action and let $W_{u+1}$ be the complete observed increment after that action, including candidate, state-transition, probe, and restore observations. Define $Y_\tau=(A_0,W_1,\ldots,A_{\tau-1},W_\tau,\tau)$ and let $T_\tau=(Y_\tau,D)$. Action selection, randomized stopping, and terminal authorization are generated by policy kernels shared across the two worlds; only the action-dependent environment law of $W_{u+1}$ differs. Pad the action/observation slots after $\tau$ with a world-independent absorbing symbol to obtain a fixed-$H$ measurable object. The common terminal decision kernel $\pi(D\mid Y_\tau)$ has zero conditional KL, so the KL of $T_\tau$ equals the KL of $Y_\tau$. Let $\mathbb P_+^\pi$ and $\mathbb P_-^\pi$ be the laws of this padded augmented transcript and define

$$
B_\pi=\operatorname{KL}(\mathbb P_+^\pi\Vert\mathbb P_-^\pi).
\tag{22a}
$$

Then every such policy satisfies

$$
\max\{\mathbb E_+R_\tau(\pi),\mathbb E_-R_\tau(\pi)\}
\ge
\frac{d_+d_-}{2(d_++d_-)}e^{-B_\pi}.
\tag{22b}
$$

If $\mathbb P_+^\pi\ll\mathbb P_-^\pi$ and the adaptive action kernel of the policy is the same in both worlds, the KL chain rule gives

$$
B_\pi
=\mathbb E_+^\pi\!\left[
\sum_{u<\tau}
\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
\mathsf K_{A_u,+}(\cdot\mid H_u)
\Vert
\mathsf K_{A_u,-}(\cdot\mid H_u)
\right)
\right].
\tag{22c}
$$

Thus an archived state that censors the distinguishing event contributes zero KL until a probe, restore, or another observation-generating action reopens the channel. More generally, if each temporary keep/exposure contributes at most $\kappa_K$ KL, each probe contributes at most $\kappa_P$, and every other pre-authorization action contributes zero conditional KL, then

$$
B_\pi\le \kappa_K\mathbb E_+N_K+\kappa_P\mathbb E_+N_P.
\tag{22d}
$$

In the symmetric case $d_+=d_-=d$, (22b) is $d e^{-B_\pi}/4$. If a wrong authorization persists for $L$ future tasks with per-task gap at least $\nu$, then $d\ge\nu L$: bounded recoverable information implies an $\Omega(L)$ lifecycle-regret lower bound. Conversely, for $0<r<d_+d_-/[2(d_++d_-)]$, driving this lower bound below $r$ requires

$$
B_\pi\ge \log\frac{d_+d_-}{2r(d_++d_-)}.
\tag{22e}
$$

**Proof of (b).** Because $D$ is included in the augmented transcript, $E=\{D=A\}$ is measurable. Bretagnolle--Huber applied to the two transcript laws gives

$$
\mathbb P_+^\pi(E)+\mathbb P_-^\pi(E^c)
\ge \tfrac12 e^{-B_\pi}.
$$

The two terms are the authorization-error probabilities in $M_+$ and $M_-$. Their regret contributions are at least $d_+\mathbb P_+^\pi(E)$ and $d_-\mathbb P_-^\pi(E^c)$ by the uniform branchwise gap assumption. Minimizing the larger weighted term subject to the displayed sum gives (22b). Applied to the padded fixed-horizon transcript, the relative-entropy chain rule yields (22c). The common initial history, diagnostic action-selection kernels, randomized stop/continue kernels, and terminal decision kernel contribute zero KL, as do the post-stopping absorbing factors. Only the action-dependent environment kernels before $\tau$ remain. The per-action bound (22d) follows by summing their conditional KL caps. If initial histories have different laws, their KL must be added to (22c). If absolute continuity fails, $B_\pi=+\infty$ and (22b) remains valid but vacuous. $\square$

**Corollary 3.1 (Priced recoverability frontier).** Let $\alpha=d_+d_-/[2(d_++d_-)]$. Suppose every pre-authorization action that reopens the distinguishing channel contributes at most $\kappa>0$ conditional KL and costs at least $c>0$, while all other actions contribute zero conditional KL. Let $N$ count these channel-opening actions, let $n=\mathbb E_+N$, and define total authorization loss relative to a world-informed immediate authorization by $\widetilde R_\tau=R_\tau+C_\tau$, where $C_\tau$ is cumulative diagnostic cost. Then

$$
\max_{w\in\{+,-\}}\mathbb E_w\widetilde R_\tau(\pi)
\ge
\max\{cn,\alpha e^{-\kappa n}\}
\ge
\frac{c}{\kappa}W_0\!\left(\frac{\kappa\alpha}{c}\right).
\tag{22f}
$$

Indeed, (22b)--(22d) give the terminal-loss term and the expected diagnostic cost in $M_+$ is at least $cn$. Minimizing their maximum over $n\ge0$ equalizes them; $cn=\alpha e^{-\kappa n}$ is equivalent to $(\kappa n)e^{\kappa n}=\kappa\alpha/c$. Consequently, any target $r<\alpha$ for worst-world total authorization loss must satisfy

$$
n\ge\kappa^{-1}\log(\alpha/r),
\qquad
(c/\kappa)\log(\alpha/r)\le r.
\tag{22g}
$$

This is the Agent-specific no-free-recovery consequence: when persistent archive endogenously censors all non-diagnostic branches, reducing authorization error requires a priced action that reopens a KL-bearing channel. The Lambert-$W$ algebra is standard; the contribution is the lifecycle transcript contract that identifies which Agent actions carry information and what they cost.

Part (a) signs only the recoverability-information term; it does not imply that keep is globally optimal. Part (b) does not require a common state kernel and instead prices the entire action-dependent Agent transcript. It shows why a prior alone cannot repair self-censoring: without KL-bearing observations, the posterior odds cannot separate the two lifecycle worlds. By (8), a more informative keep action can still lose because of immediate storage/exposure cost or negative access value from workspace crowding. Conversely, recoverability overturns an immediate archive advantage exactly when

$$
\gamma(\Delta_t^{\rm access}+\Delta_t^{\rm info})
>-\Delta_t^{\rm imm}.
\tag{22}
$$

### 3.5 Theorem 4: architecture-agnostic lifecycle trichotomy

The preceding results admit an implementation-independent capstone. Let $z\in\mathcal Z$ index a future task and let $\Omega$ be the standard-Borel space of complete future Agent transcripts, including queries, retrieved memories, LLM outputs, tool calls, state updates, and outcomes. For an operationally defined persistent intervention $a\in\{K,A\}$, define

$$
P^a(dy\mid x,z)
=\mathcal L(Y_{t:H}\in dy\mid x,\operatorname{do}(a),z),
\qquad
\nu_{x,z}=P^K(\cdot\mid x,z)-P^A(\cdot\mid x,z).
\tag{23}
$$

This covers external, retrieval, prompt, cache, adapter, or parameterized memory whenever retain/suppress/update is a well-defined intervention; immutable base parameters without such an intervention are outside the comparison. For $0<\gamma\le1$ and task utility $u$, let

$$
\Delta_{z,u}(x)
=d_z(x)+\gamma\int_\Omega u(y)\,\nu_{x,z}(dy).
\tag{24}
$$

Assume the immediate contrast is score-visible, $d_z(x)=\bar d(S(x),z)$, and $S$ is a regular standard-Borel quotient. Let $\mathcal U_z$ be a uniformly bounded utility class containing zero and separating the relevant signed kernels. The unit ball of bounded Borel utilities is separating; a restricted natural-task class need not be.

**Theorem 4 (Architecture-agnostic lifecycle trichotomy).** Exactly one branch holds:

1. **Future-null:** $\nu_{x,z}=0$ for all $x,z$, so governance reduces to the immediate contrast.
2. **Non-null and lifecycle-complete:** some $\nu_{x,z}\ne0$, but $\nu_{x,z}$ is constant on every score fiber for every task. Then every $\Delta_{z,u}$ factors measurably through $S$, and the score is uniformly sufficient over the declared task class and all scalar cost shifts.
3. **Non-null and future-lossy:** there exist $x_1,x_2,z$ with $S(x_1)=S(x_2)$ but $\nu_{x_1,z}\ne\nu_{x_2,z}$. If $\mathcal U_z$ separates these kernels, some $u\in\mathcal U_z$ gives different lifecycle values. After the common midpoint cost shift

   $$
   \lambda^*
   =\frac{\Delta_{z,u}(x_1)+\Delta_{z,u}(x_2)}{2},
   \tag{25}
   $$

   every randomized score-only rule has two-state worst-case regret at least

   $$
   \boxed{
   \frac14|\Delta_{z,u}(x_1)-\Delta_{z,u}(x_2)|
   =\frac\gamma4
   \left|\int u\,d(\nu_{x_1,z}-\nu_{x_2,z})\right|
   >0}.
   \tag{26}
   $$

   If the original contrasts already have opposite signs, no cost shift is needed.

**Proof.** Either every signed kernel vanishes or some is nonzero. Conditional on non-nullness, the kernels are either constant on every score fiber and task or an explicit same-score violation exists, so the branches are exhaustive and mutually exclusive. Branch 1 follows from (24). In branch 2, fiber constancy, regular quotient factorization, and score-visible immediate cost make every contrast score-measurable, so Theorem 2 applies. In branch 3, separation supplies $u$ with $q_i=\int u\,d\nu_{x_i,z}$ and $q_1\ne q_2$. The immediate terms agree on the score fiber, so the lifecycle-value difference is $\gamma(q_1-q_2)$. Subtracting (25) creates symmetric gaps; the two-state minimax calculation in (11) gives one quarter of their original value difference, proving (26). $\square$

This does not assert that every LLM reads every memory or that every natural task values every transcript difference. It states the bounded universal result: a task-universal score for a non-null intervention-defined memory channel must preserve the complete task-relevant signed future-transcript effect.

**Corollary 4.1 (Task drift).** For $z\sim\mu$ and a jointly measurable selector $z\mapsto u_z$,

$$
\Delta_\mu(x)
=\int_{\mathcal Z}
\left[d_z(x)+\gamma\int_\Omega u_z(y)\,\nu_{x,z}(dy)\right]\mu(dz).
\tag{27}
$$

If the drift family contains all point masses, uniform sufficiency over all $\mu$ implies pointwise sufficiency for every $z$; a jointly measurable pointwise factorization through $(S,z)$ conversely implies mixture factorization. Task drift enlarges the challenge class but is unnecessary for branch-3 failure.

Define task-relative lifecycle equivalence by

$$
x\equiv_{\rm LC}x'
\Longleftrightarrow
d_z(x)=d_z(x')
\quad\text{and}\quad
\int u\,d\nu_{x,z}=\int u\,d\nu_{x',z}
\quad\forall z,\ u\in\mathcal U_z.
\tag{28}
$$

Then

$$
T_{\rm LC}^*(x):=[x]_{\equiv_{\rm LC}}
\tag{29}
$$

is the coarsest task-relative lifecycle sufficient information object in partition order: any statistic sufficient for every declared task utility and scalar cost shift must refine this partition. A standard-Borel controller-state representative additionally requires a smooth equivalence relation, for example a countable determining family. If $\mathcal U_z$ contains the unit ball of bounded Borel functions, the partition preserves exactly the immediate contrast and complete signed future-transcript kernel. For an approximation $T$, define

$$
\varepsilon_{\rm LC}(T)
=\sup_{T(x)=T(x')}
\sup_{z,u\in\mathcal U_z}
|\Delta_{z,u}(x)-\Delta_{z,u}(x')|.
\tag{30}
$$

Under finite measurable fiber endpoints, Corollary 2.1 gives per-task, per-cost fiber-wise minimax action regret at most $\varepsilon_{\rm LC}(T)/4$. One common rule covers multiple tasks only when $z$ is a controller input. Estimating a regular representation of $T_{\rm LC}^*$, or an auditable approximation with small $\varepsilon_{\rm LC}$, is therefore the target of SQCAD rather than current retrieval relevance alone. The issue is information loss, not scalar dimensionality: a smooth standard-Borel representative can in principle be scalar-encoded.

### 3.6 Probe and defer as Bellman actions

Let a probe cost $c_P>0$, leave the current physical state unchanged, and produce an observation $Z$ before the next decision. Define

$$
\operatorname{EVI}_t(P)
=\mathbb E_Z[V_{t+1}(b^Z,s)]-V_{t+1}(b,s).
$$

Comparing its Bellman value with reversible defer gives

$$
Q_t^P>Q_t^D
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\gamma\operatorname{EVI}_t(P)>c_P.
\tag{31}
$$

This is the general decision rule. SQCAD's interval and sub-Gaussian certificates should be presented as computable sufficient conditions for (31), not as additional main theorems. Probe is globally optimal only after its value also exceeds keep and archive.

### 3.7 Joint implication and scope

Theorem 1 identifies the lifecycle terms that memory governance must price. Theorem 2 states exactly when a current score preserves the zero-threshold action or the full cost-shifted contrast, and it bounds the price of approximate score sufficiency. Theorem 3 both signs the conditional recoverability-information term under a valid Blackwell comparison and lower-bounds authorization regret by the KL information retained in the Agent transcript. Theorem 4 exhausts the future-null, lifecycle-complete, and future-lossy cases above any particular memory implementation and identifies $T_{\rm LC}^*$ as the minimal task-relative estimand. The observational, crowding, censoring, interval, and certificate results in Appendix A are verified constructions or computable subclasses of this general spine, rather than additional main theorems.

The framework does not claim that every scalar score fails, that keep is generally preferable, or that raw LLM traces satisfy the assumed kernels and certificate contracts. These are tested through the transition/observation, score-fiber, recoverability, and held-out certificate audits in Section 5.
## 4 Method: SQCAD

SQCAD is the operational answer to §3: a three-layer closed loop that turns identification status into auditable authorization. The layers are **Evidence** (what must be logged), **Qualification** (when a persistent action is authorized), and **Access** (how authorized memories are projected into the budgeted workspace).

### 4.1 Closed-loop workflow

```
Evidence (immutable log: C_t, E_t, P_t, D_t, Y_t, A_t, v, s, budget)
  -> Proposer (frozen retriever: BM25 / dense / RRF) -> candidates + scope + version
  -> Qualification: certificate interval [L, U], diagnostics, expiry
       L > 0  -> authorize keep / restore
       U < 0  -> authorize archive
       L <= 0 <= U -> defer, or VOI-priced probe (probe result feeds back)
       C3/C8 failure -> unresolved / mismatch (persistent state is untouchable)
  -> Access: budgeted projection (qualification projection -> ranking projection top-B)
  -> one-shot workspace (exposure pool) -> exposure/adoption/outcome/cost logged back to Evidence
```

The Proposer decides *who is read first among already-authorized objects*; it can never decide who stays persistently. Persistent state changes flow **only** through the qualification-authorized path. This implements Theorem 1's separation between one-shot access projection and the persistent state transition whose continuation value must be authorized.

### 4.2 Evidence layer

Theorems 1--2 say what the Evidence layer must preserve: the immediate return, candidate/workspace transition, and conditional evidence experiment required to reconstruct or bound $\Delta_t$. The layer records $(C_t,E_t,P_t,D_t,A_t,Y_t,s,v,B_t)$ with action windows and rollbacks. Appendix A's C1--C9 family makes the observational route explicit; failure of those conditions widens a certificate or returns `unresolved/mismatch` rather than silently replacing the missing lifecycle state with a score.

### 4.3 Qualification layer: certificates, not scores

> **Certificate-subclass safety requirement.** A restore certificate must carry `(H, delta)` and a two-sided error budget. A positive probe is not sufficient authorization: the Appendix A safe-recovery subclass requires either its fixed-sample threshold or its anytime/stitched boundary; otherwise the output remains `unresolved/defer`. This is a conditional implementation contract, not a fourth main theorem.

For each candidate memory and epoch, Qualification computes the **identified interval** $[L,U]$ for the lifecycle contrast and issues four certificate states, implementing Corollary 2.1's score-fiber interval decision:

| Certificate | Condition | Persistent action |
|---|---|---|
| `point` / `bound` with $L > 0$ | interval strictly above zero | **authorize keep/restore** |
| `point` / `bound` with $U < 0$ | interval strictly below zero | **authorize archive** |
| `unresolved` | positivity (C3) or stability (C8) fails — no interval authorized | **no persistent change; defer** |
| `mismatch` | scope/version/evidence-source invalid (C8) | **re-qualification required** |

**Algorithm 1 — Qualification (interval-authorization rule).** Input: candidate memory $m$, log $O$ up to the epoch boundary, costs $(C_{\text{defer}}, C_{\text{probe}}, C_{\text{restore}})$, probe budget.
1. Estimate the identified interval $[L, U]$ for $\tau_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}, \texttt{archive})$ from the pre-decision window (estimator provenance per layer, §4.5: L1 protocol-route rollout estimator over controlled worlds; L2 public proxy certificate over observable update/version events; L3 event-based reference certificate over rule-world events). The honest interval estimator is implemented and verified only where ground truth is known — the L1 protocol route (bias $-0.47$, coverage 12/12, §5.1); the L2/L3 certificates are explicitly **proxy instantiations** without an estimated $[L,U]$ (event-based: update/version evidence for L2, rule-world events for L3), because honest intervals require same-future counterfactual rollouts that public traces cannot supply. The L3 event-based certificate is audited *as* the proxy it is — §5.3 diagnoses it as the degenerate default-keep behavior the interval rule fixes — and the v2 row instantiates the interval rule's *decision structure* (authorize iff the certificate leaves zero) over the event proxy.
2. If $L > 0$: certificate `positive`, **authorize keep/restore**. If $U < 0$: certificate `negative`, **authorize archive**.
3. If $L \le 0 \le U$: certificate `unresolved` (or `mismatch` on scope failure); choose the randomized fiber rule, defer, or probe by comparing their complete Bellman values (Theorem 1, Corollary 2.1, and Eq. 23).
4. Log the certificate, interval, and decision evidence so that the score-fiber claim is auditable.

`unresolved` is not a conservative preference. Theorem 2 shows that a crossing score fiber cannot support a deterministic zero-regret authorization, while Corollary 2.1 gives its exact randomized minimax regret $R^*(L,U)$ and upper bound by one quarter of the fiber width. Probe and defer remain separate Bellman actions rather than labels attached to the score.

**VOI probe.** A candidate with a crossing interval is probed only if its expected post-probe Bellman value exceeds defer after probe cost (Eq. 23). The interval proxy $C_{\text{probe}}+R^*(L',U')<\min\{R^*(L,U),C_{\text{defer}}\}$ is used only when these interval regrets are the declared continuation objective. Probe products are one-shot exposures and enter persistent state only after refreshed authorization.

### 4.4 Access layer: budgeted projection with a bounded guard

- **Qualification projection → ranking projection**: certified candidates are ranked into the workspace up to budget $B$; the ranking (Proposer) is frozen and exchangeable (BM25/dense/RRF) so that quality differences across policies isolate the *governance* decision, not the retriever.
- **Candidate guard**: at query time, up to $g \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ additional BM25 candidates enter the *one-shot* workspace without qualification. This repairs coverage bad cases (L2, §5.2) without relaxing long-term authorization — the guard changes "what can be seen now", never "who stays forever": its extra candidates are drawn from the already-persistent pool's one-shot projection, so persistent storage changes only up to eviction-order rounding. This is the mechanism-level explanation of why Guard improves Hit/Recall while storage stays essentially unchanged.
- **Probe/restore**: archived memories can be probed at a declared cost and restored only after authorization. Theorem 3(a) signs the conditional information term only under its common-state Blackwell contract; Theorem 3(b) counts which adaptive transcript branches provide distinguishing KL when that common-state condition does not hold. Workspace competition stays in the access term. Appendix A separately gives finite-horizon fixed-sample and anytime certificate bounds for an archived-committed Gaussian subclass.
- **Lineage/version gate**: conflicting versions of the same entity+slot+scope are tracked; an unresolved lineage conflict forces re-qualification rather than silent retention (L3 evidence, §5.3).

### 4.5 Derivation map: every mechanism is a theorem consequence

| Result | Identified deficiency | Framework requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Theorem 1 | current utility omits future access and evidence effects | Evidence records state transition and observation separately; costs stay in the Bellman ledger |
| Theorem 2 | a score may collapse action-relevant belief-states | Qualification acts on an interval/fiber certificate, never on an unqualified scalar alone |
| Corollary 2.1 | exact score sufficiency may fail approximately | crossing fibers expose $R^*(L,U)$, defer, and probe choices instead of hidden default actions |
| Theorem 2 constructions (Appendix A) | association/query-local effects omit co-exposure, crowding, bridge, and scope | Evidence logs propensity, position, adoption, budget, scope/version, and action windows |
| Theorem 3 | recoverability value is distinct from candidate/workspace value | Access exposes restore experiments and reports workspace effects separately |
| Theorem 4 | implementation-specific scores may omit task-relevant signed future-transcript effects | Qualification targets $T_{\rm LC}^*$ or an auditable representation with small $\varepsilon_{\rm LC}$; experiments locate systems in the trichotomy |
| Certificate subclasses (Appendix A) | unrestricted probing can violate false-restore control | Qualification uses two-sided fixed-sample or anytime boundaries under an explicit contract |

**Relationship to adjacent lines.** SQCAD is not a ranking function (the Proposer is frozen and exchangeable), not a write-time gate (access-time authorization with windows), not a post-hoc audit (decision-time), and not a benchmark (LifecycleBench is a companion asset, §5.3).

### 5.1 L1: Theory verification

The protocol runs in two stages: **Stage 1** evaluates the identification-recovery estimator with all C1–C8 conditions satisfied; **Stage 2** violates one condition at a time and checks the gate's response.

**Main-theorem witnesses and Appendix A constructions.** Finite diagnostics reproduce Theorem 1's three-term identity and the distinction between continuation and conditional-information VoR; Theorem 2's quotient-lumpability audit, admissible-value-separated signed-kernel opposite-action construction, least-favorable Bayes/minimax regret, and Corollary 2.1 quarter-width bound including the zero-width tie; Theorem 3(a)'s binary Blackwell monotonicity; Theorem 3(b)'s exponentially decreasing transcript-KL regret floor and priced cost--error frontier; Theorem 4's future-null/lifecycle-complete/future-lossy classification and exact midpoint-shift quarter-regret identity; and the strict probe/defer threshold. The older observational construction has max-field difference 0 with lifecycle values $+1650$ vs $-1100$; the query-local construction has identical local effects of 2.0 with lifecycle values $-1784$ vs $+1776$. These are theorem witnesses, not evidence that real baseline scores necessarily induce the same fibers or KL budgets.

**Identification recovery (Appendix A protocol route).** In a controlled world with known lifecycle values, the randomized-micro-intervention estimator achieves bias $-0.47$, 95% CI coverage $12/12$, zero confident decision errors, and `unresolved` for exactly three neutral memories. On the observational sign-flip construction, a log-measurable certificate is identical across both worlds and therefore crosses zero with probability at least $1-2\alpha$ under simultaneous honest coverage. This instantiates Theorem 2's crossing-fiber condition and the Appendix A decision-consistency corollary; it is not external validation of LLM certificate coverage.

Abstention is not free: it defers value (e.g., C7 defers the useful co-exposed memory worth 48); the framework's commitment is "never a confident wrong decision", not "abstention costs nothing".

**Fiber minimax verification (Theorem 2 and Corollary 2.1).** On the identical-log fiber $[L,U]=[-1650,1100]$, exhaustive search over log-measurable rules gives best deterministic worst-case regret 1100, while a 1,001-point randomized grid attains $660$ at keep probability $0.4$, exactly $U(-L)/(U-L)$. Classification-error minimax remains $1/2$ at keep probability $1/2$ and is deliberately separated from regret minimization. The general helper additionally verifies $R^*(L,U)\le(U-L)/4$ and zero regret on non-crossing fibers.

**Restricted-subclass numerics (Appendix A).** No-recovery committing policies reproduce slope $5.8500$; removing action-dependent evidence censoring drops it to $0.044/0.066$; the paired regret identity is $11700.0$ with max-regret bound $5850.0$. Fixed-threshold and safe-recovery formulas are checked only in their declared Gaussian/Bernoulli subclasses. These diagnostics support the mechanism and certificate implementations but are not additional general theorems.

### 5.3 L3: LifecycleBench — counterfactual lifecycle governance

**Why a self-built benchmark (and how we prevent it from being self-serving).** Public traces do not contain same-future `keep`/`archive` branches, so the object of the paper — the lifecycle value of a persistent action — cannot be measured on them. LifecycleBench generates 1,380 episodes over six mechanism families (rare-bridge, self-obscuring, hitchhiker, version-update, harmful-stale, scope-mismatch) plus stable/neutral controls; for each episode the *identical* future stream (10 tasks, fixed events, shared random seed per episode) is rolled out twice, once with the decision memory forced to `keep` and once to `archive`; the discounted value difference $\tau = V_{\text{keep}} - V_{\text{archive}}$ defines the hidden oracle label (neutral iff $|\tau| \le 0.5$). The benchmark is audited by an R1–R8 chain: metadata shortcut ceiling (R1), label sensitivity with 31 perturbed runs and a declared validity domain (R2), unseen-mechanism generalization (R3, 3 boundary cells reported), pre-registered winning-region hits (R4), an independent reference implementation (R5, 1,380/1,380 bit-identical), external human anchoring (R6, 28 blinded cases exported; judge batch pending), a public/hidden/release package with an anonymous scoring tool (R7), and an iteration log (R8). Public traces carry no hidden gold; the evaluator is the only component that reads hidden labels; baseline policies include non-SQCAD strategies (keep-all/archive-all/recency/frequency/storage rules) so the benchmark is not SQCAD-shaped by construction.

**Main finding**: Interval authorization achieves 89.7% of oracle value (+0.865 vs +0.964) with 7.2% false-commit rate and 90.7% oracle agreement, significantly outperforming all score-only baselines (Memory Worth −3.476, keep-all −10.280, Table below). Mechanism validation: ablation at the honest bucket-level unit count (n≈14 rule-world instantiations) shows censoring suppression +11.34 [0.90, 30.73] (significant), confirming Theorem 1's access-state prediction and Construction A.2's mechanism (archived memories stop competing for workspace exposure). Probe/restore is net-negative under this benchmark's prices (+0.99 [−3.66, 0.00] n.s.), consistent with L2's query-time probe value and the 10:1 cost-to-task-value ratio (probe 1.0, task 10.0).

**Two findings with teeth.** First, the original event certificate is degenerate: its nonnegative states silently default to keep. This shows that an event score without Theorem 2's fiber discipline leaves `unresolved` as a hidden action. Second, mechanism diagnoses remain useful: `lineage-conflict → archive` improves mean value by +8.62 in the version-update family, and hitchhiker memories should not default-keep in the constructed bucket. These observations motivate an explicit interval decision; they do not establish universal failure of the baseline scores.

**Theory-aligned variant (`sqcad_v2`).** SQCAD authorizes keep only on a strictly positive interval, archive only on a strictly negative interval, and otherwise takes a reversible defer path with priced probe/restore. This is Corollary 2.1's interval decision embedded in Theorem 1's four-action Bellman comparison. It coincides action-for-action with `probe_willing` by construction, a sanity check rather than a win. False commits drop 0.228 to 0.072, regret 1.258 to 0.115, and mean value $-0.278$ to $+0.865$; the gain remains non-significant at the honest unit count of about 14. Keep on a non-positive certificate with oracle archive is exactly 0.0. Of 480 unresolved decisions, 65.6% resolve toward archive, 3.1% are missed keeps, and 31.3% are neutral; none of the 15 missed keeps is rescueable within the ten-task horizon.

**Event-level cost attribution.** The defer path's cost is attributed by the ablation switches under the `sqcad_v2` decision (same frozen rule worlds; each switch removes one mechanism; bucket-level CIs in brackets). Only the censoring switch is significant: v2 beats its no-censoring ablation by +11.34 [0.90, 30.73] — without the censor, archived (harmful) memories keep competing for exposure and destroy value; this is the access-state mechanism isolated by Theorem 1 and Construction A.2 predicts, and the only effect that survives the honest unit count. Six ablation contrasts were tested; one significant effect is consistent with the censoring theory but is uncorrected for multiple comparisons, so we report it as mechanism-consistent, not as a standalone discovery. Removing probes *improves* value by +0.99 [−3.66, 0.00] (n.s.) and removing restore by +0.03 [−0.11, 0.00] (n.s.): the probe/restore channel measures net-negative on this benchmark — probes cost 1.0 per task and never rescued the decision memory (recovery 0/15) — so the mechanism story is "archive is right", not "archive is cheap to undo"; the channel's lifecycle-recovery value is not established here (its query-time value on L2 is separately reported, §5.2/§6(iii)). Lineage detection is exactly neutral under `sqcad_v2` (diff 0.0000): the Corollary A.7 interval rule subsumes the lineage-conflict fix because every conflicting `unresolved` candidate is refused by construction. A VOI-probe refinement that keeps rare single-session `unresolved` candidates (`sqcad_v2_probe`) loses −0.03 [−0.12, 0.00] (n.s.): the data prefer blanket defer-archive, and the rare-keep hypothesis is not supported on this benchmark. A willingness sweep over how many public sessions a crossing-interval memory may have and still be kept traces the value/cost frontier of that decision (cloud-GPU round-2, 34- report; frozen 1,380 episodes, bucket-paired bootstrap, seed 20260817): raising willingness from 1 to 2 sessions moves mean value 0.8499 → 0.8590 and regret 0.1294 → 0.1203 at commit precision 0.8125 → 0.7222 — more crossing commits buy value at the cost of commit precision, and the probe channel then changes no action (probe_action_change 0.0725 → 0.0000), consistent with the measured net-negative probe value above; no episode has three public sessions, so w3 ≡ w2; all contrasts remain statistically marginal at the honest unit count (CI lower bounds −0.0180 / 0.0000). The frontier is monotone in the theory's direction, but its gains are not established at n ≈ 14 — we report the sweep as a robustness boundary, not a new mechanism. (Cloud-GPU re-run of this supplement: 33- report; numbers above are from the same frozen seed set, bit-identical across environments.)

**Ablations and sensitivity.** no-censoring changes the value of 730/1,380 episodes (mean −10.71 under the `sqcad_v2` decision, matching the −10.71 ablation row in the frozen JSON); under `sqcad_v2` the other four switches become informative as reported above. R2 shows the label is fragile to GAMMA (36.2% flips at 0.7; 50 neutral-family episodes — 3.6% — still flip at 0.95/0.99 inside the declared validity domain GAMMA ∈ [0.9, 0.99]), while 0 flips occur under HARM_PENALTY and TASK_VALUE thresholds; the neutral-band width (TAU_TOL) is a flip driver (50 flips at 0.2, 450 at 1.0), as expected since it sets how many episodes land in the unresolved band. This is reported as the benchmark's economic-parameter sensitivity region, not hidden.

**What L3 can and cannot claim.** It directly scores authorization by lifecycle value, regret, false-commit, and recovery under a frozen counterfactual contract. It verifies the interval-rule identity, zero blind commits on non-positive certificates, and the direction of mechanism effects; only censoring suppression is significant at the honest unit count. It does not establish that LifecycleBench represents deployed Agent-memory distributions, that the probe/restore channel has positive lifecycle value under this parameterization, or that rule-world effects transport to public traces.

**Badcase-driven paired challenge (separate internal mechanism check).** To test whether the modular story survives an adversarial trigger rather than a single illustrative trace, we generated 64 independent semi-synthetic mechanism worlds and paired every policy on the identical candidate stream. The recovery module improved utility by `+3.285` (studentized 95% CI `[+3.272,+3.297]`) and adaptive budget by `+0.262` (`[+0.256,+0.295]`) in this internal contract. The original unconditional sentinel correction was a reproducible badcase: utility `−0.121` (`[−0.149,−0.087]`) and probe cost `+0.193` (`[+0.180,+0.203]`) relative to the hierarchical candidate. We therefore added a pre-specified full-cost sentinel gate: its utility gain over the unguarded sentinel is `+0.121` (`[+0.087,+0.149]`) and probe cost changes by `−0.193` (`[−0.203,−0.180]`). The guarded row collapses to the hierarchical candidate on this trigger, which is the intended refusal of an unauthorised exploratory action, not evidence that sentinel exploration is universally harmful. These numbers are internal mechanism evidence only and are excluded from the public-data main table (artifact: `results/minimal_framework_challenge_triggered_guarded_v2_20260821.json`; report 39-).

### 5.4 Fairness, significance, reproducibility

- **Statistical protocol.** Paired studentized bootstrap over the correct units (480 traces with needed evidence for LME-S — 500 total, 20 carry none, and hit/recall are undefined on them, so they are excluded from the bootstraps; 10 conversations for LoCoMo — the 1,986 QA are answer-scoring units, not independent samples; 1,380 episodes for L3), n_boot = 2000, pre-registered seeds, CI-excludes-zero significance. The tier-B substitute rows are run through the same paired protocol on the same units (two pre-registered seeds, 20260812/20260817, reported jointly; both agree on every comparison, §5.2). For L3, framework-claim comparisons are additionally (and primarily) judged at the rule-world bucket level (n ≈ 14): episodes within a bucket are surface replications of one mechanism instantiation — verified in the frozen data, 13/14 buckets have exactly one distinct value — so episode-level precision is not claimed.

## 6 Discussion

**What drives the improvements.** The candidate guard repairs one-shot visibility without changing persistent authorization, matching Theorem 1's access-state accounting. The lineage-conflict fix turns a crossing fiber into a negative one and is subsumed by Corollary 2.1's interval rule. Probe/restore is not assumed beneficial: Theorem 3(a) signs only its conditional information component under a common-state Blackwell comparison, while Theorem 3(b) says that reducing worst-world authorization regret requires sufficient KL-bearing evidence, not that any particular probe is worth its price. On L3 the complete channel is net-negative and rescues none of the 15 missed keeps; on L2 it remains useful for query-time coverage. This contrast is expected under the decomposition, not a contradiction.

**Public vs counterfactual evidence.** L2 claims a quality–storage–exposure trade-off under a unified contract; L3 claims same-future counterfactual governance value under a frozen rule world. Neither is a deployment-SOTA claim, and the paper never writes "low persistent storage" as "low total cost": exposure, probe, restore, and harm terms are reported alongside.

**Cost semantics.** In L2, exposure tokens are a genuine increase for SQCAD (2,722 vs 493–1,973 across the retrieval and tier-B rows); in L3, the utility contract charges 0.05 per exposed memory, 1.0 per probe, 0.01 per stored token per epoch — a framework that keeps everything is punished on storage and a framework that archives everything is punished on missed-commit and recovery.

**Positioning.** Generic POMDP and controlled-sensing theory already handles action-dependent state and observation kernels. SQCAD's distinction is the persistent Agent-memory specialization and its score-fiber consequence, not the generic control machinery. Relative to memory governance systems, it separates association or query-local effects from the lifecycle contrast and requires an authorization certificate. Appendix A's reduction separation remains restricted to the fully censored, no-restore committed subclass.

**Limitations.** (1) The three main theorems are conditional mathematical statements; the deployed Agent's action-dependent kernels, score-fiber oscillation, Blackwell relation, and per-action conditional KL require direct audits. (2) The observational lifecycle estimator and real-LLM certificate calibration are not implemented; Appendix A's decision-consistency guarantees require honest coverage. (3) Scope transport and external human anchoring remain open. (4) Named systems are disclosed by evidence tier; incomplete or proxy paths are not official results. (5) LifecycleBench is parameter-sensitive, and only censoring suppression is significant at the honest unit count. (6) Cost components are reported separately; end-to-end generation latency is not measured.

---

## 7 Conclusion

Persistent memory governance is a lifecycle control problem because authorization changes future access states and evidence experiments. Theorems 1--3 expose those terms, characterize score sufficiency, and price recoverability information. Theorem 4 closes the mathematical spine by proving that every intervention-defined memory architecture is future-null, lifecycle-complete, or future-lossy relative to a declared task class, and by identifying $T_{\rm LC}^*$ (or a small-$\varepsilon_{\rm LC}$ approximation) as the object a governance framework must estimate. SQCAD implements these results through Evidence, Qualification, and Access, with interval authorization and priced probe/defer actions; the earlier counterexamples and certificate results remain auditable Appendix A subclasses. Public traces establish a quality--storage--workspace trade-off, while LifecycleBench supplies controlled mechanism evidence with explicit statistical limits. The remaining burden is empirical and external-audit based rather than another certificate lemma: locate real systems in the trichotomy, estimate score-fiber lifecycle oscillation and per-action information budgets, validate recoverability value and certificate coverage, and independently check the measure-theoretic proof.

## Appendix

### A Verified constructions, restricted propositions, and certificate subclasses

**Coupled-world construction (shared exogenous stream).** All constructions below couple the K and A worlds on a single exogenous random stream $\mathcal{E} = (T_t, d_t, \varepsilon_t, E_t)_{t \ge 1}$: identical task arrivals $T_t$, identical task difficulty $d_t$, identical noise $\varepsilon_t$, and — given identical states and actions — identical exposure indicators $E_t$. The two worlds differ only in the *structural equation* mapping $(\cdot)$ to the outcome/value $Y_t$, never in the machinery that generates observations. Every difference between worlds is therefore a difference in how the *same* stream is valued, which is exactly the unidentifiable object.

**Lemma A.1 (observational equivalence).** Under the shared-coupling construction, the K and A worlds produce bit-identical observation logs on the pre-decision window $[1, n_{\text{early}}]$.

*Proof.* In the Theorem-1 pair the two structural equations evaluate to the *identical* outcome $\beta_1 E_t - \tfrac12 d_t + \varepsilon_t$ on the shared stream at every horizon — the sign of the target enters the estimand (the intervention target), never the observed mechanism — so the logs are bit-identical a fortiori, on $[1, n_{\text{early}}]$ and beyond. In the general constructions the early window is sign-free by design: early exposures and early observations depend only on the shared exogenous stream, which is drawn independent of the latent sign $\pm\tau$; under a censored (archived-committed) trajectory no further observations arrive in either world after the decision, so realized logs coincide there as well. Both worlds receive the same task sequence, the same candidates, and the same noise, so every field of the log — timestamps, queries, candidate ids, exposure indicators, observed outcomes — is algebraically identical on $[1, n_{\text{early}}]$. In the verified instance the two joint logs have maximum field difference $0.0$. $\square$

**Construction A.1 (observational non-identifiability witness for Theorem 2).** There exist two worlds $M_1, M_2$ sharing a bit-identical observational stream on which the lifecycle estimand $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ has opposite signs.

*Proof (construction).* Fix the shared exogenous stream and set exposure $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ independent of difficulty, $E_t \perp d_t$. Consider two memories $i$ (the decision memory) and $j$ (a co-exposed companion) whose exposure indicators are always equal, $E_{i,t} = E_{j,t} =: E_t$, so the log records one exposure indicator per step. Define two structural models, identical in every observable respect, written per memory:

$$
M_1:\; Y_t = \beta_1\, E_{i,t} + 0 \cdot E_{j,t} - \tfrac{1}{2}\, d_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \beta_1 > 0 ,
$$

$$
M_2:\; Y_t = \beta_{2s}\, E_{i,t} + \beta_{2p}\, E_{j,t} - \tfrac{1}{2}\, d_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \beta_{2s} < 0,\;\; \beta_{2s} + \beta_{2p} = \beta_1 .
$$

In $M_1$, memory $i$ is genuinely useful and $j$ is a passenger; in $M_2$, $i$ genuinely degrades the outcome ($\beta_{2s} < 0$) while $j$ is the useful memory, its effect $\beta_{2p} = \beta_1 - \beta_{2s} > \beta_1$ compensating so that the *joint* exposure coefficient is identical. The two-memory structure is necessary, not cosmetic: a single variable $E$ forces the total structural effect of exposure to equal the observed coefficient, so identical logs would force identical structural effects and no sign flip; the companion $j$ — whose generation is action-invariant — is what allows the observed joint coefficient to match ($\beta_{2s} + \beta_{2p} = \beta_1$) while $i$'s own effect, the only part the persistent action touches, flips sign. On the shared stream $E_i = E_j = E_t$, both equations evaluate to $Y_t = \beta_1 E_t - \tfrac{1}{2} d_t + \varepsilon_t$ at every horizon: identical realized outcomes, identical joint laws of $(C_t, E_t, Y_t)$ — the two worlds are observationally indistinguishable from any log of any length, and every log-measurable association estimate (association, gated association) evaluates the same functional of the identical log and returns $\beta_1$ in both worlds.

The lifecycle estimand separates them because the persistent action touches only $i$'s generation mechanism. Let $n_{\text{early}}$ be the decision point (end of the pre-decision window) and $H'$ the post-decision rollout length over which lifecycle value accrues, as in §3.1; the contrast therefore sums over $(n_{\text{early}}, n_{\text{early}}+H']$. Under $\text{do}(\texttt{keep}_i)$, $i$ remains in the candidate-generation rule: $E_{i,t} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$ for $t \in (n_{\text{early}}, n_{\text{early}}+H']$. Under $\text{do}(\texttt{archive}_i)$, $i$ is excluded from the mechanism, $E_{i,t} := 0$, while $j$'s generation is unaffected (co-occurrence is not causation: $i$'s action neither generates nor suppresses $j$). $j$'s contribution $\beta_{2p} E_{j,t}$ is therefore action-invariant and cancels in the contrast, giving

$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive})
= \sum_{t = n_{\text{early}}+1}^{n_{\text{early}}+H'} \mathbb{E}[\beta_i\, E_{i,t} \mid \text{do}(\texttt{keep})] - \mathbb{E}[\beta_i\, E_{i,t} \mid \text{do}(\texttt{archive})]
= \beta_i \cdot p \cdot H' ,
$$

which is $\beta_1 \cdot p \cdot H' > 0$ in $M_1$ (keep correct) and $\beta_{2s} \cdot p \cdot H' < 0$ in $M_2$ (archive correct). This is the sign-flip pair used by Corollaries A.6--A.7 with $|\tau_1| = |\beta_1| p H'$, $|\tau_2| = |\beta_{2s}| p H'$.

No estimator measurable in the log alone — association, gated association, or query-local effect — can be sign-consistent on this pair: the perfect co-exposure makes $E_i$ and $E_j$ never vary separately in observed data, so any effect estimate computable from the log identifies the *joint* coefficient $\beta_1$ in both worlds, and the SCM-level local effect of exposing $i$ alone ($\beta_{2s}$ in $M_2$) is reachable only by intervening on $i$ while holding $j$ fixed — the protocol route of Identification family A.3/§5.1, exactly the interventional access the pair denies to log-measurable rules. (The insufficiency of *reachable* local effects — identical values across memories with opposite lifecycle signs — is Construction A.2.) $\square$

**Construction A.2 (query-local insufficiency witness for Theorem 2).** There exist worlds in which two candidate memories have *identical* source-period local effects yet *opposite* lifecycle signs, via future-rollout channels invisible to the local effect.

*Proof (construction).* Consider candidate memories $i$ (high-volume, non-critical) and $j$ (sparse) for a single rare critical memory $k$. In the source period, both $i$ and $j$ serve identical queries with identical marginal quality, so both have local effect $\alpha > 0$ (Definition, §3.1) — and $\alpha$ is all any query-local estimand can measure.

Future rollout, horizon $H'$: memory $i$ crowds out $k$. Each exposure of $i$ displaces $k$ from the store with probability $\rho_c$ (per-exposure collision), and crowding is permanent — the displacement is not reversed by later traffic. The number of steps for which $k$ remains accessible is $\sum_{t=1}^{H'} (1 - \rho_c p)^{t-1} = \frac{1 - (1 - \rho_c p)^{H'}}{\rho_c p} \approx 8$ (geometric sum; the first crowding event occurs at expected time $1/(\rho_c p)$; with $\rho_c = 0.25$, $p = 0.5$, $H' = 2000$ this is exactly $\frac{1 - 0.875^{2000}}{0.125} = 8.0$), leaving $H' - 8 \approx 1992$ crowded-out steps on which rare tasks fail. Memory $j$ provides a retrieval bridge: keeping $j$ routes rare queries to $k$ with success probability $r_{\text{high}} = 0.7$; archiving $j$ removes the channel, success $r_{\text{low}} = 0$.

Writing $\beta_k$ for the value of a successful rare-task step and $q_{\text{rare}}$ for the rare-task arrival rate, the lifecycle differences are

$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_i) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_i)
= -\, 0.7\, \beta_k\, q_{\text{rare}}\left[ H' - \frac{1 - (1 - \rho_c p)^{H'}}{\rho_c p} \right] < 0 ,
$$

$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_j) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_j)
= \beta_k\, q_{\text{rare}}\, H'\, (r_{\text{high}} - r_{\text{low}})
= 0.7\, \beta_k\, q_{\text{rare}}\, H' > 0 .
$$

Local effects are identical ($\alpha$ for both), lifecycle signs are opposite; the identifiability gap is driven entirely by future-rollout structure — crowding-out latency for $i$, retrieval reach for $j$ — none of which appears in the source-period log. $\square$

**Identification family A.3 (C1--C9; sufficient, not minimal).** C1 consistency; C2 sequential exchangeability at epoch boundary; C3 positivity (observational overlap or protocol randomization); C4 treatment observability; C5 exposure/position/budget observability; C6 adoption measurement with error model; C7 interference specification (co-memory composition in state, explicit budget); C8 measurement stability (scope/version strata). For the observational route with target policy equal to behavior policy ($\pi=\pi_b$), C1–C8 plus finite second moments, positivity bounded away from zero, cross-fitting (or an equivalent empirical-process condition), and a nuisance product-rate condition identify the sequential g-formula and support the epoch-level DR functional. For $\pi\neq\pi_b$, an additional C9 is required: the full post-action transition-density ratio under the target policy must be known/consistently estimable with finite second moment; action/exposure overlap alone is insufficient. The randomized protocol-route rollout estimator for an actually executed target policy is unbiased with honest CIs. C6/C7 failure widens the certificate interval to bounds (adoption-error bounds via the error model; bundle-retreat bounds for interference, Lemma B), C3/C8 failure yields `unresolved`/`mismatch`. Lemma A–D: each of C6/C7/C2–C3/C8 is replaceable in some world — the family is sufficient, not minimal.

**Lemma A.2 (silent freeze).** If $p_{\text{arch}} = 0$, the archived state generates no new observations: the policy's information set is frozen from the decision point until a state transition, and a committing policy has no observation-generating action to revise it.

*Proof.* Exposure is the only source of new observations. In the archived state $P(e_t = 1) = 0$ a.s., so no candidate, query, or evidence value enters the log; the information set satisfies $I_t = I_{n_{\text{early}}}$ for all $t$ while the state remains archived. A committing policy (no probe/restore channel) has no action that transitions the state or produces observations, so the information set is frozen until the horizon. $\square$

**Proposition A.4 (committing-regret lower bound).** *Proof.* (i) By Lemma A.1, the two worlds share a bit-identical log on the pre-decision window; the decision at $n_{\text{early}}$ is measurable in $I_{n_{\text{early}}}$, hence the same action in both worlds. (ii) A wrong decision loses $\nu$ per step: in the K world the oracle keeps (earning $+10$ per exposed step), while an archived state has $p_{\text{arch}} = 0$, so exposure stops and the value is $0$ — loss $\nu = \tau_{\text{step}} p = 10 \times 0.6 = 6.0$ per step; in the A world the oracle archives (value $0$), while keeping exposes at rate $p$ and earns $-10$ per exposed step — expected loss $\nu$ per step. (iii) The policy is committing: no state transition and, by Lemma A.2, no new information, so the loss is never revised. Therefore the world in which the common decision is wrong has $R_T(\pi) = \nu (T - n_{\text{early}}) = 6.0 \times 1950 = 11700 = \Theta(T)$, and every deterministic committing rule has worst-world regret at least this amount; a randomized rule with keep probability $p$ has worst-world regret at least $\nu(T-n_{\text{early}})/2$. The bound covers calibrated gates: a gate that resolves the identified set to the wrong sign (the early stream is confounded) commits with calibrated probabilities and still fails identically. Six committing strategies reproduce slope $5.8500$ exactly over 12 seeds (closed-form arithmetic, §5.1). $\square$

**Diagnostic A.4b (one-sided Bernoulli restore).** *Proof.* In the explicit model, each post-decision step independently probes with probability $q$, each probe independently succeeds with probability $\rho = \Phi((\tau_{\text{step}} - \theta)/\sigma)$, and the first success triggers an absorbing permanent restore at cost $c_{\text{restore}}$. Therefore the first-success time is geometric with parameter $q\rho$, so $\mathbb{E}[t^* - n_{\text{early}}] = 1/(q\rho)$, while the expected number of probe attempts is $1/\rho$. Until the restore, the policy is wrong at rate $\nu$ per step (the argument of (a)(ii)), each attempt costs $c_{\text{probe}}$, and the restore costs $c_{\text{restore}}$ once:

$$
\mathbb{E}[R_T] \le \nu \cdot \mathbb{E}[t^* - n_{\text{early}}] + c_{\text{probe}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[N_{\text{attempts}}] + c_{\text{restore}}
\le \frac{\nu}{q\rho} + \frac{c_{\text{probe}}}{\rho} + c_{\text{restore}} = O(1/q),
$$

independent of $T$. No claim about an arbitrary sequential posterior rule follows from this explicit model. Numerics: $q = 0.01/0.05/0.2$ give slopes $2.454/0.425/0.110$; these are diagnostic checks of the declared simulator, whose score includes lifecycle loss but excludes probe/restore expenditure. $\square$

**Proposition A.4c (action-independent-support checkpoint).** *Proof.* If $p_{\text{arch}} = p$, exposure continues at rate $p$ in the archived state. For $k$ independent observations with variance $\sigma^2$, $\mathrm{Var}(\bar X_k)=\sigma^2/k$, and Chebyshev gives $P(\bar X_k \le \theta) \le \sigma^2/[k(\tau_{\text{step}}-\theta)^2]$. Thus the fixed checkpoint $k_0 = \lceil \sigma^2/(\delta(\tau_{\text{step}}-\theta)^2)\rceil$ authorizes correction with probability at least $1-\delta$. This is a finite-sample checkpoint statement, not an expected stopping-time guarantee for an arbitrary sequential gate. The contrast with (a) is the mechanism: with $p_{\text{arch}} = 0$ the same gate cannot correct because Lemma A.2 freezes its information set. Numerics: slopes drop to $0.0440$ (W0) / $0.0663$ (W1). $\square$

**Definition A.1 (faithful feedback-preserving reduction).** A reduction $R = (R_W, R_\pi, \phi)$ from a problem class $\mathcal{C}$ to $\mathcal{C}'$ consists of (i) a world map $R_W$ assigning each source world $W$ an image world $W' = R_W(W)$; (ii) a strategy map $R_\pi$ pulling target policies back to source policies ($\pi' \mapsto \pi$), preserving action sets; (iii) a **world-independent** observation map $\phi$ — a function of observed histories only, never of latent world identity; (iv) feedback preservation: image observations of $\pi'$ under $R_W(W)$ equal $\phi$ of source observations of $R_\pi(\pi')$ under $W$; (v) fidelity: regret is preserved up to $o(T)$ per policy. The $o(T)$ slack is deliberate: even reductions that perturb per-policy regret sublinearly are faithful, so the obstruction is the world-independent map, never the regret-tracking clause.

**Lemma A.3 (reduction invariance for the censored-committed subclass).** Under a faithful reduction whose source pullback remains archived after $n_{\text{early}}$ and has no restore/probe action, the coupled K/A pair maps to image processes that are observationally identical: any deterministic target policy in this image subclass makes the same action sequence in both images.

*Proof.* In the stated subclass, the archive censors all post-decision observations in both worlds, so the source histories are identical after $n_{\text{early}}$; world-independence of $\phi$ and feedback preservation then imply identical image histories and actions. The lemma does not cover policies that keep or restore access, because those policies can observe world-dependent continuation outcomes. $\square$

**Lemma A.4 (pointwise regret identity on the censored-committed subclass).** For any coupled strategy in that subclass, $\text{Regret}_K(\pi) + \text{Regret}_A(\pi) = \nu (T - n_{\text{early}}) = 11700.0$ deterministically.

*Proof.* Let $E$ be the number of exposed steps after $n_{\text{early}}$ under $\pi$ — identical in both worlds because the subclass remains archived and fully censored. In the K world the oracle keeps and earns $\nu$ per exposed step, so $\text{Regret}_K = \nu(T - n_{\text{early}}) - \nu E$. In the A world the oracle archives and earns $0$; $\pi$'s exposure earns $-\nu E$, so $\text{Regret}_A = \nu E$. The sum is $\nu(T - n_{\text{early}})$. $\square$

**Proposition A.5 (restricted reduction separation).** For any faithful reduction whose image is restricted to the censored-committed subclass, every target strategy has worst-case regret at least $\tfrac12\nu(T-n_{\text{early}})-o(T)$. *Proof.* Fix any target strategy $\pi'$ in this subclass; let $\pi = R_\pi(\pi')$. By Lemma A.3, $\pi'$ makes the same action sequence in both image worlds, so by fidelity its image regrets equal the source regrets up to $o(T)$: $\text{Regret}_{R_W(W)}(\pi') = \text{Regret}_W(\pi) \pm o(T)$. Then

$$
\max_{W \in \{K,A\}} \text{Regret}_{R_W(W)}(\pi')
\ge \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\text{Regret}_K(\pi) + \text{Regret}_A(\pi)\bigr) - o(T)
= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\nu\,(T - n_{\text{early}}) - o(T) = 5850 - o(T),
$$
by Lemma A.4. The result is intentionally restricted: a reduction that exposes continuation feedback or adds a restore/lineage channel is outside the subclass and requires a separate analysis; it cannot be used as a counterexample to this restricted statement. $\square$

**Corollary A.6 (two-point fiber minimax).** *Proof.* Let $p = P(\text{commit } \texttt{keep})$, measurable in the log; the log is bit-identical across worlds, so the randomization cannot condition on world identity. In the keep-correct world ($\tau = +1100$), an archive decision loses $1100$: $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = +1100] = (1-p) \cdot 1100$. In the archive-correct world ($\tau = -1650$), a keep decision loses $1650$: $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = -1650] = p \cdot 1650$. The worst case is $\max\{(1-p)\cdot 1100,\; p \cdot 1650\}$, minimized at the balance point $(1-p) \cdot 1100 = p \cdot 1650$, i.e., $p^* = 1100/2750 = 0.4$, with value $660 = 1100 \cdot 1650 / 2750$ — the minimax regret of every committing rule. The transposed sign assignment swaps which magnitude is keep-correct; the minimax value is labeling-invariant, and the $p^* = 0.4$ reported here is the one the exhaustive grid of §5.1 verifies. Classification error: any log-measurable rule decides identically in both worlds and therefore errs in exactly one; under the uniform prior the error is exactly $1/2$ for *every* rule — the regret minimizer $p^* = 0.4$ and the error minimizer $p = 1/2$ differ, and conflating them is a bug, not a choice. $\square$

**Corollary A.7 (interval authorization specialization).** *Proof.* (a) If $L > 0$, every compatible world has $\tau \ge L > 0$, so committing `keep` is correct in all of them: worst-case regret $0$. (b) If $U < 0$, symmetrically, committing `archive` has worst-case regret $0$. (c) If $L \le 0 \le U$, generalize the Corollary A.6 derivation to the interval: for $p = P(\text{keep})$, $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = U] = (1-p)U$ and $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = L] = p(-L)$; the worst case $\max\{(1-p)U,\; p(-L)\}$ is minimized at $(1-p)U = p(-L)$, i.e., $p^* = U/(U-L)$, giving $R^*(L,U) = U(-L)/(U-L)$, attained by the minimax rule. `defer` makes no persistent change and carries committing-action regret $0$ at explicit price $C_{\text{defer}}$; a probe costs $C_{\text{probe}}$ and shrinks the identified set to $[L', U']$, after which the decision costs $R^*(L', U')$. The optimal decision is $\min\{R^*(L,U),\; C_{\text{defer}},\; C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L',U')\}$; the probe is worth paying iff $C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L',U') < \min\{R^*(L,U), C_{\text{defer}}\}$. Verified instance $(L,U) = (-1650, 1100)$: commit-keep regret $-L = 1650$, commit-archive regret $U = 1100$, minimax $R^* = 1100 \cdot 1650/2750 = 660$ at $p^* = 1100/2750 = 0.4$, matching the exhaustive grid. $\square$

**Proposition A.8 (fixed-sample Gaussian probe threshold).** *Proof.* For $y \sim \mathcal{N}(\pm\mu, \sigma^2)$, the likelihood-ratio test thresholds the sample mean at zero and has maximal error $e_n^* = \Phi(-|\mu|\sqrt n/\sigma)$. Solving $e_n^* \le \delta$ gives the integer threshold $n \ge \lceil(\sigma\Phi^{-1}(1-\delta)/|\mu|)^2\rceil$. A distribution-agnostic Bretagnolle--Huber/Le Cam argument gives only the weaker order lower bound $n \ge \log(1/(4\delta))/\mathrm{KL}(P_+\|P_-)$. Both statements are fixed-sample; no sequential stopping or coverage claim follows. The Gaussian mean $\mu$ is the probe-signal parameter, distinct from the per-step loss rate $\nu = \tau_{\text{step}} p$ of §3.4. $\square$

**Proposition A.9 (fixed-threshold recovery cost).** *Proof.* Write $H=T-n_{\text{early}}$ and let $S_N=\infty$ if $N$ successes are not collected by the horizon. The lifecycle loss before restoration is exactly $\nu\min\{S_N,H\}$. For any nonnegative integer-valued stopping time, the survival-sum identity gives

$$
\mathbb{E}[\min\{S_N,H\}]=\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\Pr(S_N>t).
$$

Conditional on the past, the next-step success probability is at most $q\rho$. Coupling with iid Bernoulli$(q\rho)$ trials yields $\Pr(S_N>t)\ge\Pr\{\operatorname{Bin}(t,q\rho)<N\}$, proving the finite-horizon lower bound. The probe term is exactly $c_{\text{probe}}\mathbb{E}[A_H]$ and need not equal $c_{\text{probe}}N/\rho$ when the horizon ends early. If $H\to\infty$ and restoration occurs almost surely, monotone convergence gives the stated no-truncation limits. $\square$

**Withdrawn claim (historical one-sided matching).** The former statement that the one-sided SQCAD restore calculation matches the probe lower bound is withdrawn: it compared different policy classes and omitted false restore in $W^-$. The replacement is Propositions A.11--A.12 (Appendix A), where the same `Safe(H,delta)` class, finite horizon, and false-restore budget are used on both sides. $\square$

**Corollary A.10 (honest interval authorization).** Fix any compatible world with lifecycle contrast $\tau$ and a certificate interval $[\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$ with finite-sample honest coverage $\mathbb{P}(\tau \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]) \ge 1 - \alpha$.

(i) If the rule confidently authorizes `keep` ($\hat{L} > 0$) and the authorization is wrong ($\tau \le 0$), then $\tau \le 0 < \hat{L}$, so $\tau \notin [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$. If it confidently authorizes `archive` ($\hat{U} < 0$) and the authorization is wrong ($\tau \ge 0$), then $\tau \ge 0 > \hat{U}$, also $\tau \notin [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$. Hence {confident authorization is wrong} $\subseteq$ {$\tau \notin [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$}, and by honesty
$$
\mathbb{P}(\text{confident authorization is wrong}) \le 1 - (1 - \alpha) = \alpha,
$$
uniformly over the compatible class. $\square$

(ii) On a Construction A.1 sign-flip pair ($\tau_1 > 0 > \tau_2$), the two worlds share a bit-identical log (Lemma A.1), and the certificate is a function of the log and pre-registered randomness, so the *identical* interval $[\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$ is produced in both worlds. Honesty in world 1 and in world 2, together with the union bound, gives
$$
\mathbb{P}\bigl(\tau_1, \tau_2 \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]\bigr) \ge 1 - 2\alpha.
$$
Since $\tau_2 < 0 < \tau_1$ and $[\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$ is an interval, on this event $\hat{L} \le \tau_2 < 0 < \tau_1 \le \hat{U}$, i.e., $0 \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$: the gate authorizes neither `keep` nor `archive` and defers or probes (Corollary A.7) with probability $\ge 1 - 2\alpha$. On the complementary event (probability $\le 2\alpha$), any authorized action that is wrong is still charged against (i) — its world's $\tau$ lies outside the interval. $\square$

(iii) Consistency of the certificate gives $w_T = \hat{U} - \hat{L} \to 0$ in probability (protocol-route estimator, Identification family A.3: width shrinks at the parametric rate). Fix $\tau > 0$ and threshold sequences $\delta_T \to 0$, $\alpha_T \to 0$ with $\delta_T / w_T \to \infty$. On the event $\{\tau \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]\}$ (probability $\ge 1 - \alpha_T$), we have $\hat{L} \ge \tau - w_T \ge \delta_T - w_T > 0$ eventually, so $\mathbb{P}(\text{confident keep}) \ge 1 - \alpha_T \to 1$. The symmetric argument for $\tau \le -\delta_T$ gives $\mathbb{P}(\text{confident archive}) \to 1$; combined with (i), the authorized action is correct with probability tending to 1. $\square$

**Identification family A.3 scope and estimator regularity.** The C1–C8 sentence above is an identification statement for the behavior-policy observational route ($\pi=\pi_b$), not by itself a finite-sample confidence or rate theorem. A target policy $\pi\neq\pi_b$ additionally needs C9 full-trajectory policy transport; a static treatment DR expression does not provide that transport. The protocol-route rollout estimator is unbiased only when the intervention is genuinely randomized (or assigned treatment probabilities are known), rollout draws are independent conditional on the frozen source state, and the lifecycle return has finite second moment; its interval coverage is the coverage of the declared Monte Carlo/bootstrap procedure under those conditions. The doubly robust root-$n$ statement additionally requires positivity bounded away from zero, cross-fitting (or an equivalent empirical-process condition), finite variance, and a nuisance product-rate condition such as $|\hat m-m\|_2\|\hat g-g\|_2=o_p(n^{-1/2})$; “one nuisance correct” is not sufficient without these regularity conditions. The current implementation verifies the protocol route in controlled worlds, but does not implement the full observational DR lifecycle estimator. The 12/12 coverage number is therefore a protocol-route numerical check, not external validation of real LLM certificate coverage.

**Certificate versus metric CI.** The paired studentized CIs in the L2 tables quantify policy metrics (hit, recall, exposure, storage) under a frozen reader; they are not the honest lifecycle certificate intervals assumed by Corollary A.10. Corollary A.10 is applied only when a certificate has the stated coverage property; no such coverage claim is made for raw L2 LLM or retrieval rows.

### A.1 Safe-recovery certificate subclass

The old one-sided Bernoulli restore calculation is not a minimax result because it does not control false restore in the negative world. We therefore use the following replacement theorem, stated in full in `17-安全恢复证书定理与匹配下界-20260821.md` and implemented by `src/sqcad/safe_recovery_theory.py`.

**Safe class.** The policy starts archived, cannot default keep, obtains evidence only through probes with per-step success probability at most `q rho`, and must satisfy `P_-(keep by H) <= delta`. It terminates after either keep or permanent archive; no probe is allowed after terminal archive. Lifecycle loss is charged when an epoch starts archived, so an epoch that ends in probe-triggered keep is still charged `nu` in `W+`; a false keep in `W-` costs at most `nu H`.

**Proposition A.11 (safe fixed-sample upper bound).** Let `n_delta = ceil((sigma Phi^{-1}(1-delta)/mu)^2)`. The policy that waits for `n_delta` successful probes and keeps iff their mean is positive satisfies

$$
\sup_W \mathbb E_W C_H^{tot}
\le \frac{\nu n_\delta}{q\rho} + \frac{c_{\mathrm{probe}}n_\delta}{\rho} + c_{\mathrm{restore}} + \nu H\delta .
$$

The `nu H delta` term is essential: it prices false restore. At `delta=1/H`, the bound is `O(log H)` for fixed signal and channel parameters.

**Proposition A.12 (same-class lower bound).** For every policy in the same safe class, either `P_+(keep by H)<1-delta`, in which case `E_+R_H^{life} >= nu delta H`, or `P_+(keep by H)>=1-delta`, in which case change of measure and Wald's identity give. Let `T_H^{arch}` denote the number of epochs that start archived over the whole horizon, including the terminal-keep epoch and all epochs after a terminal archive (and `H` if keep never occurs); this is exactly the quantity that multiplies the per-epoch lifecycle loss.

$$
\mathbb E_+[N_H]\ge \frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{2\mu^2/\sigma^2},
\qquad
\mathbb E_+[C_H^{tot}]\ge
\frac{\nu\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{(2\mu^2/\sigma^2)q\rho}
 + \frac{c_{\mathrm{probe}}\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{(2\mu^2/\sigma^2)\rho}
 + c_{\mathrm{restore}}(1-\delta).
$$

Thus the lifecycle, probe, and restore terms in the upper and lower bounds share the declared total-cost order within the same class; the probe term uses Wald's identity. No claim is made for default-keep policies, arbitrary sequential posterior gates, latent-world side information, or general agent-memory distributions.

**Proposition A.13 (anytime Qualification certificate).** To mirror the online gate, define

$$
b_n(\alpha)=\sigma\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}\log\frac{\pi^2n^2}{3\alpha}}.
$$

After every successful probe, authorize `keep` iff `mean_n-b_n(alpha)>0`, authorize terminal `archive` iff `mean_n+b_n(alpha)<0`, and otherwise remain `unresolved`. The union bound gives simultaneous coverage at least `1-alpha`; for nonzero `tau`, a wrong one-sided authorization is therefore no more likely than `alpha`, while `tau=0` remains unresolved by interpretation. If `|tau|>=Delta` and `n_Delta` is the first `n` with `b_n(alpha)<Delta/2`, the correct action is reached by `n_Delta` successful probes on the coverage event, with

$$
\sup_{|\tau|\ge\Delta}\mathbb E[C_H^{tot}]\le
\frac{\nu n_\Delta}{q\rho}+\frac{c_{\mathrm{probe}}n_\Delta}{\rho}+c_{\mathrm{restore}}+(\nu+c_{\mathrm{probe}})H\alpha.
$$

The tail term prices the confidence-complement event: at most `nu H` lifecycle loss and `H` further probe attempts can occur there. Because the bad event is transcript-dependent, its probe count cannot be multiplied by the marginal attempt rate `q`. Proposition A.12 supplies the lower bound for the same Gaussian two-point subclass. The matching statement is only for the sufficiently long, margin-separated regime; at shorter horizons the alternative `nu alpha H` branch applies. The margin band `|tau|<Delta` is deliberately unresolved; no automatic authorization is claimed there. This anytime result, rather than the historical one-probe restore rule, is the direct mathematical specification of SQCAD Qualification.

For the tighter rate, we use geometric stitching: on integer epoch `m_k=ceil(eta^k),...,M_k=ceil(eta^(k+1))-1`, allocate `alpha_k=6 alpha/(pi^2(k+1)^2)` and use `b_n^stitch = sigma sqrt(2(M_k/m_k) log(2/alpha_k)/n)`. A maximal sub-Gaussian inequality gives simultaneous coverage `1-alpha` with only `log log n` overhead. This is reported as near-minimax, not as an exact sequential constant; the larger certificate sample count is an explicit safety cost, not hidden tuning.

**Certificate–Censoring Bridge (B1–B4).** The preceding theorems apply directly to the implemented `QualificationAccess` path only under an explicit certificate contract: (C1) the candidate remains archived before terminal authorization and cannot be default-kept; (C2) probe attempts are predictable, occur at most once per epoch, and have conditional rate at most `q`; (C3) a successful attempt yields an observation with success channel `rho`; (C4) successful observations are conditionally `sigma`-sub-Gaussian around a fixed lifecycle contrast `tau`; and (C5) the interval is exactly `[mean_n-b_n(alpha), mean_n+b_n(alpha)]`, with strict one-sided authorization. Under this contract: (B1) `anytime_qualification_certificate` plus `authorized_action` is pathwise equivalent to the Proposition A.13 three-way gate, while `SequentialQualificationGate` enforces terminal no-probe semantics; (B2) for nonzero `tau`, wrong persistent authorization has probability at most `alpha`; (B3) for `|tau|>=Delta`, the correct action is authorized by the first `n_Delta` successful probes on the coverage event; and (B4) Proposition A.13's total-cost upper bound transfers to the code path when the unresolved gate uses the declared attempt rate exactly `q` (or an independently verified lower rate). An upper bound on a slower schedule is enough for safety but not for the displayed waiting-cost upper bound. The proof is in Appendix A and the contract-level implementation tests are in `tests/test_qualification_access.py`. This bridge is deliberately conditional: it does not claim that raw LLM certificates, estimated variance, drifting lifecycle values, or dependent probe outcomes automatically satisfy C2–C4.

*Proof of the bridge.* For B1, the code computes the same successful-probe mean and radius as the gate, and uses strict `lower>0`/`upper<0`; a crossing interval therefore returns `unresolved`, while retrieval rank cannot enter the persistent-action branch before authorization. For B2, for nonzero `tau`, conditional sub-Gaussian concentration and the summable `n^{-2}` allocation give simultaneous coverage at least `1-alpha`; on that event a positive authorization implies `tau >= lower > 0`, and a negative authorization implies `tau <= upper < 0`. The zero-contrast case is intentionally unresolved rather than assigned a correct sign. For B3, on the same event `lower_{n_Delta} >= tau-2b_{n_Delta}>0` when `tau>=Delta`, with the negative case symmetric. Finally, C1–C3 give the same negative-binomial/Wald bookkeeping as Proposition A.13: the unconditioned contribution of the coverage event is bounded by `n_Delta/(q rho)` expected lifecycle waiting and `n_Delta/rho` expected attempts (not a conditional expectation after conditioning), while the complement contributes at most `(nu+c_probe)H alpha`; hence B4. ∎

**Lemma A.14 (unresolved margin lower bound).** For the two worlds `tau=+epsilon` and `tau=-epsilon`, any policy that authorizes the correct action with probability at least `1-delta` in both worlds requires

$$
\mathbb E_+[N_H]\ge \frac{\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta)}{2\epsilon^2/\sigma^2}.
$$

The required evidence diverges as `epsilon^{-2}` as the lifecycle contrast approaches zero. Thus the unresolved band is not a heuristic fallback: no uniform finite-cost policy can safely authorize arbitrarily near-zero contrasts. The Qualification layer must defer, retain reversibility, or expose the explicit risk/cost trade-off in this band.
