## Abstract

The decision of *which memory deserves persistent access* — `keep` vs `archive` — has no agreed formal target. We argue it is a causal quantity distinct from historical association and query-local intervention, and prove the two signals most commonly used to drive memory governance *provably insufficient* for it: observationally equivalent logs can demand opposite lifecycle actions (Theorem 1), and identical query-local effects can accompany opposite lifecycle values (Theorem 2). Formalizing **persistent-access lifecycle value** as the expected discounted net utility of holding a memory's access action fixed over a decision epoch, we prove that committing without a recovery channel incurs $\Theta(T)$ regret — the action-dependent censor blocks *non-committing* recovery (Theorem 3) — that no faithful reduction to standard bandits or offline evaluation absorbs the class (Theorem 4), and that when the identified interval for the lifecycle contrast straddles zero, **refusal or probing, not commitment, is the only way below the minimax bound** $U(-L)/(U-L)$ (Theorems 5–6, worst-case criterion explicit), with the sufficiency direction that honest certificates make interval authorization decision-consistent — a bounded rate of confident errors, refusal on the unidentified class forced by the data rather than by conservatism, and gap-consistent certification (Theorem 10). We propose **SQCAD** (Evidence–Qualification–Access), whose rule is exactly interval authorization — `keep` iff the certificate lies strictly above zero, `archive` iff strictly below, crossing intervals deferred or probed at a price — with every component mapping to a theorem. On two public long-term dialogue benchmarks under a unified cost contract, the rule operates a $\sim$45× smaller persistent store than BM25/dense/RRF (reciprocal-rank fusion; 74,092 → 1,631 tokens) at a coverage/cost trade-off (hit 0.69–0.94 with a bounded candidate guard; turn-level recall 0.09–0.22; exposure 2,316–2,722 vs 493–1,973 baseline tokens) — a trade-off, not a victory. On a new counterfactual benchmark, LifecycleBench (1,380 episodes, same-future paired rollouts), the rule coincides action-for-action with the probe-willing baseline (a theory sanity check), cuts false commits to 0.072 and regret to 0.115, and audits abstention precision and recovery directly; at the honest unit count (14 rule-world instantiations) only the censoring suppression is statistically significant, and all L3 claims are labeled accordingly. Public-trajectory (coverage/cost) and counterfactual (rule-world internal validity) claims are deliberately separated.

## 1 Introduction

Long-horizon agents store task state, user constraints, environment observations, and reusable experience in a persistent external memory that is written, retrieved, updated, and exposed across interactions (Packer et al., 2023; Park et al., 2023). Unlike a transient context window, this store accumulates: redundant descriptions, obsolete versions, and conflicting evidence enlarge the retrieval space, raise inference cost, and keep influencing behavior after the conditions that produced them have changed. The governance question is not *how to store more history* but *how to regulate the future accessibility of information already in memory* — including forgetting as a change of accessibility, priority, or representation rather than physical deletion.

Existing governance methods rely on a small set of signals. MemoryBank combines temporal and importance cues (Zhong et al., 2024); FadeMem applies differential decay from semantic relevance, access frequency, and temporal patterns (Wei et al., 2026); Oblivion treats forgetting as decay of accessibility modulated by uncertainty and response contribution (Rana et al., 2026); Memory Worth updates trust from success–failure co-occurrence and explicitly characterizes its signal as *associational* (Simsek, 2026); DeMem preserves distinctions that change downstream decisions (Zou et al., 2026); and a causal line of work evaluates memories under controlled perturbations at query time (CMI; Srivastava, 2026), audits harmful memories retroactively (MemAudit; Tan et al., 2026), builds causal-semantic memory graphs (ActMem; Zhang et al., 2026), or studies persistent causal evidence and probing in long-horizon controllers (Trivium; Chang, 2026). These systems share a common dependency: governance signals are learned from the agent's own interaction trajectory, in which exposure, adoption, task outcome, and the *future* candidate stream are jointly shaped by task conditions, retrieval policy, and the memory access action itself.

We argue that the decision these systems are actually making — whether a memory remains **persistently accessible** — is a distinct causal estimand from the ones their signals estimate. Retrieval answers "which evidence should be read now"; decay and association answer "what has correlated with success in the past"; query-local intervention answers "does this memory help the current answer?"; but `keep` vs `archive` changes which candidates can even be generated in the future, how budget competition resolves, and what evidence the system will be able to see later. We call the resulting target **persistent-access lifecycle value** and show — constructively, with verified counterexamples — that historical association and query-local intervention effects are **insufficient** for it at the information level (Theorems 1–2), that committing without a recovery channel incurs $\Theta(T)$ regret and that the action-dependent censor is what blocks *non-committing* recovery (Theorem 3), that no faithful reduction to standard bandits or offline evaluation can absorb the class (Theorem 4), and that whenever the contrast is not decision-identifiable, the worst-case-optimal behavior refuses or probes rather than commits (Theorems 5–6). This is not a claim that existing signals are useless; it is a claim that the governance layer needs an explicit **authorization** step built on identification status rather than on an ever-richer scoring function.

Guided by these results we propose **SQCAD** (Scope-aware Qualification and Controlled Access for persistent memories), an Evidence–Qualification–Access framework. The Evidence layer logs the fields the theorems say are needed (exposure propensity, position, co-exposure, adoption proxy, budget, scope/version); the Qualification layer maintains an auditable certificate interval $[L, U]$ for the lifecycle contrast and authorizes `keep` only if $L > 0$, `archive` only if $U < 0$, and otherwise defers or probes according to the value of information (VOI) — with `unresolved`/`mismatch` as explicit, non-actionable states rather than conservative defaults; the Access layer projects certified memories into a budgeted one-shot workspace, so that a *bounded candidate guard* can repair coverage bad cases without relaxing long-term authorization. Every component maps to a theorem (Derivation Map, §4.5).

We evaluate SQCAD in three layers. **L1** verifies the theory in controlled worlds: the non-identifiability counterexamples reproduce (Thm-2 within Monte Carlo (MC) noise of its closed forms); the minimax content is confirmed by an exhaustive search over all decision rules measurable in the identical log (no deterministic rule beats 1100; the randomized optimum is 660 at $p^*=0.4$, error $\ge 1/2$ at $p=1/2$); a protocol-route estimator recovers known lifecycle values (bias −0.47, CI coverage 12/12, zero confident errors); five condition violations are all caught by the gate; and the self-obscuring slope (5.85), pairing identity (11700), and probe lower bound match their closed forms. **L2** evaluates external validity on public long-term dialogue traces (LongMemEval-S, 500 samples; LoCoMo, 10 conversations / 1,986 QA) under a unified cost contract: the Theorem-6 rule and the frozen score-qualified SQCAD both operate a $\sim$45× smaller persistent store than BM25/dense/RRF (1,631–1,646 vs 74,092 tokens) at lower static coverage (the rule: hit 0.690 / turn-level recall 0.092; frozen: 0.754 / 0.115 — both far below retrieval controls), with a bounded candidate guard repairing one-shot coverage (0.902–0.944) without touching long-term authorization, and LoCoMo token-F1 within the extractive reader's ceiling (0.0344/0.0339/0.0454; keep-all ceiling 0.0475). **L3** evaluates internal counterfactual validity on LifecycleBench, a new benchmark of 1,380 episodes in which the *same* future stream is rolled out under forced `keep` and forced `archive`: the Theorem-6 rule coincides action-for-action with the probe-willing baseline (theory sanity check), its audit layer measures false-commit (0.072), abstention precision (0.656), and recovery directly, and it repairs the degenerate certificate baseline's failure (value −8.90 → +0.86) — large-magnitude but not statistically significant at the honest unit count of 14 rule-world instantiations, a fact we report in full rather than paper over. We report the four-tier reproduction status of every named baseline and do not upgrade proxies to official results.

Our contributions are: **(C1, theory)** the persistent-access lifecycle-value formalization and the insufficiency results — observational non-identifiability and query-local insufficiency (Thms 1–2), with the estimand's dependence on future candidate regeneration made structural rather than exogenous; **(C2, theory)** the decision-level consequences — the $\Theta(T)$ regret of committing without a recovery channel with the censor's separate role of blocking non-committing recovery (Thm 3), the reduction-separation result bounding faithful reductions (Thm 4), and the interval-authorization decision rule with the refusal/probe bound under an explicitly stated worst-case criterion (Thms 5–6), plus the sufficiency direction — decision consistency of interval authorization with honest certificates, forced refusal on the unidentified class, and gap-consistent certification (Thm 10); **(C3, method)** SQCAD, an Evidence–Qualification–Access framework whose core algorithm is the Theorem-6 interval rule (Algorithm 1), with VOI-priced probing, a bounded candidate guard, and lineage handling specified as priced mechanisms whose measured value is reported honestly (on the counterfactual benchmark they measure nil-to-negative); **(C4, evaluation)** a complementary protocol pairing public-trajectory external validity with same-future counterfactual internal validity under a unified cost contract, with the counterfactual benchmark's statistical units, label sensitivity, and audit chain (R1–R8) disclosed as a data card rather than claimed as an independent asset. We emphasize that public-data results are coverage/cost claims, counterfactual results are rule-world internal-validity claims, and neither is presented as deployment SOTA.

### Related work and the signal–action map

Existing governance systems estimate one of two signals but act on a third object; the table maps each system's *signal* and *action* to the *object it estimates*.

| System | Signal | Action | Object estimated |
|---|---|---|---|
| MemoryBank (Zhong et al., 2024) | temporal + importance | access decay | association |
| FadeMem (Wei et al., 2026) | relevance, frequency, recency | differential decay | association |
| Oblivion (Rana et al., 2026) | uncertainty + response contribution | accessibility decay | association |
| Memory Worth (Simsek, 2026) | success–failure co-occurrence | trust update | association (self-declared) |
| DeMem (Zou et al., 2026) | decision-relevant distinctions | retention | relevance, not causation |
| CMI (Srivastava, 2026) | query-time controlled perturbation | exposure | query-local intervention |
| MemAudit (Tan et al., 2026) | post-hoc causal attribution | removal | audit |
| ActMem (Zhang et al., 2026) | causal-semantic graph | graph maintenance | query-time causal |
| Trivium (Chang, 2026) | persistent causal evidence + probes | controller state | probe evidence |
| SimpleMem (Liu et al., 2026) | embedding retrieval | retrieval | retrieval (no governance estimand) |
| SAGE (Brahma et al., 2026) | vMF novelty density | write-side Add/Update/Noop gate | novelty, not authorization |
| GovMem (Qi et al., 2026) | coverage-dependent evidence | promote/reject/needs-review (write path) | write-time qualification |
| GateMem (Ren et al., 2026) / ReMe (Cao et al., 2026) | benchmark / procedural experience | admission / distillation | evaluation or refinement, not authorization (Appendix D) |

The column that is empty by design is *lifecycle value*: none of these systems estimates the persistent-access counterfactual $V_s^{\pi}(a)$, and the rows that look closest (CMI, ActMem, Trivium) condition on the query or the model, holding future candidate regeneration fixed. Methodologically, the target sits at the intersection of three lines: **treatment-induced selection** (Robins, 1986 — the censor of §3.4 is a self-inflicted selection on the future evidence stream, the survivor-effect structure of the healthy-worker problem restated for agent memory), **surrogate endpoints** (a query-local effect stands to lifecycle value as a surrogate stands to a clinical endpoint: local effects are decision-sufficient only under a transport condition we do not assume), and **partial identification with minimax treatment choice** (Manski, 2003; Stoye, 2012; the certificate interval is an identified set and Theorem 6 thresholds it at zero exactly as in Stoye's treatment-choice rule, §3.6). Bandits (Auer et al., 2002; Lai & Robbins, 1985) and off-policy evaluation (OPE) (Dudík et al., 2011; Jiang & Li, 2016; Uehara et al., 2022) estimate value under policies that do not censor their own evidence stream; unlearning and selective retention (TOFU, Maini et al., 2024; SSD-LLM, Yang et al., 2025) target deletion and search efficiency, not authorization; GateMem (Ren et al., 2026) benchmarks governance but proposes no policy; and textbook causal inference (Hernán & Robins, 2020; Imbens & Rubin, 2015) supplies the do-calculus and sequential-exchangeability tools our protocol route uses (§5.1). The 2024–2026 lineage of this literature is largely in the same place: governance signals are learned from the agent's own trajectory without an authorization step built on identification status — the gap C1–C2 address.

## 2 Problem Setting: Persistent-Access Lifecycle Value

### 2.1 Observable history and the decision epoch

Time is discrete, $t = 1, 2, \dots$. The system re-evaluates memory access at **epoch boundaries** $t_0, t_0+H, t_0+2H, \dots$. Within an epoch, the persistent action is **held fixed** — it is a one-shot treatment per epoch, not a per-timestep choice; within-epoch exposure $E_{i,t}$ is a consequence variable (mediator), not a treatment. At the next boundary the action may be re-assessed on the new state, so across epochs the protocol is a sequence of one-shot treatments with sequential exchangeability applied at epoch boundaries.

The observable log within scope $s$ is $O_{1:H}^{(s)} = \{(C_t, X_t, E_t, P_t, D_t, A_t^{\text{obs}}, Y_t, c_t)\}_{t=1}^{H}$, where $C_t$ is the candidate set, $X_t$ the task/user/tool/model state, $E_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$ whether memory $i$ is exposed, $P_t$ position/budget allocation, $D_{i,t}$ an adoption proxy, $A^{\text{obs}}$ observed access actions, $Y_t$ task utility, and $c_t$ token/latency/probe cost. The persistent access action of memory $i$ is $A_i^{\text{pers}} \in \{\texttt{keep}, \texttt{archive}, \texttt{restore}\}$, with effective window $[\tau_0, \tau_1]$, possible rollback, and competition under finite workspace budget $B$.

### 2.2 The target estimand and two decoys

For scope $s$, target policy $\pi$, horizon $H$, discount $\gamma \in (0,1]$, and pre-registered cost/risk weights $\lambda, \zeta \ge 0$:

$$
V_s^{\pi}(a) = \mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}\bigl(Y_t - \lambda C_t - \zeta R_t\bigr) \;\middle|\; do(A_i^{\text{pers}} = a),\; s\right],
\qquad
\tau_s^{\pi}(a_1, a_0) = V_s^{\pi}(a_1) - V_s^{\pi}(a_0),
$$

where $R_t$ is a risk/harm term (e.g., harmful adoption of stale or wrong-scope memories). The $do(\cdot)$ makes the target counterfactual: what is the net value of *holding access fixed* to $a$ while the policy regenerates future candidates, exposure, budget competition, and outcomes?

Three estimands must be kept separate — conflating them is the exact failure we formalize:

| Estimand | Treatment | Held fixed | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical association | none (observational) | historical policy | "Does this memory co-occur with success?" |
| Query-local intervention $\Delta_t(i)$ | $do(E_{i,t}=1)$ vs $0$ | query, candidate set, model, evaluator | "Does this memory help the current answer?" |
| **Persistent-access lifecycle value** $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ | $do(A_i^{\text{pers}}=a)$ | future candidates regenerated by $\pi$ | "Does changing long-term access improve future trajectories?" |

The distinction is structural: a persistent action changes the *mechanism* that generates future exposure, whereas a query-local exposure changes only the current prompt composition. The future candidate transition $P_\pi(C_{t+1} \mid \cdot, A_i^{\text{pers}}=a)$ is therefore a state variable of the estimand, not exogenous nuisance — this single fact drives all of §3.

### 2.3 Cost contract

For $t$ in an epoch, net utility is composed of task success, harmful adoption, exposure, probe, and storage terms; the concrete instance used in our benchmarks (LifecycleBench) freezes $u_t = 10\cdot\text{difficulty}_t \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{task hit}] - 20\cdot[\text{harmful adoption}] - 0.05\cdot[\text{exposed}] - 1.0\cdot[\text{probes}] - 0.01\cdot[\text{stored tokens}]$ with $\gamma = 0.9$ (§5.3, Appendix B). The public contract (§5.2) reports storage tokens (persistent store size) and exposure tokens (per-task workspace) separately so that low storage is never claimed as low total cost.

### 3.1 Theorem 1: Observational non-identifiability

**Theorem 1.** Let the historical log contain only candidate identity, exposure indicator, and scalar outcome, $O_{1:H}^{(s)} = \{(C_t, E_t, Y_t)\}_{t=1}^{H}$, with a history-dependent exposure policy, possible co-exposure of two memories, and difficulty correlated with co-exposure. Then there exist two structural causal models $\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2$ with $P_{\mathcal{M}_1}(O) = P_{\mathcal{M}_2}(O)$ but $V_{\mathcal{M}_1}^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{\mathcal{M}_1}^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}) > 0$ and $V_{\mathcal{M}_2}^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{\mathcal{M}_2}^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}) < 0$. Hence $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ is not point-identified from $(C_t, E_t, Y_t)$ alone.

**Construction.** Let $H'$ denote the length of the future rollout (the post-decision horizon over which lifecycle value accrues). Two memories $i, j$ are co-exposed perfectly: $E_{i,t} = E_{j,t} = E_t \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$, a single exposure indicator in the log, with identical exogenous streams $(T_t, d_t, \varepsilon_t)$. The structural equations are written per memory:

$$
\mathcal{M}_1:\; Y_t = \beta_1 E_{i,t} + 0 \cdot E_{j,t} - \tfrac12 d_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \beta_1 > 0 ,
$$

$$
\mathcal{M}_2:\; Y_t = \beta_{2s} E_{i,t} + \beta_{2p} E_{j,t} - \tfrac12 d_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \beta_{2s} < 0,\;\; \beta_{2s} + \beta_{2p} = \beta_1 .
$$

In $\mathcal{M}_1$, memory $i$ is genuinely useful and $j$ is a passenger; in $\mathcal{M}_2$, $i$ genuinely degrades the outcome ($\beta_{2s} < 0$) while $j$ is the useful memory, its effect $\beta_{2p} = \beta_1 - \beta_{2s} > \beta_1$ compensating so the *joint* exposure coefficient is identical. Because $E_i = E_j$ always, both worlds evaluate to the same $Y_t = \beta_1 E_t - \tfrac12 d_t + \varepsilon_t$ on the shared stream — the full observable joint distribution is equal. The lifecycle contrast separates them because the persistent action touches only $i$'s generation mechanism: under $do(\texttt{keep}_i)$ the co-exposed stream continues as above ($E_{i,t} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$); under $do(\texttt{archive}_i)$ memory $i$ is excluded from the future exposure mechanism and $E_{i,t} = 0$ — the archived state is defined by its absence from the candidate stream, the censor of §3.4 — while $j$'s generation is unaffected, so $j$'s contribution is an action-invariant offset that cancels. Hence $V(\texttt{keep}_i) - V(\texttt{archive}_i) = \beta_1 p H' > 0$ in $\mathcal{M}_1$ and $\beta_{2s} p H' < 0$ in $\mathcal{M}_2$. $\square$

**Verified instance.** $\beta_1 = 1.5$, $\beta_{2s} = -1.0$, $\beta_{2p} = 2.5$, $p = 0.55$, $H' = 2000$ gives $+1650$ vs $-1100$; the observational max field difference is 0. A Memory-Worth-style association rule assigns score $> 0.99$ in both worlds and incurs regret $1100$ in $\mathcal{M}_2$.

**What this does NOT claim.** Logs are not useless — they are insufficient *without additional structure* (richer fields, exchangeability, or micro-intervention). There exist scenarios where associational methods necessarily mislead; this is a sufficient counterexample to point identification, not a claim that they always fail.

### 3.2 Theorem 2: Query-local intervention insufficiency

**Theorem 2.** Let $\Delta_t(i) = \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid do(E_{i,t}=1)] - \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid do(E_{i,t}=0)]$ be the query-local causal effect with query, candidate set, model, and evaluator fixed. Assume persistent actions alter future candidate generation, memories compete for budget $B < |\mathcal{M}|$, and memories can serve as retrieval bridges for rare tasks outside the source distribution. Then there exist memories $i, j$ with $\Delta_t(i) = \Delta_t(j)$ but $V^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_i) - V^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_i) < 0$ and $V^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_j) - V^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_j) > 0$.

**Construction.** In the source period both memories are simulated under $do(E=1)$ and $do(E=0)$ on identical queries, yielding the *same* local effect $\alpha > 0$. In the future rollout, memory $i$ provides zero marginal benefit but crowds out a rare critical memory $k$: each exposure crowds $k$ with probability $\rho_c$ and crowding is permanent, so $k$ remains accessible for an expected $\frac{1-(1-\rho_c p)^{H'}}{\rho_c p} \approx 8$ steps — the first crowding event occurs at expected time $1/(\rho_c p)$ — leaving $H' - \frac{1-(1-\rho_c p)^{H'}}{\rho_c p} \approx 1992$ crowded-out rare-task steps, and a rare task succeeds with probability $0.7$ when its critical memory is accessible: $V(\texttt{keep}_i) - V(\texttt{archive}_i) = -0.7\, \beta_k q_{\text{rare}} \bigl[H' - \frac{1-(1-\rho_c p)^{H'}}{\rho_c p}\bigr] < 0$. Memory $j$ is the retrieval bridge for those rare tasks: with $j$ kept, rare tasks reach $k$ with probability $0.7$; with $j$ archived, no retrieval channel exists and the success probability is $0$, so $(r_{\text{high}} - r_{\text{low}}) = 0.7$: $V(\texttt{keep}_j) - V(\texttt{archive}_j) = 0.7\, \beta_k q_{\text{rare}} H' > 0$. $\square$

**Verified instance.** $\alpha = 2.0$, $\beta_k = 4.0$, $\rho_c = 0.25$, $p = 0.5$, $q_{\text{rare}} \approx 1/3$, $H' = 2000$: the closed forms give $-0.7 \cdot 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot (2000 - 8) = -1859$ and $+0.7 \cdot 4 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot 2000 = +1867$; the seeded counterfactual rollouts (500 source queries, 2000 future steps) yield $−1784$ (short-term useful) vs $+1776$ (long-term bridge), matching within Monte-Carlo noise, with *identical* local effects of $2.0$; a CMI-style rule ($\Delta > 0 \Rightarrow$ keep) incurs regret $\approx 1784$.

### 3.3 Corollary 1: No automatic scope transport

**Corollary 1.** If the mechanism linking treatment to outcome may differ across scopes, $V_{s^*}^{\pi}(a \mid D_s) \neq V_s^{\pi}(a)$ in general even when $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ is identified from source-scope data. Constructively: two worlds with identical source-scope data ($\tau(s_1) = 0.8$, $\tau(s_2) = -1.6$, weighted average $-0.28$) but opposite target-scope values ($\tau(s^*) = \pm 2.0$) cannot be distinguished from source data.

### 3.4 Theorem 3: Committing without recovery, and the censor's separate role

Theorems 1–2 concern *what can be learned from a log*. Theorem 3 concerns *the cost of acting*: a persistent action changes the future evidence stream, so an early wrong commit can make its own correction impossible.

**Setup.** Two coupled worlds K (keep is correct) and A (archive is correct). Both worlds share a bit-identical observational stream of length $n_{\text{early}}$ — the pre-decision window (Lemma 1); for $t \le n_{\text{early}}$ the two are observationally indistinguishable. After the decision point, exposure is action-dependent: $P(e_t = 1 \mid s_t = \texttt{kept}) = p = 0.6$, $P(e_t = 1 \mid s_t = \texttt{archived}) = p_{\text{arch}}$. With $p_{\text{arch}} = 0$ (persistent archive censors the candidate stream), an archived state generates no new observations — the information set is frozen (Lemma 2, "silent freeze"). We write $\nu := \tau_{\text{step}} \cdot p = 10 \times 0.6 = 6.0$ for the per-step wrong-state loss rate, where $\tau_{\text{step}} = 10$ is the *per-exposed-step* lifecycle value of the verified instance (K world $+10$ per exposed step, A world $-10$; 13- §2). Note the scale: $\tau_{\text{step}}$ is a per-step rate, distinct from the horizon-total contrast $\tau$ of §3.1/§3.6 (there $\pm 1650/\pm 1100$ over $H'$ steps); the instance's horizon-total contrast is $\nu (T - n_{\text{early}}) = 11{,}700$.

**Theorem 3(a) — $\Omega(T)$ lower bound for committing policies.** Any committing policy (no probe/restore channel) that decides from the shared early stream must make the *same* decision in both worlds; if the decision is wrong, the loss is $\nu$ per step and is never revised — *because the policy is committing*, not because of the censor (this part of the bound holds for any pair of observationally equivalent worlds, censored or not):

$$
R_T(\pi) = \nu\, (T - n_{\text{early}}) = \Theta(T),
\qquad
\text{slope} = \frac{\nu (T - n_{\text{early}})}{T} = \frac{6.0 \times 1950}{2000} = 5.85 .
$$

This holds for **calibrated gates** too: a gate that correctly resolves the identified set to the wrong sign (because the early stream is confounded) commits with full calibration and still fails. Numerically, six distinct committing strategies (default-keep watchful, association, local-causal, gate variants) all reproduce slope $5.8500$ exactly over 12 seeds (closed-form arithmetic; see §5.1).

**Theorem 3(b) — the censor blocks *non-committing* recovery: $O(1/q)$ plateau with restore.** A restore-capable policy (probability $q$ per step of probing the archive; each probe succeeds with probability $\rho = \Phi((\tau_{\text{step}}-\theta)/\sigma)$ — a threshold crossing in probe noise, with $\theta$ the detection threshold and $\sigma$ the probe-noise scale, that is *independent of* $q$ — and a successful probe triggers permanent restore at cost $c_{\text{restore}}$) satisfies

$$
\mathbb{E}[R_T] \le \frac{\nu}{q\rho} + \frac{c_{\text{probe}}}{\rho} + c_{\text{restore}} = O(1/q),
$$

independent of $T$ — expected probes to success are $1/\rho$ (each probe succeeds w.p. $\rho$), so the probe-expenditure term is $q$-invariant. Numerically $q = 0.01/0.05/0.2$ gives slopes $2.454 / 0.425 / 0.110$. Inverting the model on the lifecycle-loss term alone (the simulator scores lifecycle loss only, excluding probe/restore expenditure — §5.2), the implied per-probe success probabilities $\hat\rho = \nu/(q \cdot \text{slope} \cdot T) = 0.122 / 0.141 / 0.136$ are stable across $q$, broadly consistent with the $q$-independence the model $\rho = \Phi((\tau_{\text{step}}-\theta)/\sigma)$ requires (the endpoints scatter $\pm 14\%$ around the middle estimate — the tolerance of an inversion that ignores the constant $c_{\text{probe}}/\rho + c_{\text{restore}}$ terms) — and the expected time to a successful restore $1/(q\hat\rho) = 818/142/37$ steps reproduces the slopes to within $1\%$ ($6\cdot 818/2000 = 2.454$, $6\cdot 142/2000 = 0.426$, $6\cdot 37/2000 = 0.111$). **The plateau is not free**: smaller $q$ prolongs the wrong-state loss ($\nu/(q\rho)$), not probe expenditure; absent probe interference or budget constraints the model's optimum is $q \to 1$ — our Access layer prices $q < 1$ exactly when probes interfere with task flow (the pricing rule of §3.7).

**Theorem 3(c) — the censor is the mechanism.** If candidate support is independent of the action ($p_{\text{arch}} = p$), the watchful policy self-corrects in $O(1)$ steps: slopes drop to $0.0440$ (W0) / $0.0663$ (W1). The action→evidence feedback turns "would-have-corrected" into "cannot-correct"; this is the ablation criterion that isolates the mechanism.

### 3.5 Theorem 4: A faithful reduction cannot absorb the evidence-flow state

A standard objection: "any problem can be encoded into a richer state and solved by a contextual bandit." Theorem 4 closes this loophole for *faithful* reductions — and the honesty of the framing matters: the "richer state" escape works only if the reduction is allowed to add evidence-availability state itself, which is exactly the state standard frameworks do not carry.

**Definition (faithful feedback-preserving reduction).** A reduction $R = (R_W, R_\pi, \phi)$ from a problem class $\mathcal{C}$ to $\mathcal{C}'$ preserves action sets, maps target policies back to source policies, and maps observations via $\phi$ that is **world-independent** (a function of observed histories only, never of latent world identity), preserves feedback (image observations under $\pi'$ equal $\phi$ of source observations under the pulled-back policy), and preserves regret up to $o(T)$. The $o(T)$ slack is deliberate and generous: even a reduction that may perturb per-policy regret sublinearly is still faithful, and Theorem 4 holds for all such reductions — so the obstruction is the world-independent observation map, never the regret-tracking clause.

**Lemma 3 (reduction invariance of observational equivalence).** Under a faithful reduction, the coupled K/A pair maps to image processes that are point-wise identical: any deterministic target policy makes the same action sequence in both images.

**Lemma 4 (pointwise regret identity).** For any strategy (any adaptivity, any randomization), the regret identity $\text{Regret}_K(\pi) + \text{Regret}_A(\pi) = \nu (T - n_{\text{early}}) = 11700.0$ holds deterministically — Lemma 3 makes the action sequence identical in both worlds, so the sum of the two world regrets is a fixed constant, not a probabilistic event ("almost surely" is unnecessary).

**Theorem 4 (separation).** No faithful feedback-preserving reduction from the self-obscuring class to a standard class (contextual bandit, log-based OPE) admits a sublinear-regret strategy on all images:

$$
\max_{W \in \{K, A\}} \text{Regret}_{R_W(W)}(\pi') \;\ge\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\nu\,(T - n_{\text{early}}) - o(T) = 5850 - o(T) = \Theta(T).
$$

Where, then, does the "richer state" escape live? It is a *definition change*, in one of two places. A faithful reduction preserves the observation map, so an enriched state would have to be a function of observed histories — but the missing state (whether the archive has censored the candidate stream) is latent world identity, not a function of what the policy can observe. And standard bandit/OPE frameworks are unfaithful in a different, equally load-bearing place: they assume the per-step loss is observable at decision time, which is precisely the assumption the censor violates (Theorem 3).

**Conjecture 1 (new state is necessary).** Theorem 4 closes every *faithful* escape; we conjecture the only ways below $\Theta(T)$ are (i) leaving the problem class by adding a restore/lineage channel, which achieves the $O(1/q)$ plateau (Corollary 2), or (ii) an *unfaithful* reduction that smuggles evidence-availability into the state — exactly the state SQCAD's Access/Qualification layers maintain. Formally: every sublinear strategy on the censored class either probes (i) or carries availability state (ii); richer scores, better estimation, and longer logs alone do not suffice. What is proved is sufficiency at the right complexity order (Corollary 2); the open direction is the impossibility direction of (i)/(ii).

**Corollary 2 (sufficiency, Theorem 3(b)).** Adding a restore channel achieves $\mathbb{E}[R_T] \le \frac{\nu}{q\rho} + \frac{c_{\text{probe}}}{\rho} + c_{\text{restore}}$, i.e., the necessary state is also sufficient at the correct complexity order.

**Numeric corroboration.** The *same* standard learners succeed when candidate support is action-independent and fail linearly when it is not: static bandit UCB (upper confidence bound) 0.0013 / contextual bandit 0.0030 / standard OPE 0.075 (W0/W1, decidable) vs standard OPE 5.85 / candidate-exploring UCB 5.85 (W2, censored). The failure slope equals Theorem 3(a)'s formula exactly.

### 3.6 Theorems 5–6: Decision identification and the necessity of refusal (worst-case criterion)

**Theorem 5 (committing rules fail on the unidentified class).** On a Theorem-1 sign-flip pair with magnitudes $\{|\tau_1|, |\tau_2|\} = \{1100, 1650\}$ (labeled convention fixed below to match the exhaustive-grid verification, §5.1), every committing rule (any randomization over `keep`/`archive` measurable in the log) has worst-case expected regret

$$
\ge \frac{|\tau_1|\,|\tau_2|}{|\tau_1| + |\tau_2|} = 660,
$$

attained at $p^* = |\tau_1|/(|\tau_1|+|\tau_2|) = 0.4$, where $p^*$ is the (world-independent — the log is bit-identical across worlds, so the randomization cannot condition on world identity) probability of committing `keep`; the transposed sign assignment of §3.1's verified instance swaps which magnitude is keep-correct, but the minimax value and the error bound are labeling-invariant, and the $p^* = 0.4$ reported here is the one the exhaustive grid in §5.1 verifies. The classification-error minimax is separate: error $\ge 1/2$, attained at $p = 1/2$ — the regret and error minimizers differ, and conflating them is a genuine bug, not a stylistic choice. **Refusal (`defer`/`unresolved`) or fresh interventional evidence is the only way below the bound** — gating is a theorem consequence, not a conservative preference — and the refusal trigger must be auditable: its mis-refusal rate (refusing when commitment would have been right) is a measured audit quantity (abstention precision, §5.3), not a quantity the theory bounds.

**Worst-case stance (why minimax).** "Refusal is necessary" is a statement about the minimax (no-prior) criterion; a Bayes rule with a prior over worlds may legitimately commit on unidentifiable evidence. We adopt the worst-case criterion because (i) the target population is agents whose memory state is *actively censored* by their own earlier decisions (Theorem 3), so the unidentifiable mass is not a measure-zero corner of the problem; (ii) a wrongful persistent commit is irreversible at $\Theta(T)$ cost *without a recovery channel* — and the censor is exactly what blocks the non-committing recovery that would undo it (Theorem 3); and (iii) a committing Bayes rule requires a prior over latent world identity that a deployer cannot audit. Under this criterion the gating conclusion is forced; without it, it is a preference, and we do not claim otherwise.

**The `defer` action (formal).** `defer` completes the action space $\{\texttt{keep}, \texttt{archive}, \texttt{defer}\}$: it makes no persistent change, leaves the candidate writable at the next epoch boundary, and carries an explicit price $C_{\text{defer}}$ — the opportunity value foregone by not committing now. Its *committing-action* regret on the unidentified class is 0 (it never persists a wrong action), but it is not free: the policy pays $C_{\text{defer}}$, which is what makes the Theorem 6 rule a cost comparison (the decision rule below) rather than a heuristic.

**Theorem 6 (decision identification suffices; point identification is unnecessary).** Given a log $o$ and the class of worlds $\mathcal{M}(o)$ compatible with it, the identified interval for $\tau$ is $[L(o), U(o)] = [\inf_M \tau_M(o), \sup_M \tau_M(o)]$. For binary persistent decisions: (a) if $L > 0$, committing `keep` has worst-case regret 0 — **authorize keep**; (b) if $U < 0$, **authorize archive**; (c) if $L \le 0 \le U$ (a crossing interval), any committing rule has worst-case regret $\ge R^*(L, U) = U(-L)/(U-L)$ at $p^* = U/(U-L)$ (attained by the minimax rule), and the correct decision is **defer or probe**, priced by

$$
\min\bigl\{\, R^*(L, U),\; C_{\text{defer}},\; C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L', U') \,\bigr\},
$$

where $[L', U']$ is the post-probe interval; the probe is worth paying iff its expected improvement $C_{\text{defer}} - \bigl(C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L', U')\bigr)$ is positive. Verified instance $(L, U) = (-1650, 1100)$: commit-keep worst-case regret $-L = 1650$ (keep loses in the $\tau = -1650$ world), commit-archive worst-case regret $U = 1100$ (archive loses in the $\tau = +1100$ world), randomized minimax $R^* = U(-L)/(U-L) = 660$ at $p^* = U/(U-L) = 0.4$; probing beats committing whenever $C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L', U') < \min\{660,\, C_{\text{defer}}\}$.

**Lineage (partial identification and treatment choice).** The certificate interval is an identified set in the sense of Manski (2003), and the decision rule is minimax-regret treatment choice over that set in the sense of Stoye (2012): for a bounded identified set, the minimax-regret decision rule thresholds the interval at zero exactly as Theorem 6 does, and the boundary decision is deliberately sensitive to the identified set rather than to a point estimate. The contribution relative to that literature is the *object* identified — a counterfactual over a future that the agent's own action censors (Theorem 3) — and the derived consequences: the sign of the interval, not its width, drives the authorized action, and the defer/probe prices in the decision rule above follow from the theory rather than being tuned.

### 3.7 Theorems 7–8: Probe complexity and the price of recovery

**Theorem 7 (detection lower bound).** In the archived-committed class (archive censors the candidate stream; evidence arrives only through probing), distinguishing the two worlds $y \sim \mathcal{N}(\pm\mu, \sigma^2)$ at error rate $\le \delta$ requires

$$
N_{\text{probe}} \ge \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\text{KL}} = \frac{\sigma^2 \log(1/\delta)}{2\mu^2} = \Omega\!\left(\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\Delta^2}\right),
$$

by the Le Cam / Bretagnolle–Huber two-point bound; the Gaussian mean $\mu$ is the probe-signal parameter (distinct from the per-step loss rate $\nu = \tau_{\text{step}} p$ of §3.4, which drives the loss rate). Two distinct numeric bounds instantiate this theory, and they must not be conflated. (1) **Detection sample complexity (Theorem 7):** $N_{\text{probe}} \ge \sigma^2 \log(1/\delta)/(2\mu^2)$ counts *successful probe observations*; with the CI-exclusion stopping rule ($z = 1.96 \leftrightarrow \delta = 0.05$), the simulator's expected probe count exceeds the two-point bound by a factor $2.2$–$3.4$ in the binding regime ($N^* \ge 1.5$), a fixed-$z$ conservative constant ($\approx 2.3$ for the $z = 1.96$ threshold and sequential overshoot) that is independent of the attempt rate $q$ (14- §4). (2) **Regret cost (Theorem 8 / Corollary 3):** the cost-level instance of the SQCAD bound is $\hat{R}_T = \nu/(q\rho) + c_{\text{probe}}/\rho + c_{\text{restore}} = (6+30)/0.05 + 80 = 800$ — pricing probe attempts at the full amortized rate $1/q$, a conservative upper bound on the $q$-invariant expenditure $c_{\text{probe}}/\rho$ of Theorem 3(b) — and the observed watchful-restore regret is $850.0$ over 12 seeds (frozen artifact, `self_obscuring_ablation.json`: mean correction time 141.67 steps at $\nu = 6$, so $6 \times 141.67 = 850.0$; the simulator scores lifecycle loss only, so this regret excludes probe/restore expenditure), a 6.3% excess within the discretization constants; over the $\tau$ grid the CI-exclusion rule's $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret}]$ likewise beats the Theorem-8 lower bound $L = (N^*/\rho)(\nu/q + c_{\text{probe}})$ by the same constant factor $2.2$–$3.4$ in the binding regime — the rule is tight to a constant (14- §5). The $800$ is a *cost* bound from Theorem 8, not a probe-count lower bound; the probe-count bound is (1).

**Theorem 8 (regret decomposition with censoring).** Any strategy in this class has expected K-world regret at least

$$
\mathbb{E}[\text{Regret}_K] \ge \frac{N^*(\delta)}{\rho}\left(\frac{\nu}{q} + c_{\text{probe}}\right),
$$

decomposed into lifecycle loss while wrong ($\nu \cdot N^*/(q\rho)$), probe expenditure ($c_{\text{probe}} \cdot N^*/\rho$ — the expected number of probe *attempts* needed to accumulate $N^*$ successful probes is $N^*/\rho$, independent of $q$, matching Theorem 3(b)), and recovery latency. **Corollary 3 (SQCAD instantiation).** The SQCAD-style restore channel instantiates Corollary 2's bound with the policy named — $R_T^{\text{SQCAD}} \le \nu/(q\rho) + c_{\text{probe}}/\rho + c_{\text{restore}}$ — so the recovery channel is the *statistically correct complexity* of the problem, not an engineering add-on (the two corollaries state the same bound; Corollary 3 names the SQCAD policy). (Restore is more efficient than per-probe evidence acquisition because one restore yields a continuous evidence stream at rate $p$ instead of $q\rho$.)

### 3.8 Theorem 10: Sufficiency — decision-consistent interval authorization

Theorems 1–2 establish the *necessity* direction at the information level: the two signals the field uses do not suffice. Here we state the *sufficiency* direction at the decision level: with honest certificates, the interval rule of Theorem 6 is exactly as reliable as its certificates claim — no more, and no less.

**Theorem 10 (sufficiency: honest certificates make interval authorization decision-consistent).** Let the identification family C1–C8 (Theorem 9) hold, and let $[\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$ be any certificate interval with honest coverage $1 - \alpha$ — the protocol-route estimator of §5.1 (coverage 12/12 at nominal 95%) or the paired-bootstrap CIs of §5.2. Under the Theorem-6 interval rule (`keep` iff $\hat{L} > 0$, `archive` iff $\hat{U} < 0$, else defer or probe at the Theorem-6 prices):

(i) **No confident errors (bounded rate).** $\mathbb{P}(\text{a confident authorization is wrong}) \le \alpha + o(1)$, uniformly over the compatible class. The gate cannot confidently authorize a wrong action more often than its certificate misses the target.

(ii) **Forced refusal on the unidentified class.** On any Theorem-1 sign-flip pair, the two worlds produce bit-identical logs (Lemma 1), hence identical certificates, so $\mathbb{P}\bigl(0 \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]\bigr) \ge 1 - 2\alpha$: the gate refuses to authorize — defers or probes (Theorem 6(c)) — with probability $\ge 1 - 2\alpha$. Refusal is *forced by the data*, not by conservatism: any committing rule measurable in the identical log takes the *same* action in both worlds, which is wrong in exactly one of them, so a confident authorization on the pair is wrong with probability $\ge 1/2$ no matter how rich the estimator — only refusal avoids the guaranteed error, and no estimator can separate the pair. Theorem 5's bound is the price at which this forced refusal is exercised, and §5.1's exhaustive search confirms that no committing rule avoids it.

(iii) **Gap consistency.** If the contrast is well separated from zero, certification becomes asymptotically certain: if $|\tau| > \delta_T$ with $\delta_T \to 0$ and $\delta_T / w_T \to \infty$ ($w_T = \hat{U} - \hat{L}$ the certificate width), and $\alpha_T \to 0$, then $\mathbb{P}(\text{confident, correct authorization}) \to 1$.

The proof is a direct consequence of honesty (Appendix A, Theorem 10); the guarantees are certified at exactly the level the certificates claim — decision consistency is inherited from certificate coverage, not assumed. The protocol route verifies (i)–(ii) in one pass (§5.1): coverage 12/12, zero confident decision errors, `unresolved` on exactly the 3 sign-ambiguous (neutral) memories; and on the Theorem-1 pair itself the estimator is deterministic in the identical log, so Lemma 1 + Theorem 10(ii) give the gate a defer probability $\ge 1 - 2\alpha$ — while every committing rule measurable in that log pays $\ge 660$ (§5.1).

### 3.9 Claim boundaries

- We prove *insufficiency* of association and query-local effects for the lifecycle estimand; we do **not** prove all existing systems fail everywhere, nor that C1–C8 (Theorem 9, Appendix A) is the minimal identification route (Lemma A–D in the appendix give alternative routes: protocol independence, bundle retreat, IV, current-value identification).
- The verified constants (660, 5850, 5.85, 11700) are closed-form instances of theorem parameters, not transferable system-performance claims; the minimax instance (R* = 660, p* = 0.4, error 1/2 at p = 1/2) is additionally verified by exhaustive search over all decision rules measurable in the identical log (§5.1).
- Theorem 9's observational route (sequential g-formula / doubly-robust (DR)) is stated with the sufficiency family C1–C8 but its full observational implementation remains future work; what is verified is the protocol route and the gate behavior under condition violations (§5.1).
- Theorem 10's guarantees are conditional on *honest* certificates (Theorem 9's conditions hold, coverage at the claimed level): if a certificate is miscalibrated — its true coverage below $1 - \alpha$ — the no-confident-error bound (i) degrades at the same rate, and we make no claim of robustness to dishonest certificates.

## 4 Method: SQCAD

SQCAD is the operational answer to §3: a three-layer closed loop that turns identification status into auditable authorization. The layers are **Evidence** (what must be logged), **Qualification** (when a persistent action is authorized), and **Access** (how authorized memories are projected into the budgeted workspace).

### 4.1 Closed-loop workflow

```
Evidence (immutable log: C_t, E_t, P_t, D_t, Y_t, A_t, v, s, budget)
  -> Proposer (frozen retriever: BM25 / dense / RRF) -> candidates + scope + version
  -> Qualification: certificate interval [L, U], diagnostics, expiry
       L > 0  -> authorize keep / restore
       U < 0  -> authorize archive
       L <= 0 <= U -> defer, or VOI-priced probe (probe result feeds back)
       C3/C8 failure -> unresolved / mismatch (persistent state is untouchable)
  -> Access: budgeted projection (qualification projection -> ranking projection top-B)
  -> one-shot workspace (exposure pool) -> exposure/adoption/outcome/cost logged back to Evidence
```

The Proposer decides *who is read first among already-authorized objects*; it can never decide who stays persistently. Persistent state changes flow **only** through the qualification-authorized path. This is the structural answer to Theorem 4: retrieval power and authorization power are separated.

### 4.2 Evidence layer

Theorems 1–2 say which log fields are load-bearing: without exposure propensity, position, co-exposure, budget, adoption proxy, and scope/version tags, the lifecycle contrast is not identified. The Evidence layer therefore records $(C_t, E_t, P_t, D_t, A_t, Y_t, s, v, B_t)$ with action windows $[\tau_0, \tau_1]$ and rollbacks (C1–C8 in Appendix A make each field's role precise: C5 gives exposure/position/budget kernels, C6 the adoption kernel with error model, C8 the version strata).

### 4.3 Qualification layer: certificates, not scores

For each candidate memory and epoch, Qualification computes the **identified interval** $[L, U]$ for $\tau_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}, \texttt{archive} \mid S_{t_0})$ and issues a certificate with four output states (Theorem 6's output space):

| Certificate | Condition | Persistent action |
|---|---|---|
| `point` / `bound` with $L > 0$ | interval strictly above zero | **authorize keep/restore** |
| `point` / `bound` with $U < 0$ | interval strictly below zero | **authorize archive** |
| `unresolved` | positivity (C3) or stability (C8) fails — no interval authorized | **no persistent change; defer** |
| `mismatch` | scope/version/evidence-source invalid (C8) | **re-qualification required** |

**Algorithm 1 — Qualification (interval-authorization rule).** Input: candidate memory $m$, log $O$ up to the epoch boundary, costs $(C_{\text{defer}}, C_{\text{probe}}, C_{\text{restore}})$, probe budget.
1. Estimate the identified interval $[L, U]$ for $\tau_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}, \texttt{archive})$ from the pre-decision window (estimator provenance per layer, §4.5: L1 protocol-route rollout estimator over controlled worlds; L2 public proxy certificate over observable update/version events; L3 event-based reference certificate over rule-world events). The honest interval estimator is implemented and verified only where ground truth is known — the L1 protocol route (bias $-0.47$, coverage 12/12, §5.1); the L2/L3 certificates are explicitly **proxy instantiations** without an estimated $[L,U]$ (event-based: update/version evidence for L2, rule-world events for L3), because honest intervals require same-future counterfactual rollouts that public traces cannot supply. The L3 event-based certificate is audited *as* the proxy it is — §5.3 diagnoses it as the degenerate default-keep behavior the interval rule fixes — and the v2 row instantiates the interval rule's *decision structure* (authorize iff the certificate leaves zero) over the event proxy.
2. If $L > 0$: certificate `positive`, **authorize keep/restore**. If $U < 0$: certificate `negative`, **authorize archive**.
3. If $L \le 0 \le U$: certificate `unresolved` (or `mismatch` on C8/scope failure) — no persistent change is authorized; choose $\min\{R^*(L,U),\, C_{\text{defer}},\, C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L',U')\}$: defer, or probe in VOI-descending order (Theorem 6(c)).
4. Log certificate, interval, and decision evidence to the Evidence layer — the audit record Theorem 5 requires.

`unresolved` is not "conservative preference": by Theorem 5 it is the only behavior below the 660 worst-case regret bound on the unidentified class, and by Theorem 6 the decision rule is: authorize iff the interval does not straddle zero; when it does, choose $\min\{R^*(L,U), C_{\text{defer}}, C_{\text{probe}} + R^*_{\text{after}}\}$.

**VOI probe.** A candidate with a crossing interval is probed only if its pre-registered expected post-probe interval $[L', U']$ and cost satisfy $\text{VOI} = C_{\text{defer}} - (C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L', U')) > 0$, in VOI-descending order. Probe products are one-shot exposures; they enter the persistent store only if a refreshed certificate authorizes them (Theorem 6(c) auditable-refusal counterpart: keep the evidence of why).

### 4.4 Access layer: budgeted projection with a bounded guard

- **Qualification projection → ranking projection**: certified candidates are ranked into the workspace up to budget $B$; the ranking (Proposer) is frozen and exchangeable (BM25/dense/RRF) so that quality differences across policies isolate the *governance* decision, not the retriever.
- **Candidate guard**: at query time, up to $g \in \{1, 2, 4\}$ additional BM25 candidates enter the *one-shot* workspace without qualification. This repairs coverage bad cases (L2, §5.2) without relaxing long-term authorization — the guard changes "what can be seen now", never "who stays forever": its extra candidates are drawn from the already-persistent pool's one-shot projection, so persistent storage changes only up to eviction-order rounding. This is the mechanism-level explanation of why Guard improves Hit/Recall while storage stays essentially unchanged.
- **Probe/restore**: archived memories can be probed (cost $c_{\text{probe}}$) at most once per task; a probed memory is restored only if it wins a workspace slot under competition (Theorem 3(b)'s threshold semantics) — restore is the audited recovery channel whose complexity order is matched by Theorem 8.
- **Lineage/version gate**: conflicting versions of the same entity+slot+scope are tracked; an unresolved lineage conflict forces re-qualification rather than silent retention (L3 evidence, §5.3).

### 4.5 Derivation map: every mechanism is a theorem consequence

| Theorem | Identified deficiency | Framework requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Thm 1 | $(C, E, Y)$ logs insufficient | Evidence: exposure propensity, position, co-exposure, adoption, task context |
| Thm 2 | query-local do-effect insufficient | Access: persistent-treatment semantics (window, rollback); Evidence: policy feedback + next candidate stream |
| Thm 2 (estimand separation) | one-shot exposure ≠ persistent authorization | Access: candidate guard changes exposure only, never retention (one-shot workspace) |
| Thm 9 (C1/C4–C6) | consistency/observability | Evidence separates $E, D, A, Y$; Qualification checks measurement quality |
| Thm 9 (C2/C8) | exchangeability/stability | Evidence: full covariate history + scope/version tags; Qualification: stability gate |
| Thm 9 (C3) | positivity may fail | Qualification gates randomized micro-intervention to safe candidates |
| Thm 9 (C7) | interference | Access: workspace budget + explicit co-memory composition |
| Cor 1 | source ≠ target | Qualification audits target-scope support before transport |
| Thm 5 | committing rules fail on unidentified class | Qualification: `unresolved` is necessary; refusal trigger auditable |
| Thm 6 | point identification unnecessary | Qualification: interval-crossing rule; $\min\{R^*, C_{\text{defer}}, C_{\text{probe}} + R^*_{\text{after}}\}$ |

| Thm 3 / Thm 7–8 | self-obscuring; probe is not free | Access: restore channel with threshold semantics; probe/restore priced in the cost contract |

**Relationship to adjacent lines.** SQCAD is not a ranking function (the Proposer is frozen and exchangeable), not a write-time gate (access-time authorization with windows), not a post-hoc audit (decision-time), and not a benchmark (LifecycleBench is a companion asset, §5.3).

Short-term memories (local effect $+0.2$, lifecycle $\approx -5.2$) are correctly archived — the Theorem 2 lesson holds inside Stage 1. **Minimax verification (Theorem 5, exhaustive).** The 660 bound is verified as a *decision problem*, not just arithmetic: an exhaustive search over all rules measurable in the identical log $O$ enumerates exactly two deterministic rules (both constant actions, since $O$ is identical across worlds; best worst-case regret 1100, attained by always-archive under the §3.6 label convention — keep-always pays $-L = 1650$), and a randomized grid (1,001 points) attains $660.0000$ at $p = 0.4$, matching the closed forms $R^* = |\tau_1||\tau_2|/(|\tau_1|+|\tau_2|) = 660$ and $p^* = 0.4$; the classification-error minimax is $1/2$ attained at $p = 1/2$, distinct from the regret minimizer (the round-1 draft conflated the two; the search is the check that keeps the corrected statement honest). The same construction's observational-max-field gap (0), association-rule regret (1100), and gate outputs are closed-form re-checks of the §3 constructions (labeling note: the association rule's 1100 is its regret in the §3.1 transposed instance's negative world $\tau = -1100$; under the §3.6 label convention fixed for the grid, the same constant-keep rule pays $-L = 1650$ — identical construction, relabeled worlds).

**Theorems 3/4/7–8 numerics.** No-recovery committing policies reproduce slope $5.8500$ exactly (12 seeds, six distinct strategies); restore policies plateau at $0.425$ (q=0.05) and $0.110$ (q=0.2) with implied per-probe success probabilities $\hat\rho = 0.141/0.136$ stable across $q$ (§3.4); removing the action→evidence feedback (W0/W1) drops the slope to $0.044/0.066$; the paired regret identity holds pointwise at $11700.0$ with max-regret bound $5850.0$; standard OPE/UCB learners succeed on W0/W1 — OPE 0.075, W0 static UCB 0.0013, W0 contextual bandit 0.0030, W1 candidate-UCB 0.0037 (the 0.0030 and 0.0037 belong to different rows, §5.1) — and fail linearly on W2 (5.85); the watchful-restore cost bound is $800$ (Theorem 8 instance) and the observed regret is $850.0$ over 12 seeds, a 6.3% excess within the discretization constants (§3.7).

**Reading the results honestly.** (i) The primary SQCAD bad case is one-shot candidate coverage, and a bounded candidate guard repairs it without touching long-term authorization (storage unchanged across guards up to eviction-order rounding — by construction, the guard is a one-shot exposure pool). (v) Public data cannot measure `keep`/`archive` lifecycle causality — there is no same-future counterfactual branch; that is L3's job.

### 5.3 L3: LifecycleBench — counterfactual lifecycle governance

**Why a self-built benchmark (and how we prevent it from being self-serving).** Public traces do not contain same-future `keep`/`archive` branches, so the object of the paper — the lifecycle value of a persistent action — cannot be measured on them. LifecycleBench generates 1,380 episodes over six mechanism families (rare-bridge, self-obscuring, hitchhiker, version-update, harmful-stale, scope-mismatch) plus stable/neutral controls; for each episode the *identical* future stream (10 tasks, fixed events, shared random seed per episode) is rolled out twice, once with the decision memory forced to `keep` and once to `archive`; the discounted value difference $\tau = V_{\text{keep}} - V_{\text{archive}}$ defines the hidden oracle label (neutral iff $|\tau| \le 0.5$). The benchmark is audited by an R1–R8 chain: metadata shortcut ceiling (R1), label sensitivity with 31 perturbed runs and a declared validity domain (R2), unseen-mechanism generalization (R3, 3 boundary cells reported), pre-registered winning-region hits (R4), an independent reference implementation (R5, 1,380/1,380 bit-identical), external human anchoring (R6, 28 blinded cases exported; judge batch pending), a public/hidden/release package with an anonymous scoring tool (R7), and an iteration log (R8). Public traces carry no hidden gold; the evaluator is the only component that reads hidden labels; baseline policies include non-SQCAD strategies (keep-all/archive-all/recency/frequency/storage rules) so the benchmark is not SQCAD-shaped by construction.

**Two findings with teeth.** First, the original SQCAD certificate decision ("negative/mismatch → archive, else keep") is *degenerate* on this benchmark: its `negative` signal fires only on visible correction events, so it coincides with the event-rule baseline (0 divergence) and its other gates never fire (four of five ablations are bit-identical). This is not evidence against the framework — it is evidence that a scoring/event-based qualification without the Theorem-6 interval discipline leaves `unresolved` un-actioned, which is exactly the failure §3.6 predicts: un-actioned crossing intervals mean default-keep, and default-keep is the 660-bound behavior on the unidentified class. Second, the mechanism-level diagnoses are quantitatively confirmed and are subsumed by the Theorem-6 rule below: `lineage-conflict → archive` improves mean value by +8.62 (regret 9.88 → 1.26) by fixing the version-update family (keep-all value −119.5 in that bucket vs −0.54 with the fix), and hitchhiker memories should not default-keep (bucket value −1.90 vs +4.93 when archived).

**Theory-aligned variant (`sqcad_v2`) — SQCAD's Qualification rule.** We implement the Theorem-6 rule as SQCAD's actual Qualification rule: authorize `keep` only when the certificate interval is strictly positive (`positive`), archive when strictly negative (`negative`/`mismatch`), and on a crossing interval (`unresolved`) refuse to commit — the reversible defer path (archive + priced probe/restore, $C_{\text{probe}} = 1.0$ per task). With reversible probes this is exactly the decision probe-willing makes, and the two coincide action-for-action exactly — bootstrap diff 0.0000 to machine precision across all 1,380 episodes, because the decision functions are the same rule, not an empirical coincidence — we report this identity as a *sanity check of the equivalence*, not as a win: the rule's distinct content is the theory attribution, the audit layer (abstention precision, authorization, recovery below), and its external-validity row on public traces (§5.2) (round-1 review, 32- §2.1). The empirical confirmation of §3.6: false commits drop 0.228 → 0.072, regret 1.258 → 0.115, mean value −0.278 → +0.865. Significance is judged at the honest unit — the rule-world instantiations, n ≈ 14, bucket-paired bootstrap (n_boot = 2000, seed 20260817): vs the degenerate `sqcad_cert` the gain is +8.99 [−0.01, 27.48] (n.s., the interval straddles zero, so even the *direction* of the gain is not established at the honest unit count), vs `sqcad_cert_conflict` +0.50 [−0.02, 1.94] (n.s.) — large-magnitude but **not statistically significant at the honest unit count of 14**; we report the conservative bucket-level unit and disclose that the episode-level point estimate is +9.77 (n = 1,380; significant by the CI-excludes-zero rule) — the conclusions are drawn from the honest unit, not the flattering one. Keep on a non-positive certificate with oracle `archive` (the Theorem-5 blind commit) is exactly 0.0 across all 1,380 episodes. Of the 480 `unresolved` decisions, 65.6% are correctly resolved toward archive (abstention precision), 3.1% are missed keeps (15 episodes — every one with rescue_possible = false, so the probe/restore channel never rescues a wrongly archived decision memory within the 10-task horizon), and 31.3% are neutral. Where the restore channel is triggered (100/100 `harmful_stale/no_correction` episodes with an unresolved certificate), it runs with measured precision 0.0 — it restores *a* memory, never the decision memory.

**Event-level cost attribution.** The defer path's cost is attributed by the ablation switches under the `sqcad_v2` decision (same frozen rule worlds; each switch removes one mechanism; bucket-level CIs in brackets). Only the censoring switch is significant: v2 beats its no-censoring ablation by +11.34 [0.90, 30.73] — without the censor, archived (harmful) memories keep competing for exposure and destroy value; this is the mechanism Theorem 3 predicts, and the only effect that survives the honest unit count. Six ablation contrasts were tested; one significant effect is consistent with the censoring theory but is uncorrected for multiple comparisons, so we report it as mechanism-consistent, not as a standalone discovery. Removing probes *improves* value by +0.99 [−3.66, 0.00] (n.s.) and removing restore by +0.03 [−0.11, 0.00] (n.s.): the probe/restore channel measures net-negative on this benchmark — probes cost 1.0 per task and never rescued the decision memory (recovery 0/15) — so the mechanism story is "archive is right", not "archive is cheap to undo"; the channel's lifecycle-recovery value is not established here (its query-time value on L2 is separately reported, §5.2/§6(iii)). Lineage detection is exactly neutral under `sqcad_v2` (diff 0.0000): the Theorem-6 rule subsumes the lineage-conflict fix because every conflicting `unresolved` candidate is refused by construction. A VOI-probe refinement that keeps rare single-session `unresolved` candidates (`sqcad_v2_probe`) loses −0.03 [−0.12, 0.00] (n.s.): the data prefer blanket defer-archive, and the rare-keep hypothesis is not supported on this benchmark. (Cloud-GPU re-run of this supplement: 33- report; numbers above are from the same frozen seed set, bit-identical across environments.)

**Ablations and sensitivity.** no-censoring changes the value of 730/1,380 episodes (mean −10.71 under the `sqcad_v2` decision, matching the −10.71 ablation row in the frozen JSON); under `sqcad_v2` the other four switches become informative as reported above. **What L3 can and cannot claim.** It can claim: under the frozen rule world and the counterfactual contract, authorization policies are directly scored by lifecycle value, regret, false-commit, and recovery; the Theorem-6 rule is verified to make exactly the decision probe-willing makes (identity, sanity-checked at 0.0000), never commits on a non-positive certificate (0.0 blind commits), and measures abstention precision (0.656), authorization, and recovery directly; and the *direction* of the mechanism effects is stable across units, with only the censoring suppression significant at n ≈ 14. It cannot claim: that LifecycleBench represents real agent-memory distributions, that effects beyond the censoring suppression are statistically significant at the honest unit count, that the probe/restore channel has positive lifecycle value on this parameterization, or that rule-world results are deployment results. Phase B (end-to-end agent loop with the same contract) and external human anchoring (R6) remain open.

### 5.4 Fairness, significance, reproducibility

- **Statistical protocol.** Paired studentized bootstrap over the correct units (480 traces with needed evidence for LME-S — 500 total, 20 carry none, and hit/recall are undefined on them, so they are excluded from the bootstraps; 10 conversations for LoCoMo — the 1,986 QA are answer-scoring units, not independent samples; 1,380 episodes for L3), n_boot = 2000, pre-registered seeds, CI-excludes-zero significance. The tier-B substitute rows are run through the same paired protocol on the same units (two pre-registered seeds, 20260812/20260817, reported jointly; both agree on every comparison, §5.2). For L3, framework-claim comparisons are additionally (and primarily) judged at the rule-world bucket level (n ≈ 14): episodes within a bucket are surface replications of one mechanism instantiation — verified in the frozen data, 13/14 buckets have exactly one distinct value — so episode-level precision is not claimed.
- **Reproducibility.** One cross-environment nondeterminism (set-iteration-order dependence in the version map) was found and fixed; after the fix the 60-policy × 3-metric public contract is bit-identical across Windows/Python and Linux/Python3.12 environments; LifecycleBench hashes match across environments; 67 tests pass (23 contract + 21 fairness + 23 reproducibility); all data assets are frozen with SHA-256. Determinism claims cover CPU execution; GPU float non-determinism is outside the claim (the frozen dense rows are pinned to cached CPU artifacts; the tier-B Qwen3-0.6B/Qwen3-8B rows' embeddings are computed on the cloud GPU and cached, with model files SHA-verified against HF hashes and loaded offline — 33- §5 — but their bit-identity across GPU runs is explicitly not claimed).
- **Transductive disclosure, quantified.** The `memory_worth` and `causal_item` proxy rows batch-score over the full QA query set of their trace (future-query leakage on LoCoMo's 1,986 QAs). Strict-online versions (`memory_worth_online` / `causal_item_online`) that update counters strictly in QA order — retention for task $t$ sees only tasks $1..t{-}1$ — are run through the identical contract and official scorer (Appendix D). Result: the leakage does **not** materially inflate the proxies — LoCoMo official F1 0.0146 vs 0.0146 (MW) and 0.0092 vs 0.0082 (CMI, online slightly *higher*); LME-S hit 0.048 → 0.000 (MW) and 0.071 → 0.071 (CMI). On LongMemEval-S's one-task cold start, the online rows honestly report the uninformative-prior behavior the transductive rows masked. The proxies remain tier-B structural controls; the leak concern is now empirically bounded, not merely disclosed.

## 6 Discussion

**What drives the improvements.** (i) The candidate guard repairs coverage because the primary L2 failure is one-shot candidate visibility, not long-term authorization — the mechanism analysis shows the guard changes exposure, never retention. (ii) The lineage-conflict fix works because it converts a *crossing* interval into a *negative* one for conflicting versions, i.e., it is literally the Theorem-6 rule applied to a specific certificate source — and the rule then subsumes it by construction (0.0000 diff, §5.3). (iii) Probe/restore are query-time coverage machinery whose complexity order is matched to the statistical information cost of the archived-committed class (Theorem 8); their *lifecycle-recovery* value is parameterization-dependent, and on L3 it is structurally asymmetric and measured net-negative (episode-level no_probe/no_restore improvements, n.s. at the bucket level). The asymmetry: a wrongly *kept* memory stays observable and can be re-archived at the next epoch boundary at zero cost, while a wrongly *archived* memory is recoverable only through the priced probe/restore channel — which on this benchmark never rescues the decision memory (0/15 missed keeps are rescueable — all have rescue_possible = false; the 100 triggered restores measure precision 0.0; 565/565 archive-oracle episodes are unrescueable by construction) — while on L2 the same machinery is load-bearing for QA-time recovery (removing probes drops LoCoMo hit 0.212 → 0.084 and F1 0.0339 → 0.0158, 16- §2.2). The honest summary: the channel is the statistically correct recovery mechanism, its realized value depends on the parameterization, and on the counterfactual benchmark it does not pay for itself — we say "the channel's complexity order is matched", not "the channel improves the benchmark".

**Public vs counterfactual evidence.** L2 claims a quality–storage–exposure trade-off under a unified contract; L3 claims same-future counterfactual governance value under a frozen rule world. Neither is a deployment-SOTA claim, and the paper never writes "low persistent storage" as "low total cost": exposure, probe, restore, and harm terms are reported alongside.

**Cost semantics.** In L2, exposure tokens are a genuine increase for SQCAD (2,722 vs 493–1,973 across the retrieval and tier-B rows); in L3, the utility contract charges 0.05 per exposed memory, 1.0 per probe, 0.01 per stored token per epoch — a framework that keeps everything is punished on storage and a framework that archives everything is punished on missed-commit and recovery.

**Positioning.** SQCAD differs from causal memory (CMI: query-local estimand), OPE/bandit (lacks the action-dependent evidence-flow state), selective retention (lacks certificate/authorization semantics), and governance benchmarks (GateMem: evaluation, not policy). The reduction-separation theorem (Theorem 4) formalizes why the evidence-flow state cannot be absorbed into a richer contextual encoding.

**Limitations.** (1) Theorem 9's observational route (sequential g-formula / DR at full lifecycle level) is stated and its protocol route verified; the full observational implementation is future work, and Theorem 10's decision-consistency guarantees are conditional on honest certificates — a miscalibrated certificate degrades the no-confident-error bound at the same rate, which we do not claim to repair. (2) Scope transport (Cor 1) needs Phase B; the `future_in_s2` family exposes the look-ahead gap and the scope-literal fix over-corrects globally. (3) External human anchoring (R6) is pending; the rule world is synthetic. (4) Named systems are reproduced at most to the verified-layer tier (Appendix D); proxy rows are disclosed. (5) Parameter sensitivity is real: R2's GAMMA region must be respected when reading L3. (6) L3's headline effects are not significant at the honest unit count of 14; only the censoring suppression is, and every other L3 mechanism claim is qualitative (§5.3). (7) We do not measure end-to-end latency, wall-clock time, or FLOPs — cost is reported in contract tokens (exposure/storage/probe) only; a weighted total cost exists in the contract ($u_t$, §2.3) but is reported per component so that no single number hides which component moves.

## 7 Conclusion

Agent memory governance lacks a formal target for its central action — whether a memory should remain persistently accessible. We formalized that target (persistent-access lifecycle value), proved that the two signals the field currently uses (association, query-local intervention) are insufficient for it, proved that committing without a recovery channel costs $\Theta(T)$ self-obscuring regret that no faithful reduction to standard sequential decision-making can avoid, and proved that when the lifecycle contrast is not decision-identifiable, refusal or probing is necessary — under the worst-case (no-prior) criterion we adopt for governance of shared persistent state — rather than merely conservative behavior, with the matching sufficiency direction that honest certificates make interval authorization decision-consistent: bounded confident-error rate, refusal on the unidentified class forced by the data, and gap-consistent certification (Theorem 10). SQCAD operationalizes these results as an Evidence–Qualification–Access framework in which persistent actions are authorized only by auditable certificates (the Theorem-6 interval rule, Algorithm 1), and the bounded candidate guard repairs coverage without relaxing authorization. Public traces (L2) and the counterfactual LifecycleBench (L3) provide complementary external and internal validity: L2 claims a measured quality–storage–exposure trade-off under a unified cost contract, with the proposed rule's own public row reported; L3 verifies the decision-rule identity with the probe-willing baseline (sanity check), the censoring mechanism at the honest unit count (n ≈ 14, the only significant L3 effect), and reports all other mechanism directions as qualitative. The path forward — Phase B end-to-end deployment, the full observational route (Theorem 9), and external anchoring — is spelled out, not claimed.

### A Full proofs and supporting lemmas

**Coupled-world construction (shared exogenous stream).** All constructions below couple the K and A worlds on a single exogenous random stream $\mathcal{E} = (T_t, d_t, \varepsilon_t, E_t)_{t \ge 1}$: identical task arrivals $T_t$, identical task difficulty $d_t$, identical noise $\varepsilon_t$, and — given identical states and actions — identical exposure indicators $E_t$. The two worlds differ only in the *structural equation* mapping $(\cdot)$ to the outcome/value $Y_t$, never in the machinery that generates observations. Every difference between worlds is therefore a difference in how the *same* stream is valued, which is exactly the unidentifiable object.

**Lemma 1 (observational equivalence).** Under the shared-coupling construction, the K and A worlds produce bit-identical observation logs on the pre-decision window $[1, n_{\text{early}}]$.

*Proof.* In the Theorem-1 pair the two structural equations evaluate to the *identical* outcome $\beta_1 E_t - \tfrac12 d_t + \varepsilon_t$ on the shared stream at every horizon — the sign of the target enters the estimand (the intervention target), never the observed mechanism — so the logs are bit-identical a fortiori, on $[1, n_{\text{early}}]$ and beyond. In the general constructions the early window is sign-free by design: early exposures and early observations depend only on the shared exogenous stream, which is drawn independent of the latent sign $\pm\tau$; under a censored (archived-committed) trajectory no further observations arrive in either world after the decision, so realized logs coincide there as well. Both worlds receive the same task sequence, the same candidates, and the same noise, so every field of the log — timestamps, queries, candidate ids, exposure indicators, observed outcomes — is algebraically identical on $[1, n_{\text{early}}]$. In the verified instance the two joint logs have maximum field difference $0.0$. $\square$

**Theorem 1 (observational non-identifiability of the lifecycle estimand).** There exist two worlds $M_1, M_2$ sharing a bit-identical observational stream on which the lifecycle estimand $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ has opposite signs.

*Proof (construction).* Fix the shared exogenous stream and set exposure $\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ independent of difficulty, $E_t \perp d_t$. Consider two memories $i$ (the decision memory) and $j$ (a co-exposed companion) whose exposure indicators are always equal, $E_{i,t} = E_{j,t} =: E_t$, so the log records one exposure indicator per step. Define two structural models, identical in every observable respect, written per memory:

$$
M_1:\; Y_t = \beta_1\, E_{i,t} + 0 \cdot E_{j,t} - \tfrac{1}{2}\, d_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \beta_1 > 0 ,
$$

$$
M_2:\; Y_t = \beta_{2s}\, E_{i,t} + \beta_{2p}\, E_{j,t} - \tfrac{1}{2}\, d_t + \varepsilon_t, \qquad \beta_{2s} < 0,\;\; \beta_{2s} + \beta_{2p} = \beta_1 .
$$

In $M_1$, memory $i$ is genuinely useful and $j$ is a passenger; in $M_2$, $i$ genuinely degrades the outcome ($\beta_{2s} < 0$) while $j$ is the useful memory, its effect $\beta_{2p} = \beta_1 - \beta_{2s} > \beta_1$ compensating so that the *joint* exposure coefficient is identical. The two-memory structure is necessary, not cosmetic: a single variable $E$ forces the total structural effect of exposure to equal the observed coefficient, so identical logs would force identical structural effects and no sign flip; the companion $j$ — whose generation is action-invariant — is what allows the observed joint coefficient to match ($\beta_{2s} + \beta_{2p} = \beta_1$) while $i$'s own effect, the only part the persistent action touches, flips sign. On the shared stream $E_i = E_j = E_t$, both equations evaluate to $Y_t = \beta_1 E_t - \tfrac{1}{2} d_t + \varepsilon_t$ at every horizon: identical realized outcomes, identical joint laws of $(C_t, E_t, Y_t)$ — the two worlds are observationally indistinguishable from any log of any length, and every log-measurable association estimate (association, gated association) evaluates the same functional of the identical log and returns $\beta_1$ in both worlds.

The lifecycle estimand separates them because the persistent action touches only $i$'s generation mechanism. Let $n_{\text{early}}$ be the decision point (end of the pre-decision window) and $H'$ the post-decision rollout length over which lifecycle value accrues, as in §3.1; the contrast therefore sums over $(n_{\text{early}}, n_{\text{early}}+H']$. Under $\text{do}(\texttt{keep}_i)$, $i$ remains in the candidate-generation rule: $E_{i,t} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$ for $t \in (n_{\text{early}}, n_{\text{early}}+H']$. Under $\text{do}(\texttt{archive}_i)$, $i$ is excluded from the mechanism, $E_{i,t} := 0$, while $j$'s generation is unaffected (co-occurrence is not causation: $i$'s action neither generates nor suppresses $j$). $j$'s contribution $\beta_{2p} E_{j,t}$ is therefore action-invariant and cancels in the contrast, giving

$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive})
= \sum_{t = n_{\text{early}}+1}^{n_{\text{early}}+H'} \mathbb{E}[\beta_i\, E_{i,t} \mid \text{do}(\texttt{keep})] - \mathbb{E}[\beta_i\, E_{i,t} \mid \text{do}(\texttt{archive})]
= \beta_i \cdot p \cdot H' ,
$$

which is $\beta_1 \cdot p \cdot H' > 0$ in $M_1$ (keep correct) and $\beta_{2s} \cdot p \cdot H' < 0$ in $M_2$ (archive correct). This is the sign-flip pair used by Theorems 5–6 with $|\tau_1| = |\beta_1| p H'$, $|\tau_2| = |\beta_{2s}| p H'$.

No estimator measurable in the log alone — association, gated association, or query-local effect — can be sign-consistent on this pair: the perfect co-exposure makes $E_i$ and $E_j$ never vary separately in observed data, so any effect estimate computable from the log identifies the *joint* coefficient $\beta_1$ in both worlds, and the SCM-level local effect of exposing $i$ alone ($\beta_{2s}$ in $M_2$) is reachable only by intervening on $i$ while holding $j$ fixed — the protocol route of Theorem 9/§5.1, exactly the interventional access the pair denies to log-measurable rules. (The insufficiency of *reachable* local effects — identical values across memories with opposite lifecycle signs — is Theorem 2's separate construction.) $\square$

**Theorem 2 (local effects are insufficient; crowding-out and bridge).** There exist worlds in which two candidate memories have *identical* source-period local effects yet *opposite* lifecycle signs, via future-rollout channels invisible to the local effect.

*Proof (construction).* Consider candidate memories $i$ (high-volume, non-critical) and $j$ (sparse) for a single rare critical memory $k$. In the source period, both $i$ and $j$ serve identical queries with identical marginal quality, so both have local effect $\alpha > 0$ (Definition, §3.1) — and $\alpha$ is all any query-local estimand can measure.

Future rollout, horizon $H'$: memory $i$ crowds out $k$. Each exposure of $i$ displaces $k$ from the store with probability $\rho_c$ (per-exposure collision), and crowding is permanent — the displacement is not reversed by later traffic. The number of steps for which $k$ remains accessible is $\sum_{t=1}^{H'} (1 - \rho_c p)^{t-1} = \frac{1 - (1 - \rho_c p)^{H'}}{\rho_c p} \approx 8$ (geometric sum; the first crowding event occurs at expected time $1/(\rho_c p)$; with $\rho_c = 0.25$, $p = 0.5$, $H' = 2000$ this is exactly $\frac{1 - 0.875^{2000}}{0.125} = 8.0$), leaving $H' - 8 \approx 1992$ crowded-out steps on which rare tasks fail. Memory $j$ provides a retrieval bridge: keeping $j$ routes rare queries to $k$ with success probability $r_{\text{high}} = 0.7$; archiving $j$ removes the channel, success $r_{\text{low}} = 0$.

Writing $\beta_k$ for the value of a successful rare-task step and $q_{\text{rare}}$ for the rare-task arrival rate, the lifecycle differences are

$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_i) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_i)
= -\, 0.7\, \beta_k\, q_{\text{rare}}\left[ H' - \frac{1 - (1 - \rho_c p)^{H'}}{\rho_c p} \right] < 0 ,
$$

$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_j) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_j)
= \beta_k\, q_{\text{rare}}\, H'\, (r_{\text{high}} - r_{\text{low}})
= 0.7\, \beta_k\, q_{\text{rare}}\, H' > 0 .
$$

Local effects are identical ($\alpha$ for both), lifecycle signs are opposite; the identifiability gap is driven entirely by future-rollout structure — crowding-out latency for $i$, retrieval reach for $j$ — none of which appears in the source-period log. $\square$

**Theorem 9 — sufficient identification family C1–C8.** C1 consistency; C2 sequential exchangeability at epoch boundary; C3 positivity (observational overlap or protocol randomization); C4 treatment observability; C5 exposure/position/budget observability; C6 adoption measurement with error model; C7 interference specification (co-memory composition in state, explicit budget); C8 measurement stability (scope/version strata). Under C1–C8 the sequential g-formula over the post-intervention process identifies $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ (Robins 1986; Murphy 2003); the protocol-route rollout estimator is unbiased with honest CIs; the DR functional is $\sqrt{n}$-consistent under one correct nuisance (Dudík et al. 2011; Jiang & Li 2016); C6/C7 failure widens the certificate interval to bounds (adoption-error bounds via the error model; bundle-retreat bounds for interference, Lemma B), C3/C8 failure yields `unresolved`/`mismatch`. Lemma A–D: each of C6/C7/C2–C3/C8 is replaceable in some world (protocol independence, bundle retreat, IV, current-value identification) — the family is sufficient, not minimal.

**Lemma 2 (silent freeze).** If $p_{\text{arch}} = 0$, the archived state generates no new observations: the policy's information set is frozen from the decision point until a state transition, and a committing policy has no observation-generating action to revise it.

*Proof.* Exposure is the only source of new observations. In the archived state $P(e_t = 1) = 0$ a.s., so no candidate, query, or evidence value enters the log; the information set satisfies $I_t = I_{n_{\text{early}}}$ for all $t$ while the state remains archived. A committing policy (no probe/restore channel) has no action that transitions the state or produces observations, so the information set is frozen until the horizon. $\square$

**Theorem 3(a).** *Proof.* (i) By Lemma 1, the two worlds share a bit-identical log on the pre-decision window; the decision at $n_{\text{early}}$ is measurable in $I_{n_{\text{early}}}$, hence the same action in both worlds. (ii) A wrong decision loses $\nu$ per step: in the K world the oracle keeps (earning $+10$ per exposed step), while an archived state has $p_{\text{arch}} = 0$, so exposure stops and the value is $0$ — loss $\nu = \tau_{\text{step}} p = 10 \times 0.6 = 6.0$ per step; in the A world the oracle archives (value $0$), while keeping exposes at rate $p$ and earns $-10$ per exposed step — expected loss $\nu$ per step. (iii) The policy is committing: no state transition and, by Lemma 2, no new information, so the loss is never revised. Hence $R_T(\pi) = \nu (T - n_{\text{early}}) = 6.0 \times 1950 = 11700 = \Theta(T)$, slope $11700/2000 = 5.85$. The bound covers calibrated gates: a gate that resolves the identified set to the wrong sign (the early stream is confounded) commits with calibrated probabilities and still fails identically. Six committing strategies reproduce slope $5.8500$ exactly over 12 seeds (closed-form arithmetic, §5.1). $\square$

**Theorem 3(b).** *Proof.* Probe attempts arrive at rate $q$ per step; each succeeds with probability $\rho = \Phi((\tau_{\text{step}} - \theta)/\sigma)$ — a threshold crossing in probe noise, independent of $q$ — and a success triggers permanent restore at cost $c_{\text{restore}}$. Coupling the attempt clock to a geometric clock with success probability $q\rho$ per step, the time to first success satisfies $\mathbb{E}[t^* - n_{\text{early}}] \le 1/(q\rho)$, and the number of attempts satisfies $\mathbb{E}[N_{\text{attempts}}] = 1/\rho$ (each attempt succeeds w.p. $\rho$; the probe-expenditure term is $q$-invariant). Until the restore, the policy is wrong at rate $\nu$ per step (the argument of (a)(ii)), each attempt costs $c_{\text{probe}}$, and the restore costs $c_{\text{restore}}$ once:

$$
\mathbb{E}[R_T] \le \nu \cdot \mathbb{E}[t^* - n_{\text{early}}] + c_{\text{probe}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[N_{\text{attempts}}] + c_{\text{restore}}
\le \frac{\nu}{q\rho} + \frac{c_{\text{probe}}}{\rho} + c_{\text{restore}} = O(1/q),
$$

independent of $T$. After the successful probe restores the memory, evidence arrives at the uncensored rate $p$, the running mean converges to $\tau_{\text{step}} > \theta$, and the loss stops; the bound is attained at $q \to 1$ absent probe interference. Numerics: $q = 0.01/0.05/0.2$ give slopes $2.454/0.425/0.110$, and the inverted $\hat\rho = \nu/(q \cdot \text{slope} \cdot T) = 0.122/0.141/0.136$ is $q$-stable as the model requires (§3.4). $\square$

**Theorem 3(c).** *Proof.* If $p_{\text{arch}} = p$, exposure continues at rate $p$ in the archived state, so observations keep arriving. The running mean of evidence values converges to $\tau_{\text{step}} > \theta$ a.s.; by Chebyshev's inequality, $P(\bar{x}_k > \theta) \ge 1 - \delta$ once $k \ge \lceil \sigma^2\, \Phi^{-1}(1 - \delta/2)^2 / (\tau_{\text{step}} - \theta)^2 \rceil$, so a watchful running-mean gate self-corrects after $O(1)$ steps independent of $T$ (at rate $p$, $k_0/p$ steps), and the wrong-state loss is bounded by $\nu \cdot k_0/p = O(1)$. The contrast with (a) is the mechanism: with $p_{\text{arch}} = 0$ the *same* gate cannot correct because Lemma 2 freezes its information set; the action→evidence feedback turns "would-have-corrected" into "cannot-correct". Numerics: slopes drop to $0.0440$ (W0) / $0.0663$ (W1) under $p_{\text{arch}} = p$ — the ablation criterion isolating the censor. $\square$

**Definition 1 (faithful feedback-preserving reduction).** A reduction $R = (R_W, R_\pi, \phi)$ from a problem class $\mathcal{C}$ to $\mathcal{C}'$ consists of (i) a world map $R_W$ assigning each source world $W$ an image world $W' = R_W(W)$; (ii) a strategy map $R_\pi$ pulling target policies back to source policies ($\pi' \mapsto \pi$), preserving action sets; (iii) a **world-independent** observation map $\phi$ — a function of observed histories only, never of latent world identity; (iv) feedback preservation: image observations of $\pi'$ under $R_W(W)$ equal $\phi$ of source observations of $R_\pi(\pi')$ under $W$; (v) fidelity: regret is preserved up to $o(T)$ per policy. The $o(T)$ slack is deliberate: even reductions that perturb per-policy regret sublinearly are faithful, so the obstruction is the world-independent map, never the regret-tracking clause.

**Lemma 3 (reduction invariance of observational equivalence).** Under a faithful reduction, the coupled K/A pair maps to image processes that are observationally identical: any deterministic target policy makes the same action sequence in both images.

*Proof.* By the coupled construction and Lemma 1, $\text{obs}_K(\pi) = \text{obs}_A(\pi)$ for every source policy $\pi$: after the decision point the shared exogenous stream and identical action sequences keep the observation streams identical — the worlds differ only in the *value* of observations, never in the machinery that generates them. By world-independence of $\phi$ and feedback preservation, $\text{obs}'_K(\pi') = \phi(\text{obs}_K(R_\pi(\pi'))) = \phi(\text{obs}_A(R_\pi(\pi'))) = \text{obs}'_A(\pi')$, so $\pi'$ receives identical histories in both image worlds and executes the same action sequence. $\square$

**Lemma 4 (pointwise regret identity).** For any strategy (any adaptivity, any randomization), $\text{Regret}_K(\pi) + \text{Regret}_A(\pi) = \nu (T - n_{\text{early}}) = 11700.0$ deterministically.

*Proof.* Let $E$ be the number of exposed steps after $n_{\text{early}}$ under $\pi$ — identical in both worlds by Lemma 3. In the K world the oracle keeps and earns $\nu$ per exposed step, so $\text{Regret}_K = \nu(T - n_{\text{early}}) - \nu E$. In the A world the oracle archives and earns $0$; $\pi$'s exposure earns $-\nu$ per exposed step, so $\text{Regret}_A = 0 - (-\nu E) = \nu E$. The sum is $\nu(T - n_{\text{early}})$, a fixed constant — the identity holds pathwise, and "almost surely" is unnecessary. $\square$

**Theorem 4.** *Proof.* Fix any target strategy $\pi'$; let $\pi = R_\pi(\pi')$. By Lemma 3, $\pi'$ makes the same action sequence in both image worlds, so by fidelity its image regrets equal the source regrets up to $o(T)$: $\text{Regret}_{R_W(W)}(\pi') = \text{Regret}_W(\pi) \pm o(T)$. Then

$$
\max_{W \in \{K,A\}} \text{Regret}_{R_W(W)}(\pi')
\ge \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\text{Regret}_K(\pi) + \text{Regret}_A(\pi)\bigr) - o(T)
= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\nu\,(T - n_{\text{early}}) - o(T) = 5850 - o(T),
$$

by Lemma 4. Since the bound holds for every $\pi'$ in the image of any faithful reduction, no faithful reduction admits a sublinear-regret strategy on all images; the escape routes are exactly Conjecture 1's: add a restore/lineage channel (sufficiency, Corollary 2) or break faithfulness by smuggling evidence-availability into the state — the state standard bandit/OPE frameworks do not carry, and the assumption that per-step loss is observable at decision time, which the censor violates (Theorem 3). $\square$

**Theorem 5.** *Proof.* Let $p = P(\text{commit } \texttt{keep})$, measurable in the log; the log is bit-identical across worlds, so the randomization cannot condition on world identity. In the keep-correct world ($\tau = +1100$), an archive decision loses $1100$: $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = +1100] = (1-p) \cdot 1100$. In the archive-correct world ($\tau = -1650$), a keep decision loses $1650$: $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = -1650] = p \cdot 1650$. The worst case is $\max\{(1-p)\cdot 1100,\; p \cdot 1650\}$, minimized at the balance point $(1-p) \cdot 1100 = p \cdot 1650$, i.e., $p^* = 1100/2750 = 0.4$, with value $660 = 1100 \cdot 1650 / 2750$ — the minimax regret of every committing rule. The transposed sign assignment swaps which magnitude is keep-correct; the minimax value is labeling-invariant, and the $p^* = 0.4$ reported here is the one the exhaustive grid of §5.1 verifies. Classification error: any log-measurable rule decides identically in both worlds and therefore errs in exactly one; under the uniform prior the error is exactly $1/2$ for *every* rule — the regret minimizer $p^* = 0.4$ and the error minimizer $p = 1/2$ differ, and conflating them is a bug, not a choice. $\square$

**Theorem 6.** *Proof.* (a) If $L > 0$, every compatible world has $\tau \ge L > 0$, so committing `keep` is correct in all of them: worst-case regret $0$. (b) If $U < 0$, symmetrically, committing `archive` has worst-case regret $0$. (c) If $L \le 0 \le U$, generalize the Theorem 5 derivation to the interval: for $p = P(\text{keep})$, $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = U] = (1-p)U$ and $\mathbb{E}[\text{regret} \mid \tau = L] = p(-L)$; the worst case $\max\{(1-p)U,\; p(-L)\}$ is minimized at $(1-p)U = p(-L)$, i.e., $p^* = U/(U-L)$, giving $R^*(L,U) = U(-L)/(U-L)$, attained by the minimax rule. `defer` makes no persistent change and carries committing-action regret $0$ at explicit price $C_{\text{defer}}$; a probe costs $C_{\text{probe}}$ and shrinks the identified set to $[L', U']$, after which the decision costs $R^*(L', U')$. The optimal decision is $\min\{R^*(L,U),\; C_{\text{defer}},\; C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L',U')\}$; the probe is worth paying iff $C_{\text{probe}} + R^*(L',U') < \min\{R^*(L,U), C_{\text{defer}}\}$. Verified instance $(L,U) = (-1650, 1100)$: commit-keep regret $-L = 1650$, commit-archive regret $U = 1100$, minimax $R^* = 1100 \cdot 1650/2750 = 660$ at $p^* = 1100/2750 = 0.4$, matching the exhaustive grid. $\square$

**Theorem 7.** *Proof.* For $y \sim \mathcal{N}(\pm\mu, \sigma^2)$, the per-sample KL divergence is $\text{KL}(\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)\,\Vert\,\mathcal{N}(-\mu,\sigma^2)) = 2\mu^2/\sigma^2$. For $n$ independent samples the Hellinger affinity is $\int \sqrt{dP_+^n\, dP_-^n} = \exp(-n \cdot \mu^2/(2\sigma^2)) = \exp(-n \cdot \text{KL}/4)$. By the Le Cam two-point bound with the Bretagnolle–Huber inequality, any test $\varphi$ has minimax error $e^* := \inf_\varphi \max\bigl(P_+^n[\varphi = 0],\; P_-^n[\varphi = 1]\bigr) \ge \tfrac{1}{2}\exp(-n \cdot \text{KL}/2)$. Requiring $e^* \le \delta$ gives $n \ge \frac{\sigma^2 \log(1/\delta)}{2\mu^2}$ (up to the constants of the inequality, $\log(1/2\delta) \approx \log(1/\delta)$ for small $\delta$); writing $\Delta = 2\mu$ for the separation of the two means, $n = \Omega(\log(1/\delta)/\Delta^2)$. The Gaussian mean $\mu$ is the probe-signal parameter, distinct from the per-step loss rate $\nu = \tau_{\text{step}} p$ of §3.4. $\square$

**Theorem 8.** *Proof.* Any strategy in the archived-committed class must accumulate $N^* = N^*(\delta)$ successful probes before it can distinguish the worlds at error $\le \delta$ (Theorem 7, applied to the probe evidence). Each probe attempt succeeds w.p. $\rho$, so the expected number of attempts to reach $N^*$ successes is $\mathbb{E}[N_{\text{attempts}}] = N^*/\rho$ (negative-binomial mean), and since attempts arrive at rate at most $q$ per step, $\mathbb{E}[t^* - n_{\text{early}}] \ge \mathbb{E}[N_{\text{attempts}}]/q = N^*/(q\rho)$. Until the world is resolved, the policy is wrong and loses $\nu$ per step (Theorem 3(a)(ii)); each attempt costs $c_{\text{probe}}$; the recovery latency is absorbed in the time bound. Hence

$$
\mathbb{E}[\text{Regret}_K] \ge \nu \cdot \frac{N^*}{q\rho} + c_{\text{probe}} \cdot \frac{N^*}{\rho}
= \frac{N^*}{\rho}\left(\frac{\nu}{q} + c_{\text{probe}}\right),
$$

decomposed into lifecycle loss while wrong ($\nu N^*/(q\rho)$), probe expenditure ($c_{\text{probe}} N^*/\rho$, $q$-invariant, matching Theorem 3(b)), and recovery latency. $\square$

**Corollary 3.** *Proof.* The SQCAD-style restore channel instantiates Corollary 2's bound with the policy named: $R_T^{\text{SQCAD}} \le \nu/(q\rho) + c_{\text{probe}}/\rho + c_{\text{restore}}$, matching the lower bound within constants independent of $\tau$, $q$, $T$ — because one successful probe yields a continuous evidence stream at rate $p \gg q\rho$, restore is statistically more efficient than per-probe evidence acquisition. The recovery channel is the *correct complexity* of the problem, not an engineering add-on. $\square$

**Theorem 10 — sufficiency: decision-consistent interval authorization.** Fix any compatible world with lifecycle contrast $\tau$ and a certificate interval $[\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$ with honest coverage $\mathbb{P}(\tau \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]) \ge 1 - \alpha$.

(i) If the rule confidently authorizes `keep` ($\hat{L} > 0$) and the authorization is wrong ($\tau \le 0$), then $\tau \le 0 < \hat{L}$, so $\tau \notin [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$. If it confidently authorizes `archive` ($\hat{U} < 0$) and the authorization is wrong ($\tau \ge 0$), then $\tau \ge 0 > \hat{U}$, also $\tau \notin [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$. Hence {confident authorization is wrong} $\subseteq$ {$\tau \notin [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$}, and by honesty
$$
\mathbb{P}(\text{confident authorization is wrong}) \le 1 - (1 - \alpha) = \alpha + o(1),
$$
uniformly over the compatible class. $\square$

(ii) On a Theorem-1 sign-flip pair ($\tau_1 > 0 > \tau_2$), the two worlds share a bit-identical log (Lemma 1), and the certificate is a function of the log and pre-registered randomness, so the *identical* interval $[\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$ is produced in both worlds. Honesty in world 1 and in world 2, together with the union bound, gives
$$
\mathbb{P}\bigl(\tau_1, \tau_2 \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]\bigr) \ge 1 - 2\alpha.
$$
Since $\tau_2 < 0 < \tau_1$ and $[\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$ is an interval, on this event $\hat{L} \le \tau_2 < 0 < \tau_1 \le \hat{U}$, i.e., $0 \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]$: the gate authorizes neither `keep` nor `archive` and defers or probes (Theorem 6(c)) with probability $\ge 1 - 2\alpha$. On the complementary event (probability $\le 2\alpha$), any authorized action that is wrong is still charged against (i) — its world's $\tau$ lies outside the interval. $\square$

(iii) Consistency of the certificate gives $w_T = \hat{U} - \hat{L} \to 0$ in probability (protocol-route estimator, Theorem 9: width shrinks at the parametric rate). Fix $\tau > 0$ and threshold sequences $\delta_T \to 0$, $\alpha_T \to 0$ with $\delta_T / w_T \to \infty$. On the event $\{\tau \in [\hat{L}, \hat{U}]\}$ (probability $\ge 1 - \alpha_T$), we have $\hat{L} \ge \tau - w_T \ge \delta_T - w_T > 0$ eventually, so $\mathbb{P}(\text{confident keep}) \ge 1 - \alpha_T \to 1$. The symmetric argument for $\tau \le -\delta_T$ gives $\mathbb{P}(\text{confident archive}) \to 1$; combined with (i), the authorized action is correct with probability tending to 1. $\square$
