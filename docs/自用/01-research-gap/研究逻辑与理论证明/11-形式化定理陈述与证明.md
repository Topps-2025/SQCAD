## 0. Notation and Problem Setup

### 0.1 Observable history

For a memory-augmented agent operating over $H$ timesteps within scope $s$, the observable log is:

$$
O_{1:H}^{(s)} = \{(C_t, X_t, E_t, P_t, D_t, A_t^{\text{obs}}, Y_t, c_t)\}_{t=1}^{H},

$$

where:
- $C_t \subset \mathcal{M}$ — candidate memory set generated at time $t$;
- $X_t$ — task, user, tool, and model state;
- $E_{i,t} \in \{0,1\}$ — whether memory $i$ is exposed (inserted into the prompt/workplace) at $t$;
- $P_t$ — position and budget allocation across exposed memories;
- $D_{i,t} \in [0,1]$ — observable adoption proxy (e.g., attention weight, citation count);
- $A_{i,t}^{\text{obs}}$ — observed access action;
- $Y_t \in \mathbb{R}$ — task utility or outcome;
- $c_t \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ — token, latency, probe, or review cost.

### 0.2 Persistent access treatment

For each memory $i \in \mathcal{M}$, the persistent access action is:

$$
A_i^{\text{pers}} \in \mathcal{A} = \{\texttt{keep}, \texttt{downweight}, \texttt{isolate}, \texttt{archive}, \texttt{restore}\}.

$$

This action, once taken, changes which candidates $C_t$ can include memory $i$ at future timesteps $t' > t$. The action has an **effective duration** $\Delta t_{\text{eff}}$, may be **reversible** (restore), and may compete with other memories under a finite **workspace budget** $B$.

Distinguish from **query-local exposure treatment**:

$$
E_{i,t} \in \{0,1\},

$$

which affects only the current prompt composition at a single timestep $t$. A persistent action $A_i^{\text{pers}}$ changes the **mechanism** by which $E_{i,t}$ is generated at future $t$; a query-local exposure does not.

### 0.3 Target estimand: persistent-access lifecycle value

For scope $s$, target policy $\pi$, horizon $H$, and discount $\gamma \in (0,1]$:

$$
\boxed{
V_s^{\pi}(a) = \mathbb{E}^{\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1}\bigl(Y_t - \lambda C_t - \rho R_t\bigr) \;\middle|\; do(A_i^{\text{pers}} = a), s\right],
}

$$

where $\lambda, \rho \ge 0$ are pre-registered cost and risk weights, and $R_t$ is a risk or harm term.

The **lifecycle comparative estimand** is:

$$
\tau_s^{\pi}(a_1, a_0) = V_s^{\pi}(a_1) - V_s^{\pi}(a_0).

$$

### 0.4 Three estimands that must be kept separate

| Estimand | Treatment | What is held fixed | Question answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical association value | None (observational) | Historical policy | "Does this memory co-occur with success?" |
| Query-local intervention effect $\Delta_t(i)$ | $do(E_{i,t}=1)$ vs $do(E_{i,t}=0)$ | Query, candidate set, model, evaluator | "Does this memory help the current answer?" |
| Persistent-access lifecycle value $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ | $do(A_i^{\text{pers}}=a)$ | Future candidates regenerated by policy | "Does changing long-term access improve future trajectories?" |

## 1. Theorem 1: Observational Non-identifiability

### 1.1 Statement

**Theorem 1 (Observational non-identifiability of persistent-access lifecycle value).**
Let $O_{1:H}^{(s)} = \{(C_t, E_t, Y_t)\}_{t=1}^{H}$ be the historical log containing only candidate identity, exposure indicator, and scalar outcome. Assume:

1. **(A1)** The agent follows a history-dependent exposure policy $\pi_E$ such that $E_{i,t} = f_{\pi_E}(H_{t-1}, X_t)$.
2. **(A2)** At least two distinct memories $(i, j)$ may be co-exposed: $\exists t$ with $E_{i,t}=E_{j,t}=1$.
3. **(A3)** Task difficulty correlates with co-exposure patterns: $\text{Cov}(\text{difficulty}_t, E_{i,t} \mid E_{j,t}=1) \neq 0$ is possible.

Then there exist two structural causal models (SCMs) $\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2$ such that:

$$
P_{\mathcal{M}_1}(O_{1:H}^{(s)}) = P_{\mathcal{M}_2}(O_{1:H}^{(s)}),

$$

but:

$$
V_{s,\mathcal{M}_1}^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{s,\mathcal{M}_1}^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}) > 0,

$$
$$
V_{s,\mathcal{M}_2}^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{s,\mathcal{M}_2}^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}) < 0.

$$

Consequently, $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ is not point-identified from $(C_t, E_t, Y_t)_{t=1}^{H}$ alone.

### 1.2 Constructive proof

**Construction.** Fix a shared sequence of exogenous random variables across both models: task types $T_t \sim \text{Categorical}(\pi_T)$, base difficulties $d_t \sim U[0,1]$, and noise terms $\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$. Two memories $i$ (the target) and $j$ (a co-exposed memory) are exposed with **perfect correlation**: $E_{i,t} = E_{j,t} =: E_t$ for all $t$, where $E_t \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)$ with fixed exposure rate $p \in (0,1)$.

**Model $\mathcal{M}_1$ (memory $i$ is genuinely useful).**

$$
Y_t = \beta_1 \cdot E_t - \frac{1}{2}d_t + \varepsilon_t,

$$

where $\beta_1 > 0$. Memory $i$ exerts a positive causal effect $\beta_1$ on the outcome whenever exposed. Memory $j$ is a passenger: it is exposed alongside $i$ but contributes nothing to $Y_t$.

**Model $\mathcal{M}_2$ (memory $i$ is confounded and harmful).**

$$
Y_t = (\beta_{2s} + \beta_{2p}) \cdot E_t - \frac{1}{2}d_t + \varepsilon_t,

$$

where $\beta_{2s} < 0$ and $\beta_{2p} = \beta_1 - \beta_{2s} > \beta_1$. Memory $i$ exerts a **negative** causal effect $\beta_{2s}$ on the outcome. Memory $j$ exerts a strong positive effect $\beta_{2p}$. Because $E_{i,t} = E_{j,t} = E_t$, the observed outcome under co-exposure is $(\beta_{2s} + \beta_{2p})E_t = \beta_1 E_t$ — identical to $\mathcal{M}_1$.

**Verification of $P_{\mathcal{M}_1}(O) = P_{\mathcal{M}_2}(O)$.**

Since (i) the exogenous sequence $(T_t, d_t, \varepsilon_t, E_t)_{t=1}^{H}$ is identical across models, (ii) the structural equations for $Y_t$ produce algebraically identical values ($\beta_1 E_t - d_t/2 + \varepsilon_t$ in both models), the joint distribution of $(C_t, E_t, Y_t)_{t=1}^{H}$ is identical in $\mathcal{M}_1$ and $\mathcal{M}_2$.

**Lifecycle value divergence.**

Under a future rollout of length $H'$ with policy $\pi$:

- $\mathcal{M}_1$: keeping $i$ yields expected cumulative contribution $\beta_1 \cdot p \cdot H' > 0$. Archiving $i$ removes this contribution. Hence $V_{\mathcal{M}_1}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{\mathcal{M}_1}(\texttt{archive}) = \beta_1 p H' > 0$.

- $\mathcal{M}_2$: keeping $i$ yields expected cumulative contribution $\beta_{2s} \cdot p \cdot H' < 0$ (negative!). Archiving $i$ removes this harm. Hence $V_{\mathcal{M}_2}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{\mathcal{M}_2}(\texttt{archive}) = \beta_{2s} p H' < 0$.

The two lifecycle values have **opposite signs** despite identical observational distributions. Therefore $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ is not identified from $O_{1:H}^{(s)}$. $\square$

### 1.3 Concrete instance (verified in code)

With parameters $\beta_1 = 1.5$, $\beta_{2s} = -1.0$, $\beta_{2p} = 2.5$, $p = 0.55$, $H' = 2000$:

$$
V_{\mathcal{M}_1}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{\mathcal{M}_1}(\texttt{archive}) = 1.5 \times 0.55 \times 2000 = +1650,

$$
$$
V_{\mathcal{M}_2}(\texttt{keep}) - V_{\mathcal{M}_2}(\texttt{archive}) = -1.0 \times 0.55 \times 2000 = -1100.

$$

Memory Worth assigns score $>0.99$ to memory $i$ in both worlds (recommending "keep"), producing regret $1100.0$ in $\mathcal{M}_2$. See `gap_proof_experiments.py::run_proposition_a`.

### 1.4 What this theorem does NOT claim

- It does **not** claim that historical logs are useless — only that they are insufficient **without additional structure** (additional log fields, exchangeability, or micro-intervention).
- It does **not** claim that all associational methods always fail — only that there **exist** scenarios where they necessarily mislead.
- It does **not** claim that the co-exposure pattern in the proof is universal — it is a **sufficient** counterexample to point-identifiability.

## 2. Theorem 2: Local-Intervention Insufficiency

### 2.1 Statement

**Theorem 2 (Insufficiency of query-local intervention effects for lifecycle decisions).**
Let $\Delta_t(i) = \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid do(E_{i,t}=1)] - \mathbb{E}[Y_t \mid do(E_{i,t}=0)]$ be the query-local causal effect of exposing memory $i$ at timestep $t$, with query context, candidate set, model, and evaluator held fixed. Assume:

1. **(B1)** Persistent access actions can alter future candidate generation: $P(C_{t+1} \mid A_i^{\text{pers}}=a) \neq P(C_{t+1} \mid A_i^{\text{pers}}=a')$ for some $a \neq a'$.
2. **(B2)** Memories compete for a finite workspace budget $B < |\mathcal{M}|$.
3. **(B3)** Memories may serve as retrieval bridges for rare task types whose value is not captured in the source-period task distribution.

Then there exist two memories $(i, j)$ and a fixed query context such that:

$$
\Delta_t(i) = \Delta_t(j),

$$

but:

$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_i) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_i) < 0,

$$
$$
V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{keep}_j) - V_s^{\pi}(\texttt{archive}_j) > 0.

$$

Consequently, knowledge of all query-local intervention effects $\{\Delta_t(i)\}_{i \in \mathcal{M}}$ does **not** suffice to determine the optimal persistent-access action for each memory.

### 2.2 Constructive proof

**Construction.** Consider two memories: $i$ (short-term useful, long-term harmful) and $j$ (short-term appears same, long-term essential).

**Source period** ($t = 1, \dots, T_0$). For each query context, we simulate both $do(E=1)$ and $do(E=0)$ on the **same** query. By construction:

$$
Y_t \mid do(E_{i,t}=1) - Y_t \mid do(E_{i,t}=0) = \alpha,

$$
$$
Y_t \mid do(E_{j,t}=1) - Y_t \mid do(E_{j,t}=0) = \alpha,

$$

with $\alpha > 0$. Both memories produce the **exact same** local causal effect in the source period.

**Future rollout** ($t = T_0+1, \dots, T_0 + H'$). The key divergence:

- **Memory $i$ (m_short):** Its apparent usefulness in the source period was specific to the source task distribution. In the future rollout, $i$ provides **zero** marginal benefit. However, keeping $i$ active causes it to occupy workspace slots. With probability $\rho$ per exposure, $i$ crowds out a rare critical memory $k$ whose true effect is $\beta_k \gg \alpha$. Once $k$ is displaced, rare critical tasks ($P(\text{rare}) = q_{\text{rare}}$) lose their retrieval path and fail.

$$
V(\texttt{keep}_i) - V(\texttt{archive}_i) = \underbrace{0}_{i\text{'s future benefit}} - \underbrace{\beta_k \cdot q_{\text{rare}} \cdot H' \cdot [1 - (1-\rho p)^{H'}]}_{\text{expected loss from crowding out } k} < 0.

$$

- **Memory $j$ (m_long):** In the future rollout, $j$ serves as a retrieval bridge for rare critical tasks. Without $j$, the success rate on rare critical tasks drops from $r_{\text{high}}$ to $r_{\text{low}}$.

$$
V(\texttt{keep}_j) - V(\texttt{archive}_j) = \beta_k \cdot q_{\text{rare}} \cdot H' \cdot (r_{\text{high}} - r_{\text{low}}) > 0.

$$

Both $\Delta_t(i) = \Delta_t(j) = \alpha$, but their lifecycle values have opposite signs. Therefore query-local effects are insufficient for lifecycle decisions. $\square$

### 2.3 Concrete instance (verified in code)

With $\alpha = 2.0$, $\beta_k = 4.0$, $\rho = 0.25$, $p = 0.5$, $q_{\text{rare}} \approx 1/3$, $H' = 2000$:

$$
V(\texttt{keep}_{\text{short}}) - V(\texttt{archive}_{\text{short}}) \approx -1784,

$$
$$
V(\texttt{keep}_{\text{long}}) - V(\texttt{archive}_{\text{long}}) \approx +1776.

$$

A CMI-style decision rule ($\Delta > 0 \Rightarrow$ keep) recommends keeping both, producing regret $\approx 1784$ on the short memory. See `gap_proof_experiments.py::run_proposition_b`.

### 2.4 What this theorem does NOT claim

- It does **not** claim that CMI or local interventions are useless — they correctly answer "does this memory help the current answer?"
- It claims they are **insufficient** for the distinct question "should this memory remain persistently accessible?"
- It does **not** claim crowding always dominates — only that it **can**, and therefore local effects alone cannot guarantee correct lifecycle decisions.

## 3. Theorem 3: Identification under Sufficient, Operable, Auditable Authorization Conditions

> **更名说明（2026-08-13，必要性证明 `12` 文档）**：本定理原名 "minimal conditions"。Lemma A–D 表明完整 C1–C8 并非唯一识别路线：C6 对协议路线可不必要，C2/C3 可在额外 IV 假设下被替代，C7 可通过改变治理粒度退守到 bundle，C8 漂移时可改为识别当期 estimand。它们没有逐项证明 C1、C4、C5 不必要，也不应把改变 estimand/granularity 写成原问题上的逐条件反例。Theorem 4 证明治理必要性落在未识别类上的门控，而非完整条件族本身。故更名为“一组可操作、可审计的充分识别与治理授权条件”（a set of operable, auditable, sufficient identification and governance-authorization conditions）。

### 3.0 Decision-epoch model (treatment semantics made precise)

Time is discrete, $t = 1, 2, \dots$. The system re-evaluates access at **epoch boundaries** $t_0, t_0+H, t_0+2H, \dots$. Within an epoch $(t_0, t_0+H]$, the persistent action is **held fixed**.

**Pre-epoch state** at $t_0$:

$$
S_{t_0} = \bigl(\mathcal{M}_{t_0},\; B_{t_0},\; \pi,\; s,\; v_{\text{model}}, v_{\text{tool}}, v_{\text{eval}},\; \bar{H}_{t_0}\bigr),

$$

where $\mathcal{M}_{t_0}$ is the memory state (access status of every memory), $B_{t_0}$ the workspace budget, $\pi$ the target policy, $s$ the scope, $v$ the version identifiers, and $\bar{H}_{t_0}$ the full history.

**Persistent action is a one-shot treatment per epoch.** $A_i^{\text{pers}} \in \mathcal{A} = \{\texttt{keep}, \texttt{downweight}, \texttt{isolate}, \texttt{archive}, \texttt{restore}\}$ is assigned **once** at $t_0$ and held for the whole epoch. It is **not** re-randomized at every $t$ within the epoch; within-epoch exposure $E_{i,t}$ is a *consequence variable* (mediator), not a treatment. At the next epoch boundary the action may be re-assessed on the new state — so across epochs the protocol is a sequence of one-shot treatments, and each epoch's estimand conditions on the pre-epoch state. (This resolves "one-time vs time-varying": **one-shot within epoch, repeated across epochs**, with sequential exchangeability applied at epoch boundaries.)

**Post-intervention process** (the outcome process of the estimand), $t = t_0+1, \dots, t_0+H$ under $do(A_i^{\text{pers}}=a)$ with policy $\pi$:

$$
S_{t+1} \sim P_\pi\!\bigl(\cdot \mid \bar{H}_t,\; A_i^{\text{pers}}=a,\; s\bigr),

$$

where the state transition decomposes into the **identified kernels**:

$$
S_{t+1} = \bigl(C_{t+1},\; E_{t},\; P_{t},\; D_{t},\; Y_{t},\; \mathcal{M}_{t+1},\; B_{t+1}\bigr),

$$

- $C_{t+1}$ — **future candidate transition**: candidate generation given access status, budget, and policy (memory $i$'s inclusion is governed by $A_i^{\text{pers}}$); this is a **state variable of the estimand**, not an exogenous nuisance;
- $E_{t}$ — exposure/position allocation under budget $B$;
- $D_{t}$ — adoption proxy;
- $Y_{t}$ — **rollout outcome** (task utility accumulated during the epoch), with costs $C_t$ (tokens, latency, probes) and risk $R_t$;
- $\mathcal{M}_{t+1}$ — memory state updated by usage/adoption;
- $B_{t+1}$ — budget dynamics (co-memory competition).

**Target estimand (epoch form).** With pre-registered $(H, \gamma, \lambda, \rho)$:

$$
\boxed{
V_s^{\pi}(a \mid S_{t_0}) =
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}\!\left[\sum_{t=t_0+1}^{t_0+H} \gamma^{\,t-t_0-1}\bigl(Y_t - \lambda C_t - \rho R_t\bigr)
\;\middle|\; do(A_i^{\text{pers}}=a),\; S_{t_0},\; s\right],
}

$$

and the comparative estimand $\tau_s^{\pi}(a_1, a_0 \mid S_{t_0}) = V_s^{\pi}(a_1) - V_s^{\pi}(a_0)$.

**Answers to the five formulation questions:**

| 评述问题 | 精确定义 |
|---|---|
| persistent action 是一次性还是时变 treatment？ | **每个 epoch 内一次性**（t₀ 赋值后固定 H 步）；跨 epoch 是重复的一次性 treatment 序列，estimand 以 epoch 前状态为条件 |
| future candidate transition 是否进入状态？ | **是**：C_{t+1} 是后干预过程的状态变量，其生成核 P_π(C_{t+1} \| ·, a) 是 g-formula 的被积部分 |
| outcome 是否是真正的 rollout outcome？ | **是**：Y_t 在 epoch 内随时间累积；估计器的 horizon、discount、action window 必须与 estimand 定义完全一致 |
| adoption / interference / budget 如何进入状态？ | D_t 进入状态转移；workspace budget B 与 co-memory composition 显式建模（C7），不假设可加性 |
| target policy π 与 behavior policy π_b 的关系？ | π 定义价值（决定干预后候选/暴露机制）；π_b 生成日志。识别有两条路：协议随机化路（直接运行 π）与观测路（用 π_b 数据 + 核的 π 索引 + overlap/重要性加权） |

### 3.1 Statement

**Theorem 3 (Identification of persistent-access lifecycle value under sufficient, operable, auditable authorization conditions).**
Consider the decision-epoch model of §3.0. Let the logged data satisfy:

1. **(C1 — Consistency).** The executed access action equals the assigned action, and observed exposure is the consequence of the retained mechanism: $A_{i,t}^{\text{obs}} = A_i^{\text{pers}}$ on the assigned window, $E_{i,t} = E_{i,t}^{\text{mech}}(a)$.
2. **(C2 — Sequential exchangeability at the epoch).** $A_i^{\text{pers}} \perp\!\!\!\perp Y_{t_0+1:t_0+H}^{\text{pot}} \mid S_{t_0}$ — the access decision is unconfounded given the pre-epoch state. (Within the epoch there is no further treatment assignment, so no additional exchangeability is required.)
3. **(C3 — Positivity / overlap).** (i) Observational: $\pi_b(A_i^{\text{pers}}=a \mid S_{t_0}) \ge \delta > 0$ for all $a \in \mathcal{A}$ in every realized stratum; or (ii) **protocol**: micro-intervention randomizes $A_i^{\text{pers}}$ on eligible candidates with known propensity (design guarantee). Additionally, the target policy $\pi$'s action support lies within the support of logged exposure mechanisms.
4. **(C4 — Treatment observability).** Action type, effective window $[\tau_0, \tau_1]$, and any rollback are recorded.
5. **(C5 — Exposure / position / budget observability).** $C_t$, exposure propensity $\pi_E$, position $P_t$, and budget allocation $B_t$ are logged.
6. **(C6 — Adoption measurement).** Adoption proxy $D_{i,t}$ is recorded with an error model $P(D \mid \text{true adoption})$, or explicit non-identification bounds are reported.
7. **(C7 — Interference specification).** Co-memory composition enters the state; workspace budget is explicit; per-memory additive effects are not assumed; joint (bundle) interventions are used when co-exposure is collinear (cf. Proposition A).
8. **(C8 — Measurement stability).** $(s, v_{\text{model}}, v_{\text{tool}}, v_{\text{eval}})$ fixed within the evaluation window or explicitly version-stratified.

Then:

**(a) Protocol route (randomized micro-intervention).** If SQCAD randomizes $A_i^{\text{pers}}$ with known propensity and rolls the environment forward under $\pi$, the **rollout estimator**

$$
\boxed{
\hat{V}_s^{\pi}(a \mid S_{t_0}) =
\frac{1}{n_a}\sum_{k=1}^{n_a} \sum_{t=t_0+1}^{t_0+H}
\gamma^{\,t-t_0-1}\bigl(Y_t^{(k)} - \lambda C_t^{(k)} - \rho R_t^{(k)}\bigr)
}

$$

is **unbiased** for $V_s^{\pi}(a \mid S_{t_0})$: $\mathbb{E}[\hat{V}] = V$ (exact, no weighting needed — the protocol assigns the action), with honest standard errors from the between-trajectory variance, so $\hat{V} \pm 1.96\,\text{SE}$ is a valid 95% CI as $n_a \to \infty$.

**(b) Observational route (logged behavior).** Under (C1)–(C8) with behavior policy $\pi_b \neq \pi$, the lifecycle value is identified by the **sequential g-formula over the post-intervention process**:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
V_s^{\pi}(a \mid s_0) = \sum_{t=1}^{H} \gamma^{t-1} \int
\bigl(y_t - \lambda c_t - \rho r_t\bigr)\;
\prod_{\tau=1}^{t} \Bigl[
& P_\pi\bigl(c_\tau, e_\tau, p_\tau, d_\tau \mid \bar{h}_{\tau-1}, a, s_0\bigr) \\
& \times P\bigl(y_\tau, \mathcal{m}_\tau, b_\tau \mid \bar{h}_{\tau-1}, a, s_0\bigr)
\Bigr]\; d\bar{h}_{t-1},
\end{aligned}
}

$$

where $\bar{h}_{\tau-1}$ includes access status, budget, co-memory composition, adoption history, and version. Each kernel is identified from logs under (C1)–(C8) (C5 gives the exposure/position/budget kernels; C6 the adoption kernel; C8 the version strata). The **π-indexing** of the candidate/exposure kernels is the explicit encoding of the target policy; estimating them from $\pi_b$-data requires modeling the policy difference or overlap + importance weights on the exposure mechanism.

**(c) DR / OPE functional.** For the observational route, the causal contrast admits the efficient sequential DR estimator (one treatment per epoch; trajectory-level outcome):

$$
\hat{\tau}_s^{\pi}(a_1, a_0) =
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\Bigl[
\frac{\mathbf{1}[A_k = a_1]}{\hat{\pi}_A(a_1 \mid s_0^{(k)})}\bigl(G^{(k)} - \hat{Q}(a_1, s_0^{(k)})\bigr) + \hat{Q}(a_1, s_0^{(k)})
\Bigr] -
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\Bigl[
\frac{\mathbf{1}[A_k = a_0]}{\hat{\pi}_A(a_0 \mid s_0^{(k)})}\bigl(G^{(k)} - \hat{Q}(a_0, s_0^{(k)})\bigr) + \hat{Q}(a_0, s_0^{(k)})
\Bigr],

$$

where $G^{(k)} = \sum_t \gamma^{t-1}(Y_t^{(k)} - \lambda C_t^{(k)} - \rho R_t^{(k)})$ is the full trajectory reward, $\hat{\pi}_A$ the estimated epoch propensity, and $\hat{Q}(a, s_0)$ the estimated trajectory-value regression. The estimator is $\sqrt{n}$-consistent when either $\hat{\pi}_A$ or $\hat{Q}$ is correctly specified (Robins 1986; Murphy 2003; Jiang & Li 2016).

**(d) Partial identification.** If (C6) fails, bounds over the adoption error model $\eta_D$:

$$
V_s^{\pi}(a) \in \bigl[\underline{V}_s^{\pi}(a; \eta_D),\; \overline{V}_s^{\pi}(a; \eta_D)\bigr],

$$

computed by extremal adoption misclassification. If (C7) fails, bounds over the co-memory composition distribution. If (C3) or (C8) fails, **no bound is authorized**: the output is `unresolved` (positivity/overlap failure) or `mismatch` (version/scope failure).

### 3.2 Proof sketch

**Step 1 — One-shot reduction.** Within the epoch, exactly one treatment $A_i^{\text{pers}}$ is assigned at $t_0$; candidate generation, exposure, adoption, and outcomes are consequences. Hence the estimand is a single-treatment, long-term-outcome expectation over the post-intervention process — no within-epoch treatment history to reweight.

**Step 2 — Post-intervention factorization.** Under (C2) (unconfounded epoch decision) and (C7) (no cross-memory interference conditional on state), the joint post-intervention distribution factorizes as $\prod_{\tau=1}^{t} P_\pi(\text{transition}_\tau \mid \bar{h}_{\tau-1}, a)$, a conditional Markov structure over the extended state (access status, budget, co-memory composition, adoption, version).

**Step 3 — Kernel identification.** Each transition kernel is identified from the logs: (C1) ties observed execution to the counterfactual mechanism; (C5) identifies the exposure/position/budget kernels; (C6) identifies the adoption kernel up to the error model; (C8) makes measurement stable within stratum. The g-formula integration over the post-intervention process then identifies $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ (standard g-computation; (C3) guarantees support).

**Step 4 — Estimators.** (a) Protocol route: the rollout estimator is the sample mean over randomized trajectories — unbiased by design, SE from trajectory variance. (b) Observational route: the DR functional is the influence-function-based estimator for the epoch-level treatment, consistent under (C3) and one correct nuisance model. (c) C6/C7 failure: extremal bounds; C3/C8 failure: `unresolved` / `mismatch`.

### 3.3 What this theorem claims

- Under (C1)–(C8), $V_s^{\pi}(a \mid S_{t_0})$ is **point-identified**, and the protocol-route rollout estimator is unbiased with honest CIs.
- The identification formula is the g-formula over the **post-intervention process** (candidate/exposure/adoption as consequence kernels), not over a within-epoch treatment sequence.
- The DR functional is the efficient estimator for the observational route; consistency holds when either nuisance is correct.
- When (C6)/(C7) fail, only **bounds**; when (C3)/(C8) fail, `unresolved`/`mismatch` — output space $\{\text{point}, \text{bound}, \text{unresolved}, \text{mismatch}\}$.

### 3.4 What this theorem does NOT claim

- It does **not** claim (C1)–(C8) hold in practice — they are the *definition* of when SQCAD is authorized to act; the Qualification gate checks them.
- It does **not** claim (C1)–(C8) are **minimal or individually necessary** — Lemma A–D (`12-必要性证明与识别路线分类学`) give worlds where each of C6/C7/C2–C3/C8 fails yet the contrast is identified via an alternative route (protocol-route independence, bundle retreat, IV, current-value identification). They are a *sufficient, operable, auditable* family.
- It does **not** claim the observational DR route works without the π/π_b encoding (target policy ≠ behavior policy must be explicit).
- It does **not** claim the protocol route is cheap or safe — randomized persistent-action changes are gated by safety eligibility and reversibility.
- It does **not** address scope transport (Gap 2) — that requires additional transport assumptions (see §4).
- What *is* necessary — refusal capability in the unidentified class — is Theorem 4 (`12` 文档 §3): every committing rule suffers worst-case error probability ≥ 1/2 and regret ≥ |τ1||τ2|/(|τ1|+|τ2|) on the Theorem 1 pair; only refusal or fresh interventional evidence beats the bound, and the refusal trigger must be auditable (Theorem 4(c′)).

## 4. Corollary: Scope Transport Limitation (Gap 2)

**Corollary 1 (Scope transport non-identifiability).**
Let $D_s = \tilde{O}_{1:H}^{(s)}$ be data from source scope $s$, and let $s^*$ be a target scope. Assume the mechanism linking treatment to outcome may differ across scopes: $P_{s}(Y \mid do(A), \cdot) \neq P_{s^*}(Y \mid do(A), \cdot)$ in general. Then:

$$
V_{s^*}^{\pi}(a \mid D_s) \neq V_s^{\pi}(a)

$$

in general, even when $V_s^{\pi}(a)$ is identified from $D_s$.

**Proof sketch.** Construct two worlds with identical source-scope data but different target-scope structural mechanisms (Proposition C in `gap_proof_experiments.py`). Source scope: $\tau(s_1) = 0.8$, $\tau(s_2) = -1.6$; source weighted average $\approx -0.28$. World 1 target: $\tau(s^*) = 2.0$; World 2 target: $\tau(s^*) = -2.0$. Source data cannot distinguish the two target worlds. $\square$

**Implication.** Scope transport requires additional assumptions: target support within source support, invariant causal mechanisms across scopes, or explicit transport weights. This is deferred to Gap 2.

## 5. From Theorems to Framework: The Derivation Map

The three theorems directly motivate SQCAD's Evidence–Qualification–Access architecture:

| Theorem | Identified deficiency | Framework requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Thm 1 | $(C, E, Y)$ logs insufficient | **Evidence layer** must additionally record exposure propensity, position, co-exposure, task context, adoption proxy |
| Thm 2 | Query-local $do$-effect insufficient | **Access layer** must define persistent treatment semantics (duration, rollback); **Evidence layer** must track policy feedback and next candidate stream |
| Thm 3 (C1) | Consistency required | **Access layer** records action type + effective window |
| Thm 3 (C2) | Exchangeability required | **Evidence layer** records full covariate history |
| Thm 3 (C3) | Positivity may fail observationally | **Qualification layer** gates randomized micro-intervention on safety-eligible candidates |
| Thm 3 (C4–C6) | Treatment/adoption/exposure observability | **Evidence layer** separates $E, D, A, Y$; **Qualification layer** checks measurement quality |
| Thm 3 (C7) | Interference | **Access layer** models workspace budget + co-memory composition |
| Thm 3 (C8) | Measurement stability | **Evidence layer** tags scope/version; **Qualification layer** runs stability gate |
| Cor 1 | Source ≠ target | **Qualification layer** audits target support before authorizing transport |
| Thm 4 | Committing rules fail on the unidentified class (error ≥ 1/2, regret ≥ \|τ1\|\|τ2\|/(\|τ1\|+\|τ2\|)) | **Qualification layer's refusal (`unresolved`) is necessary**, not conservative preference; the refusal trigger must be auditable (Thm 4(c′)) |

**The `unresolved` state** arises directly from Theorem 3's partial identification case: when any of (C3)–(C8) fail, the identification procedure cannot produce a point estimate or a decision-stable bound, and the system is **theoretically prohibited** from changing persistent access. Theorem 4 (`12` 文档 §3) upgrades this prohibition from design choice to necessity: on the unidentified class (Theorem 1's sign-flip pair), every committing rule has worst-case error probability ≥ 1/2 and expected regret ≥ |τ1||τ2|/(|τ1|+|τ2|) = 660 in the verified instance; refusal (or fresh interventional evidence) is the only way below the bound.

## 6. Verification: Two-Stage Identification Recovery Experiments

Theorem 3 makes testable predictions; experiments are staged so claims only climb after verification. **Status (2026-08-12): Stage 1 passes; Stage 2 verifies gate abstention under every violation.** Full report: `实验证据链/03-识别恢复实验报告-20260812.md`; code: `src/sqcad/identification_recovery_experiment.py`; results: `results/identification_recovery_stage_all.json`.

**Stage 1 — Ideal identification environment (validates Theorem 3(a)): PASSES.**
A controlled synthetic world with **known ground-truth** lifecycle values (oracle computed by the same mechanism, same horizon $H=150$, discount $\gamma=0.98$, and action window as the estimator; independent RNG streams). Micro-intervention randomizes $A_i^{\text{pers}}$ and rolls out under $\pi$ (C1–C8 satisfied by construction). Results (default config, seed=7; multi-seed 5 seeds):
- bias = **−0.47** (MC level; per-memory range −1.74…+2.16, no systematic direction — oracle's own MC error is ±1.6);
- 95% CI coverage = **12/12** (multi-seed 0.97±0.05 — honest SEs);
- confident decision error vs oracle = **0**; n_mismatch = **0**;
- `unresolved` = **exactly the 3 neutral memories** (|V| within CI reach);
- short-term memories (local effect +0.2, lifecycle ≈ −5.2) **archived correctly** — the Theorem 2 lesson holds inside Stage 1;
- comparators: association regret 91.3 (err 0.42), CMI/naive OPE regret 10.3 (err 0.17, failing exactly on short-term); SQCAD gated regret 2.6 (err 0.0) vs forced-by-sign 3.0 (err 0.25).

**Stage 2 — Progressive condition violation (validates Theorem 3(d) + the gate): PASSES.**
Violate one condition at a time; the gate must abstain exactly when a condition fails, and must NOT abstain in Stage 1:

| Violation | Condition | Gate output | Forced variant (blind to gate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| adoption error | C6 ($D\neq E$, misattribution 0.3) | 12 `unresolved`, err 0.0 | regret 0.4, err 0.17 (protocol RCT does not use $D$; deployment cannot verify it landed) |
| co-exposure | C7 (bundle $E[h]=E[u]$, corr 1.00) | pair `unresolved`, err 0.0 | regret 41.0, err 0.08 (bundle value +9.0 hides the harmful member; individual attribution keeps it) |

**Gate ablation.** The forced variant makes confident decisions under every violation; the gated variant always abstains (unresolved/mismatch) with **zero confident errors**. Abstention is not free — it defers the value of memories whose evidence is invalidated (e.g. C7 defers the useful member worth 48); the framework's commitment is "never a confident wrong decision", not "abstention costs nothing".
